# AU Corporate Positioning: Project Office in India

## Relevant Service Lines for This Page

- **FEMA & RBI Compliance** — this is the page's core subject, not an add-on. Form FNC filing through an AD Category-I Bank, the general-permission (Regulation 5) vs specific-approval (RBI Central Office) routing decision, sectoral/geographic prior-approval triggers, and the closure sequence are all FEMA/RBI mechanics AU Corporate actually executes for clients (the same team that runs this on the live Branch Office and Liaison Office pages). This should read as the page's spine, not a bolted-on service mention.
- **Company Incorporation** (narrowly, for the ROC leg only) — Form FC-1 registration with the Registrar of Companies under Section 380, and PAN/TAN allotment. Frame this as "the second, separate registration a Project Office needs" (mirroring how both sibling pages draw the RBI-approval-vs-ROC-registration distinction explicitly), not as a push toward full company incorporation — a Project Office is never incorporated, and the writer should not blur that line the way the sibling pages take care not to.
- **International Tax / Corporate Tax (PE angle)** — the single clearest differentiation lever per Stage 4. A Project Office is, by definition, executing a specific contract in India and is therefore the entity type in this three-page set most likely to create a Permanent Establishment. Construction/Installation PE thresholds (typically shorter than the general fixed-place-of-business or service-PE tests under most of India's DTAAs) and profit attribution to that PE are genuine, non-generic tax content this page needs and that AU Corporate's tax practice can speak to credibly.
- **GST Advisory & Compliance** — relevant here in a way it is not on the Liaison Office page. A Project Office is revenue/contract-linked (unlike a Liaison Office, which the live LO page confirms has no taxable outward supply by default), so GST registration is a live, practical question tied to the specific contract being executed, not a hypothetical. Reverse-charge treatment on payments back to the parent (already described on `/services/taxation-regulatory`) is also directly relevant if the PO receives funding or services from the parent.
- **Compliance & Regulatory Services / Audit & Assurance** (for the AAC) — the Annual Activity Certificate is CA-certified and routed to both the AD bank and the Directorate General of Income Tax, exactly as described on the Branch Office and Liaison Office pages. Naming AAC, FLA return, and ITR filing with real specificity (per Stage 4's Differentiation List item 3) is squarely inside what AU Corporate's compliance and audit practice does daily — this is where "named, dated compliance items instead of generic language" becomes credible rather than a keyword-stuffing exercise.
- **Accounting & Outsourcing** — only as a light, natural mention (books feeding the AAC and eventual closure certificate), matching how the sibling pages treat it — not a standalone pitch.

## Service Lines Deliberately Excluded

- **Virtual CFO** — a Project Office is inherently short-lived and single-contract-scoped; positioning an ongoing CFO-as-a-service offering against a structure explicitly built to wind down at project completion would read as mismatched to the reader's actual need. Neither sibling page mentions Virtual CFO, and this page shouldn't be the one to introduce it.
- **Payroll** — the page should note, briefly and factually (as both siblings do), that a Project Office can hire local staff subject to standard labour-law/payroll obligations. It should not expand into a Payroll service pitch; that's a generic fact about any employer in India, not something distinctive to Project Offices.
- **Business Valuation, Transaction Advisory** — no plausible connection to a foreign company standing up a time-bound project presence. Forcing either in would be a non-sequitur.
- **Risk Advisory, Internal Controls** — too generic/unconnected to this specific entity type; the page already has a genuine, specific risk topic (PE exposure) and should go deep on that rather than gesture at a broader "risk advisory" service that isn't actually about Project Offices.
- **FDI Advisory** — a Project Office is an establishment under FEMA 22(R), not an equity/FDI-route question under FEMA 20(R). The Liaison Office page makes this distinction explicitly ("A Liaison Office isn't an 'FDI route' question at all"); the Project Office page should carry the same discipline rather than conflating the two frameworks the way generic competitor content sometimes does.
- **India Entry Strategy / Virtual CFO as a broad "we do everything" close** — the AU Corporate support section should stay specific to the four-authority coordination problem (AD bank, ROC, tax authority, and — uniquely for this entity type — the PE/tax-exposure assessment), not widen into a generic "we support your India entry" pitch that could apply to any page on the site.

## International-Investor Considerations Relevant Here

Only the items this specific topic actually needs (not a full checklist):

- **Indian entity structures** — yes, via the existing four-way comparison card pattern (Liaison / Branch / Project / Wholly Owned Subsidiary) already published on both sibling pages. Reuse the same framing device for consistency across the matched set; the Project Office card text already exists verbatim on both live pages and should be treated as the anchor description to build the full page around, not contradicted.
- **FDA/FDI route** — explicitly *not* relevant in the equity sense. State plainly (as the LO page does) that a Project Office sits under FEMA 22(R) establishment law, not FEMA 20(R) equity investment, so readers evaluating this alongside an FDI equity question know they're in the wrong framework.
- **FEMA/RBI angle** — central and non-negotiable: Regulation 5's four general-permission conditions, Form FNC, AD Category-I Bank routing, general-permission vs specific-approval decision framework, sectoral/geographic triggers, the six-month opening window (+ discretionary extension).
- **Tax treatment** — central and the page's real differentiation opportunity: PE exposure via construction/installation PE, why PO carries materially higher PE risk than Liaison Office (and somewhat higher, more contract-specific risk than a Branch Office's broader activity-based PE exposure), profit attribution to the project, and the corporate tax rate consequence of being treated as a foreign company with an Indian PE (consistent with the 35% base rate figure already stated on the Branch Office page — do not restate a different number).
- **GST** — relevant and should be addressed, unlike on the Liaison Office page where it's addressed only to explain why it typically doesn't apply. Here the natural framing is closer to the Branch Office treatment: address it as a live compliance question tied to the specific contract.
- **Accounting/compliance obligations** — AAC, FLA return, ITR filing, named with real specificity per the content gap's differentiation opportunity.
- **Post-incorporation requirements** — not "incorporation" (a PO is registered, not incorporated — reuse the LO page's precise language on this point), but the FC-1 ROC registration and PAN/TAN sequence.
- **Practical India-entry considerations** — the six-month opening window, AD-bank due-diligence criteria (promoter antecedents, source of funds, KYC), and the closure sequence at vjmglobal's level of step-by-step specificity (CA certificate → ROC deregistration → AD-bank submission → AD-bank compliance verification → AD-bank reporting to RBI), matched or exceeded and paired with primary-source citation neither vjmglobal nor any analyzed competitor currently provides.

## AU Corporate's Right-to-Win Angle

Two things make this page more useful than the competitive set, and both are grounded in what AU Corporate's practice actually does, not branding:

1. **The registration-mechanics team and the tax team are the same firm, so this page can honestly connect them on one page.** Stage 3/4 confirm zero analyzed competitors integrate PE/tax-risk content into the core registration page — it lives in a disconnected tax-journal tier instead. AU Corporate's taxation & regulatory practice (`/services/taxation-regulatory`) already states, on a live page, that a foreign company operating through a branch or project office is "taxed differently, and generally less favourably" than an incorporated subsidiary — this page's job is to make that one clause fully concrete for Project Offices specifically (construction/installation PE, shorter treaty thresholds, profit attribution), which is a natural extension of an existing claim, not a new one invented for SEO purposes.
2. **Named compliance mechanics instead of generic "ensure compliance" language.** AAC routing to two authorities in parallel, FLA return, ITR filing, and the CA-certification requirement at closure are all things AU Corporate's compliance and audit practice files routinely — this page can state them with the same operational precision the Branch Office and Liaison Office pages already demonstrate (e.g., "routed to two authorities in parallel: the AD Category-I bank... and the Directorate General of Income Tax"), which is a genuine practitioner-level detail most competitor pages skip.

A secondary, lower-key right-to-win: **disciplined hedging on genuinely unresolved points** (the UIN question, Regulation 5 paragraph numbering, FNC vs FNC-1 naming) rather than asserting a confident answer no source actually supports. This isn't a marketing angle — it's the one credibility move Stage 2/4 explicitly call out as differentiating, and it mirrors the Branch Office page's own existing "we could not confirm X against a primary source" convention, so it will read as consistent with the site's established voice rather than as a new gimmick.

## Explicit Guidance for the Writer

- **Match the Branch Office/Liaison Office register exactly**: precise, slightly technical, practitioner-toned prose; specific numbers and named authorities rather than vague "ensure compliance" phrasing; willingness to say "we could not confirm X against a primary source" rather than guessing. Avoid any tonal drift toward more promotional language just because this is a newer, less-established page.
- **Do not invent numeric specifics that were not confirmed in Stage 1-4** — the six-month opening window and its discretionary extension were found on only one competitor source; state it but do not embellish with a false sense of universal confirmation. Same discipline applies to Regulation 5's exact paragraph numbering and Form FNC vs FNC-1 naming — hedge precisely where Stage 2 flagged genuine ambiguity, without over-hedging points that are actually well established (e.g., AD Category-I bank as routing authority, Form FC-1/30-day ROC filing).
- **The "How AU Corporate Supports..." section should describe a real coordination problem, not a features list.** Follow the sibling pages' pattern exactly: name the specific authorities involved (AD bank, ROC/MCA, Directorate General of Income Tax) and state what AU Corporate actually coordinates between them, ending in a plain, low-pressure CTA — not a bulleted service menu and not superlative language ("best-in-class," "trusted partner," "industry-leading"). Neither sibling page uses that kind of language anywhere; this page must not either.
- **The PE/tax-risk section is the page's genuine point of difference — give it real depth, but keep it correct and appropriately caveated.** Name construction/installation PE and profit attribution as concepts; do not assert a specific universal treaty-article number or day-threshold as if it applies identically across all of India's DTAAs, since that varies by the parent's home jurisdiction (this is the same discipline the Branch Office page already applies when it declines to give a fixed validity period it can't confirm). Link out to `/services/taxation-regulatory` for the fuller international-tax/DTAA picture rather than trying to fully resolve treaty-specific mechanics on this page.
- **Avoid at all costs**: bullet lists that exist only to name-check AU Corporate's full service catalogue; superlative claims about AU Corporate's positioning or size; any framing that positions Project Office registration as an upsell into unrelated services (Virtual CFO, Payroll, Valuation) that don't genuinely serve this reader; and a UIN-question FAQ or H2 phrased as a confidently-answered fact, per Stage 2's explicit instruction.
- **Reader in mind**: a CFO, in-house counsel, or founder at a foreign company that has already won a specific Indian contract and now needs to know how to stand up a compliant, time-bound India presence to execute it, what it costs them in tax exposure, and how to close it out cleanly when the project ends. Write for that person's actual decision sequence (eligibility → route → application → activity boundary → tax exposure → ongoing compliance → closure), not for a generic "everything about Project Offices" reference dump.
