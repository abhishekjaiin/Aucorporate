## Per-Page Analysis

### beaconfiling.com — "China Plus One PLI Scheme India 2026" + USA cluster
**Does well:** Leads with hard, dated numbers rather than generic scheme description — 836 approved PLI applications, ₹2.16 lakh crore committed investment, ₹28,748 crore disbursed, all "as of December 2025," plus the Apple/iPhone-manufacturing-share example (India's global iPhone assembly share rising from under 1% in 2017 toward a 2026 projection, overtaking China as top smartphone exporter to the US) as concrete proof China+1 is happening, not theoretical. Maintains a companion "PLI Scheme Results Tracker 2026" post that appears to function as a living, updated data page rather than a one-time write. The separate USA-audience cluster is real and substantive on its own pages: the FEMA-compliance/USA page names the specific apostille requirement and document list (Certificate of Incorporation, Board Resolution, MOA/AOA, FIRC, SEBI-registered valuation certificate); the register-company-in-india-from-usa page states the 30-day FC-GPR filing deadline and explicitly frames missed deadlines as a FEMA violation; the payroll/USA page connects payroll recharges to India-US DTAA withholding (15% on Fees for Included Services) — a specific, correct-sounding cross-border tax mechanic not seen elsewhere in this research.
**Does poorly:** Based on available evidence, the China+1/PLI post itself stays largely at the "why manufacture in India now" and scheme-results level — it does not appear to pull the FEMA/FC-GPR/incorporation mechanics from the sibling USA-cluster pages into itself. The manufacturing-entity-structuring depth exists on Beacon Filing's site, but split across at least four separate URLs (the PLI/China+1 post, the FEMA-compliance/USA page, the payroll/USA page, the register-company page) rather than unified into one page that walks a US manufacturer from "why India" through "which entity" through "PLI eligibility" through "ongoing FEMA/payroll compliance." No evidence surfaced of Section 482/transfer-pricing/Form 5471 content connected to manufacturing anywhere on the domain.
**Why it likely ranks:** Freshness and specificity (2026-dated figures, a maintained tracker post) combined with a genuinely US-audience-mirrored site architecture that signals topical focus to search engines across many related queries at once — not one standout page but a coherent cluster reinforcing itself. Cannot verify domain-authority/backlink contribution (Semrush blocked); the content specificity here looks like it's doing real work, not just riding domain strength.
**What would beat it:** A single page that does what Beacon Filing splits across four — connect the China+1/PLI strategic hook directly to entity choice, FC-GPR/FEMA filing obligations, and PLI eligibility mechanics in one reading path for a US manufacturer, plus add the ECMS/successor-scheme transition context (see Cross-Page Patterns) and the US-side Section 482 angle Beacon Filing never touches.

### india-briefing.com (Dezan Shira & Associates) — "India Market Entry Strategy for US Investors"
**Does well:** Frames entry as a structured, multi-dimensional decision (entity structure, FDI rules, tax, GST, transfer pricing, state selection, incentives, compliance) rather than a checklist, and explicitly argues state selection is one of the most consequential decisions because labor availability, land access, SEZ status, industrial policy, incentives, logistics, and supplier clusters vary meaningfully by state — a genuinely more sophisticated framing than most competitors' generic "register a company" sequencing. Names PLI as rewarding "US electronics, semiconductor, and industrial manufacturers relocating capacity to India" specifically, and frames China+1 with real nuance (India as a strong destination for some functions, explicitly not a wholesale China replacement) rather than boosterism. Evidence (via a related india-briefing PLI overview piece surfaced in search) shows the publisher also segments US entrants into distinct personas — SaaS/GCC builders, listed enterprise software companies opening go-to-market offices, PE/growth funds, and industrial manufacturers establishing a second production base — which is a useful buyer-segmentation idea, though manufacturing is only one of four personas on this page, not its focus.
**Does poorly:** No evidence found of Factories Act, environmental clearance, land acquisition, or other physical-plant-setup mechanics in this page — that operational layer is absent here (it belongs to Ahlawat/manufacturingplantindia-style content instead). No evidence this specific page discusses PLI's 2026 transition or ECMS/successor schemes — Dezan Shira covers ECMS as its own separate news article elsewhere on the domain, not integrated into this US-investor entry guide, so a reader of this page alone would not learn PLI is mid-transition. No evidence of US-side tax content (Section 482, Form 5471, Form 5472) — the tax coverage found is India-side (transfer pricing in India, GST), not the US parent's own reporting obligations for a manufacturing subsidiary.
**Why it likely ranks:** Almost certainly publisher scale and domain authority more than unique manufacturing depth — Dezan Shira runs a large, cross-linked "Doing Business in India" guide ecosystem plus parallel China/ASEAN briefing sites, which is a structural trust and internal-linking advantage no single-country advisory firm in this SERP can match. The manufacturing-specific content in this particular page is real but thin relative to its authority; this looks like a page that would rank even if its manufacturing section were removed entirely.
**What would beat it:** Not "be more authoritative" (a losing fight against a multi-decade pan-Asia publisher) but be more specifically useful to the one persona india-briefing treats as 1-of-4: fold in the physical-plant and PLI-eligibility mechanics india-briefing doesn't cover, add the ECMS transition context this page skips, and add the US-side tax angle it never addresses.

### knmindia.com — "PLI 2.0 India: Structure Your Entity for Maximum Incentives"
**Does well:** The strongest entity-structuring angle found in this research — explicitly connects the JV-vs-wholly-owned-subsidiary choice to PLI eligibility and operational control, recommends a Private Limited Company for R&D-heavy operations with a stated rationale (capital-raising flexibility, limited liability, incentive eligibility), and gives concrete figures: ₹50–100 crore sector-dependent minimum investment thresholds, local value-addition targets scaling from 40–60% in Year 1 to 60–80% by Year 3, and incentive rates of 18–25% on incremental sales. Also frames PLI 2.0 itself as a shift from volume/assembly-linked payouts toward domestic-value-addition-linked payouts — this is a form of transition-awareness, though framed as scheme evolution rather than explicitly naming ECMS or a 2026 sector-window closure. The publisher's pattern of spinning out narrow persona-specific variants (a Japanese-manufacturer version, a Japanese AI/tech-firm version) shows a content strategy built around buyer-specific angles, though no US-manufacturer-specific version was found.
**Does poorly:** No evidence of China+1 framing anywhere in this post — it treats PLI as an incentive-capture exercise, not as part of a supply-chain-diversification narrative, missing the "why now" urgency that Beacon Filing and india-briefing both use. No FEMA/FC-GPR filing detail found. No Section 482/US-tax content found. No explicit ECMS naming or 2026-sector-window-closing language found (contrast with Beacon Filing, where that specific framing did surface).
**Why it likely ranks:** Entity-structuring specificity for a genuinely underserved query angle ("how does my entity choice affect PLI capture") — this looks like a case of a narrow, well-targeted post winning on relevance/specificity rather than domain authority, consistent with the general pattern noted in guardrails (short and specific can beat long and generic).
**What would beat it:** Add the China+1 strategic framing this post lacks entirely, name the PLI transition/ECMS explicitly rather than only implying evolution, and extend "entity choice affects incentive capture" to also cover the US parent's own FEMA and Section 482 obligations once that entity is chosen and operating.

### ahlawatassociates.com — "Setting up a New Manufacturing Plant in India: Complete Guide"
**Does well:** By far the most operationally concrete physical-plant content found across all pages examined — specific Factories Act, 1948 registration thresholds (10+ workers with power, 20+ without), the actual pre-registration sequence (building-plan approval, NOCs from Fire/Water/Pollution Control departments, a 15-day advance notice to the chief inspector before commencing manufacturing), and differentiated, realistic timelines (GST/IEC in 7–15 working days vs. factory/environmental clearances taking 30 days to 6 months depending on the plant's pollution category). The firm also runs an adjacent, separately-titled post specifically on manufacturing setup "in the wake of US-China trade tensions" — the closest thing found on this domain to a China+1-style hook, but kept as a distinct piece rather than merged into the main guide, and a separate dedicated US-companies market-entry page also exists apart from both.
**Does poorly:** No PLI content evidenced in the Complete Guide post itself — the incentive-scheme angle is entirely absent from this specific piece, meaning a reader gets factory-registration mechanics but no incentive-eligibility guidance in the same place. No FEMA/entity-incorporation-for-foreigners depth found in this post (that appears to live on Ahlawat's separate US-market-entry page instead). No Section 482/US-tax content found anywhere.
**Why it likely ranks:** Genuine procedural specificity that satisfies a real sub-intent (the "then what do I actually have to file to open the factory" question) that PLI/incentive-focused competitors skip entirely — this looks like content-depth-driven ranking on a narrow, well-served sub-topic, not authority-driven.
**What would beat it:** Combine this level of physical-plant procedural specificity with the PLI-eligibility and China+1 framing Ahlawat splits into other posts, in one page — Ahlawat's own site is a clear illustration of the fragmentation problem: nobody, including Ahlawat itself, unifies "why now (China+1)" + "which entity + FEMA" + "PLI eligibility" + "then what do you actually file to open the plant" in one place.

## Cross-Page Patterns

**What most strong pages share:** Every page that reads as genuinely useful wins on *specificity in one narrow lane*, not breadth — Beacon Filing wins on dated PLI results-tracker numbers and USA-cluster filing specifics, KNM India wins on entity-choice-to-incentive mechanics, Ahlawat wins on physical-plant registration procedure, ASC Group wins on regulatory-currency (2026 budget tariff changes), india-briefing wins on structured, multi-dimensional entry-decision framing plus sheer publisher scale. None of them wins by being long or generic — the guardrail against using length as a quality proxy holds up against this evidence: Tecnova's page is thin *and* unspecific, and that combination, not its length, is why it reads weak.

**What's uniformly weak across all of them:** (1) Fragmentation — every direct competitor that has the pieces (Beacon Filing, Ahlawat) splits China+1 strategic framing, PLI eligibility mechanics, entity/FEMA incorporation steps, and physical-plant procedure across separate posts/pages rather than unifying them for one reader journey; no page was found that does all four in one place. (2) PLI-transition risk: only Beacon Filing's content ecosystem (via its results-tracker post, not confirmed to be the flagship China+1 post itself) was found naming ECMS and the ₹22,919 crore successor-scheme outlay explicitly; KNM India shows implicit transition-awareness (PLI 2.0 shifting to value-addition-linked incentives) without naming ECMS; every other page examined (india-briefing's US-investor guide, ASC Group, Tecnova, Ahlawat's manufacturing guide) presents PLI as static/current with no visible acknowledgment that original sector windows are closing in 2026 — this is a real, mostly-open gap, not a fully-closed one. (3) US-side tax mechanics: no competitor examined connects India manufacturing entry to the US parent's own Section 482/transfer-pricing exposure, Form 5471/5472 filing obligations, or how a manufacturing (tangible-goods, contract-manufacturing-style) transfer-pricing setup differs from a services/SaaS subsidiary's arrangement — this gap is completely open across every page assessed. (4) No competitor page found packages Factories Act/environmental-clearance procedure, PLI eligibility, China+1 strategic framing, FEMA/entity mechanics, AND the PLI-transition/ECMS context together — the closest any single domain gets is Beacon Filing's cluster, and even that requires reading four separate URLs.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely better AU Corporate page does not need to be longer than any of these — it needs to close four specific gaps none of them close together: (1) explicitly date and frame PLI as a scheme in transition (name ECMS and the 2026 sector-window closures the way Beacon Filing's tracker post does, not present PLI as static the way india-briefing, ASC Group, Tecnova, and Ahlawat's manufacturing guide all currently do); (2) connect China+1's strategic "why now" directly to concrete entity-choice and PLI-eligibility mechanics in the same reading path, rather than splitting strategy from mechanics across separate pages the way every competitor examined does; (3) address the US parent's own compliance exposure for a manufacturing (not services) subsidiary specifically — Section 482/transfer pricing for tangible-goods intercompany transactions, Form 5471/5472 — a gap that is completely open across all eight pages assessed; and (4) where physical-plant mechanics are relevant, match Ahlawat's level of procedural specificity (actual registration thresholds, NOC sequence, realistic clearance timelines) rather than staying at the incentive/strategy level the way most PLI-focused competitors do. None of this requires outlength or outrank-by-volume tactics — it requires unifying pieces that currently exist only in fragments across seven different domains.
