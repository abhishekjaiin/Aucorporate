## Foreign/International Framing Check

**The rule:** foreign investor is the audience; it does not automatically become the keyword. Checked explicitly below.

**Evidence found for international/foreign-investor phrasing (real, but a distinct cluster, not this page's target):**
An independent WebSearch run for this stage (separate from Stage 1's queries) surfaced a genuinely distinct set of ranking pages under "foreign"/"foreigner"/"foreign investor" phrasing, confirming this is real searched language somewhere in the market:
- Commenda: "Register a company in India for foreigners"
- Global Law Experts: "How To Register A Foreign Company In India 2026"
- Companiesnext: "Company Registration in India by Foreigners"
- Ebizfiling: "Company Registration in India by Foreigners Online"
- Saudi Helpline Group: "How to Register a Company in India as a Foreign Investor: The 2025 Guide"

**Why this does not change the primary/secondary keyword targeting for this page:** every one of these ranking pages targets a *nationality-agnostic* "foreigner/foreign investor" searcher (the copy has to work for a Saudi, Chinese, or European buyer as much as a US one), which is a different, broader query cluster than "…from USA" / "…from the USA." A US founder or CFO self-identifies by country ("from USA") in the same way the competitor titles matched in the Primary/Secondary sections above consistently do — not by the abstract category "foreign investor." Inserting "foreign" or "international" into this page's H1, title tag, or primary/secondary keyword targets would blend two distinct searcher phrasings without evidence that the USA-specific searcher uses the broader term.

**Where this evidence should still be used:** the "foreign national," "foreigner," and "for foreign investors" phrasing is legitimate for (a) FAQ coverage of edge-case qualifying questions (e.g., "Does this process differ for a US citizen vs. other foreign nationals?"), (b) schema/alt-text mentions so the page can also catch adjacent nationality-agnostic long-tail traffic without being optimized for it, and (c) a possible *future, separate* page in AU's cluster targeting the broader "foreign company registration in India" query if that's ever prioritized — that is a Stage 5 strategy decision, not something to fold into this page's keyword targets.

One additional internal caveat: the phrase "for foreign investors" appears in this document only inside Stage 1's own PAA-proxy question ("How long does it take to register a private limited company in India for foreign investors?") — that was Stage 1's paraphrase choice, not an observed query string, and is flagged again here so it isn't mistaken for verified evidence.

**Conclusion:** No search-demand evidence justifies inserting "foreign," "international," "overseas," or "cross-border" into this page's primary or secondary keyword targets. Target the broad-market, country-specific queries ("register company in India from USA" and its direct variants); address the foreign-investor/foreign-national angle through FAQ content and audience framing in the body copy, not through the targeted keyword set.
