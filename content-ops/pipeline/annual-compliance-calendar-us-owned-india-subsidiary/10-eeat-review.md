## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The DIN-holding, non-resident-director section is the strongest part of the page.** It distinguishes DIR-3 KYC's personal-vs-company filing responsibility, walks through the specific failure mode of a US director assuming the company secretary "just handles it," ties DSC renewal into the same tracking system as KYC (a real, specific practitioner observation about a common compliance-gap cause), and flags V3-portal lead time for foreign filers as a planning cushion rather than a stated SLA. This reads like something a firm that has actually filed these for non-resident directors would write, not generic AI output with India compliance facts pasted in.
- **The CFO-sequencing overlay ("How the Compliance Year Actually Unfolds")** answers a real, sophisticated question ("in what order do these obligations run relative to each other") that a flat form list doesn't, and explicitly separates FLA and DIR-3 KYC as running on independent clocks — a nuance most competitor pages in Stage 3's teardown reportedly miss.
- **The CCFS 2026 section's tone is exactly right for a closed relief window**: dated, no urgency language, explicit "check MCA's current notifications directly rather than assuming a similar window is open now" — this is the kind of freshness discipline Stage 3 flagged competitors for failing at, and the page succeeds at it.
- **The penalties section and "What Happens If a Deadline Is Missed"** connect a missed filing to a real downstream business consequence (a later fundraise, share transfer, or due-diligence process catching the gap) rather than stopping at the fee figure — that's the difference between informing and actually helping someone weigh the risk.
- **The virtual-AGM confirmation** directly answers the single most common logistical worry this audience raises ("do we need to fly someone to India"), stated with appropriate confidence because it's backed by a specific, Stage-9-verified MCA circular (03/2025).

## Where It Falls Short

1. **"Based on this pipeline's research" appears twice in client-facing body copy** — in the DIN-holder section ("### The cadence, stated precisely and hedged": *"Based on this pipeline's research, the filing has moved from an annual to a triennial cadence..."*) and in FAQ #2 (*"Based on this pipeline's research, the filing has moved to a triennial cadence..."*). "This pipeline" is internal production terminology referring to this content-ops workflow, not a phrase any real advisory firm would use in client-facing copy. As literally written, this is a visible tell that the page was produced by an automated content pipeline rather than a firm's own review process — which is close to the exact failure mode this review exists to catch ("generic AI-generated content with a logo on it"). This needs rewording before publish to something like "Based on our review of the relevant MCA notifications" or simply presenting the hedge without naming its source-process at all.
2. **Three instances of internal pipeline artifacts referenced as if they were live-page content**: the ADT-1 calendar-table citation ("...the underlying MCA notification number is still being verified; **see Writer's Notes**"), the DIN cadence section ("...Confirm the current cadence against MCA's own notifications before relying on it for a specific director's filing year; **see Writer's Notes below**"), and the first-AGM parenthetical ("...before relying on it for a specific filing date — **flagged in Writer's Notes**"). The "Writer's Notes — Needs Verification" section is explicitly pipeline documentation addressed to "Agent 9 (Fact Checker)" and sits outside the "## Page Content" boundary — it is not intended to ship. A reader (or, worse, an implementation stage that transcribes the draft mechanically) following any of these three references on the live page would find nothing. These need to be rewritten as self-contained hedges (the underlying hedge in each case is fine and should stay — e.g., "the underlying MCA notification number has not been independently confirmed against the primary gazette text" — just without the internal cross-reference).
3. **FAQ #5's "hasn't been independently sourced in this research"** is a softer version of the same pattern — less damaging than "this pipeline's research" but still slightly meta/process-facing rather than reading as a firm's own voice. Minor tidy recommended alongside the fixes above.
4. **Board-meeting-cadence citation is less authoritative than it could now be.** Stage 9 confirmed Section 173(1) as the correct, citable provision (resolving Writer's Note #6), but neither the calendar table nor the "Board meeting cadence" section incorporates it — both still read "Companies Act, 2013 board-meeting provisions" with no section number, an available and low-risk authority upgrade that the 2026-09-30 correction pass didn't pick up (it only addressed Notes 1, 5, and 12).
5. **No named individual attribution**, despite the page being exactly the content type (a dated regulatory compliance calendar) where a named CA/CS credit would do the most trust work — see recommendation below.

None of the above are hedging-into-meaninglessness problems — the substantive hedges throughout the page are specific, honest, and well-calibrated (this is not a page that hedges every claim reflexively; unhedged claims like the virtual-AGM confirmation and the Section 96/137/92 citations are stated with earned confidence). The issues above are narrower and more mechanical: internal process language that leaked into what should be finished, external-facing copy.
