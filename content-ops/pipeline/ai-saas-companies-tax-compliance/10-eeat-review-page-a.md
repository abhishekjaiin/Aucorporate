## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- The REG-10 vs. REG-09 distinction is explained with real command of *why* the two forms differ (zero-presence digital supplier vs. occasional-physical-presence non-resident taxable person, e.g. a trade-fair exhibitor) rather than just naming the correct form number. This is exactly the kind of precise, functional use of a citation that separates genuine expertise from name-dropping a section number.
- The NTOR vs. reverse-charge explanation correctly identifies the *structural reason* the obligation falls on the supplier (the recipient can't self-assess), not just the rule outcome — that's the kind of explanation that comes from actually having walked a client through it, not from paraphrasing a summary article.
- The OIDAR/SEP disambiguation (indirect tax vs. direct tax, GST vs. income tax, two separate statutory bases) is handled with real precision and matches exactly what Stage 5's positioning brief flagged as a genuine accuracy risk if blurred. The page gets this right on both the first mention and in the FAQ.
- The entity-cutover mechanics ("two separate registrations under two separate legal persons... the old one needs to be formally wound down") is a specific, non-obvious, practically useful detail that a generic competitor summary wouldn't think to include — this is genuine experience-shaped content, not textbook regurgitation.
- Calling out that "some public guidance... runs the domestic threshold and the OIDAR rule together as if they're the same test" mirrors Stage 3's actual competitor-research finding (the Kanakkupillai conflation) without naming a competitor or inventing a claim — appropriately generic and defensible.

## Where It Falls Short

1. **SAC 9983 stated as unhedged fact on a customer-facing invoicing point, despite unresolved sourcing.** This is the one place on the page where the tone is more confident than the verification status warrants — precisely the failure mode this review is meant to catch, and precisely the same category of error (a specific code/number stated as settled fact) as the REG-09 error Stage 9 already caught once on this page. Given the site's own precedent of a fabricated citation being caught only at fact-check, and this page's own confirmed form-number error, this is not a place to leave stated plainly. **This needs an orchestrator/content decision, not a mechanical edit**: either (a) caveat it generally ("OIDAR supplies are generally classified under the SAC 9983 heading — confirm the precise code with your GST advisor before finalizing invoice templates"), or (b) drop the specific code number and keep only the 18% rate (which Stage 9 confirmed cleanly), or (c) hold publication of this sentence pending the professional sign-off Stage 9 already recommended. I have not made this edit myself since it's a content/scope decision, not a small tone fix.
2. **SEP citation (Income-tax Act 2025 §9(9)) has no hedge despite Stage 9 flagging elevated currency risk.** Minor relative to #1 since the page correctly scopes SEP depth elsewhere, but stating a section number from a very recently commenced Act with no qualifier is a small instance of the same pattern. A light touch ("currently codified at Section 9(9)...") would fix this without adding defensive hedging elsewhere on the page.
3. **Internal production artifacts must not ship.** The bracketed component notes, the full "Writer's Notes — Needs Verification" section (lines 204–222 of the draft), and the writer's-note callouts embedded mid-copy are clearly meant for internal pipeline use, but if any of this shipped to the live page it would be a severe, self-inflicted trust failure — a published page literally telling the reader "writer's note: exact CBIC notification number... to be confirmed" would read as unfinished or, worse, as evidence the firm published without checking its own citations. This is a publish-gate QA item rather than an EEAT content issue per se, but it belongs in this review because the downside (an admission of unverified sourcing going live) is exactly the kind of thing that destroys trust instantly. Flagging explicitly for whoever owns final CMS assembly.
4. **Confidence calibration is otherwise well-judged, which makes #1 stand out more, not less.** The DGGI enforcement paragraph is a good model for how to hedge appropriately (real trend, sourced generally, phrased as "reportedly" rather than asserted as a specific confirmed mechanism) — which is exactly the treatment the SAC code claim should have gotten and didn't. The page clearly knows how to hedge correctly elsewhere; the SAC line reads as an oversight rather than a considered choice.

## Author/Expert Attribution Recommendation

This page should carry a named reviewer credit, and currently doesn't — it only has "Last updated 2026 · Compliance guide for foreign digital-services providers" with no named individual or credential attached. Three reasons this page specifically warrants it, more than a typical page would:

- It makes specific statutory claims with real penalty/interest exposure attached (CGST §§122, 50) and gives customer-facing invoicing guidance (SAC code, GST rate) — this is functionally YMYL-adjacent content (a wrong answer has real financial/compliance consequences for a reader who acts on it), which is exactly the category Google's own guidance treats as benefiting most from visible expert review.
- Stage 9's fact-check explicitly requires "a qualified AU Corporate tax/GST professional" to sign off on several remaining items before publish. That sign-off is currently invisible to the reader. Surfacing it as a named byline ("Reviewed by [Name], [Title], AU Corporate") turns a purely internal QA step into a visible trust signal, and closes the loop between "we did this verification" and "the reader can see we did."
- The page's own voice already claims first-person practitioner experience ("we've been seeing more of this," "one of the more common misconceptions we run into") — a named reviewer attribution would substantiate that voice rather than leave it as an anonymous "we."

Recommend adding a byline near the top (next to "Last updated 2026") along the lines of: *"Reviewed by [Name], [GST/Indirect Tax practice title], AU Corporate"* — tied to whichever professional actually performs the sign-off on the open items in Stage 9's list. This should happen as part of, not separately from, resolving the outstanding sign-off items — the byline and the actual review should be the same event.
