## Current Page vs. Current SERP
Ran a fresh SERP scan for "India business setup guide for foreign companies" and adjacent phrasings. Two findings:

1. **The literal head term "India business setup" / "business setup in India" is dominated by broad, domestic-founder-oriented content** (ClearTax-style, RegisterKaro, IncorpX, wisemonk, generic "2026 guide" content-marketing pages), most of which cover the *full* Indian entity taxonomy — Sole Proprietorship, Partnership Firm, OPC, Section 8 Company, Private Limited — with much lower cost figures (₹5,000–₹30,000, 5–15 working days) than AU Corporate's foreign-investor figures (₹55,000–₹1.15L+, 4–12 weeks). AU Corporate's page, and its whole `/india-business-setup/*` cluster (confirmed via each sub-page's own metadata — all five explicitly say "for Foreign Companies" / "foreign-owned businesses"), is deliberately scoped to foreign companies only. This is a **genuine, structural intent mismatch between the literal query and AU Corporate's actual buyer**, not a content-quality gap — and not one this page should chase by broadening into domestic-founder territory, since that would misalign with the firm's actual service (foreign-company India entry advisory) and with every sibling page's positioning.
2. **Competitor pages specifically targeting the foreign-company angle** (Ahlawat & Associates, VJM Global, Commenda, KDP Accountants) cover largely the same ground AU Corporate's cluster already covers (entity types, Automatic/Government route, registration steps, banking, labour compliance, ongoing compliance) — no major topical gap found there. (Note: WebFetch to commenda.io, india-briefing.com, ahlawatassociates.com and vjmglobal.com was blocked by the sandbox's egress proxy for all four domains, so this comparison relies on WebSearch summaries rather than direct page reads — flagging this as a real limitation, not glossing over it.)

**Conclusion on intent:** search intent has **not** shifted in a way this page should chase (see Search Intent Assessment below) — the position problem is more likely a mix of (a) genuine low domain authority on a very competitive head term the site is realistically not going to win outright, which no on-page edit fixes, and (b) real, fixable structural/on-page issues found below.

## Search Intent Assessment
**Unchanged**, but clarified rather than shifted. The literal head term "India business setup" sits inside a broad SERP that includes domestic-founder content AU Corporate deliberately doesn't compete for (confirmed against the whole cluster's own metadata scoping — every sibling page explicitly targets "foreign companies"). This refresh does not attempt to broaden the page into that domestic territory — doing so would misalign with the firm's actual service line and every sibling page's positioning, and would be exactly the kind of full-rewrite-chasing-the-SERP move the refresh-agent guardrails caution against. Instead, the fixes target **structural/on-page issues that were genuinely present and fixable**: an internal inconsistency between stated and actual page structure (five sections promised, seven delivered), a primary CTA pointing outside the topic cluster, missing differentiation signalling versus the sibling pillar, and thinner structured-data/metadata than the SERP's better-resourced competitors likely carry. None of these alone is guaranteed to move a position-~80 ranking on a highly competitive head term against much higher-authority domains — that ceiling is a real, acknowledged limitation of what on-page content work can do here — but they are the legitimate, evidence-backed levers available at the content layer, and they were genuinely broken/missing, not invented busywork.
