## Claims That Could Not Be Verified — MUST FIX

| Claim | Issue |
|---|---|
| **"India has a network of RoC offices under the MCA, with most states having one and a couple of the larger ones — Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu — having two."** (body copy, "Does Company Registration in India Really Differ From State to State?" section) | **This is now factually outdated and must be corrected before publish.** The MCA reorganized RoC jurisdictions nationally, effective 16 February 2026 (under Sections 396(1)/396(2), Companies Act, 2013, amending an earlier notification dated 23 October 2025; implementation deferred from 1 January to 16 February 2026). Under the new structure: **Maharashtra now has three RoCs — Mumbai-I, Mumbai-II and Nagpur — not two.** Delhi now has two RoCs (Delhi-I, Delhi-II). West Bengal now has two RoCs (Kolkata-I, Kolkata-II). Uttar Pradesh now has two RoCs (UP-I/Kanpur, UP-II/NOIDA). Reported totals vary slightly by source (roughly 20 offices before the reorganization, ~23 after), but the specific claim in the draft — that Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu are the only two states with more than one RoC — is incorrect as of today's date (this reorganization has been in effect for over seven months). Tamil Nadu's "two RoCs" (Chennai, Coimbatore) claim is still accurate and was unaffected by this reorganization. **Recommended fix:** either remove the specific "which states have two" enumeration and just state the general principle (RoC is determined by registered office location, full detail on request/via MCA), or update it to reflect the current post-February-2026 structure (Maharashtra: three; Delhi, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh: two each; Tamil Nadu: two) sourced directly from MCA's own current RoC jurisdiction notification — do not simply substitute the figures found in secondary reporting into the body without confirming against MCA's own published notification, since WebFetch could not reach mca.gov.in this session to pull the notification directly. |

## Regulatory Currency Check

**One material issue found:** The MCA reorganized Registrar of Companies jurisdictions nationally, effective 16 February 2026 (notification under Sections 396(1)/396(2) of the Companies Act, 2013). This post-dates whatever source the Stage 1/2 research relied on (the draft's Writer's Notes flag the RoC claim as sourced from "a Wikipedia snippet," which evidently reflected the pre-reorganization structure). As of today's date, the draft's statement that only Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu have more than one RoC is out of date — Maharashtra now has three RoCs, and Delhi, West Bengal and Uttar Pradesh now have two each in addition to Tamil Nadu. This must be corrected before publish (see table above); it is a genuine regulatory-currency gap, not a hypothetical one.

No other superseded rule, rate or process was identified in the draft. Specifically checked and found current/consistent with the draft's framing: the SPICe+ incorporation process and its national uniformity, the constitutional (Article 276) basis and structure of Professional Tax, the State List basis for stamp duty, IFSCA's status as unified IFSC regulator, the PLI Scheme's federal (not state) administration, and the FEMA/DPIIT FDI Automatic/Government Route framework. The draft's own repeated caution — that specific rates, thresholds and rankings should be confirmed against primary sources at the time of use rather than treated as fixed from any guide including this one — is well-founded and should be retained, not softened, in the published version.

## Overall Fact-Check Verdict

**Needs writer revision** — one specific, currently-incorrect factual claim (the RoC office count/"Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu have two" statement) must be corrected before publish, per the table above. This is a contained, single-paragraph fix, not a structural problem with the piece: the draft's overall approach of describing mechanisms rather than asserting unverified figures, and explicitly deferring capital-band-specific stamp duty numbers, GIFT City incentive specifics, and state incentive percentages, is exactly the right posture for a piece touching this many regulatory domains and should be preserved as-is.

Once the RoC claim is corrected (and ideally verified directly against MCA's current RoC jurisdiction notification, since this fact-check could not reach mca.gov.in or pib.gov.in directly due to egress restrictions this session), this piece is publish-ready as blog-tier content **on the basis of what is actually stated in the body copy**. However, because this is AU Corporate content in a regulated professional-services domain (company law, tax, FEMA-adjacent commentary), and because several of the items in the "Needs Human Professional Verification" table above concern figures the piece deliberately holds in reserve for likely future revisions (stamp duty tables, PT cap, incentive percentages, GIFT City specifics), a qualified AU Corporate professional should still review the piece prior to publication and treat this check as the record of what was and was not independently confirmed — not as a substitute for that sign-off.
