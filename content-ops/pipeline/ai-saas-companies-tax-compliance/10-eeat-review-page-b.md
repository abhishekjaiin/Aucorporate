## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The three-scenario decision framework** ("Which Situation Are You In?") is the clearest EEAT signal on the page. It's not a generic "factors to consider" list — it gives concrete, checkable signals (a signed PO requiring India-registered invoicing, hiring outgrowing an EOR, a tender requiring proof of entity) and a direct consequence for each. This reads as distilled from actually having these conversations with clients, exactly as Stage 5's positioning brief intended it to.
- **The entity-selection reasoning** (WOS vs. Liaison/Branch/Project/JV) explains *why* each structure fits or doesn't fit a SaaS/AI business's actual operating model (can't invoice from a Liaison Office; RBI-approval-gated activity list on a Branch; JV only when a local partner's market access is actually wanted) rather than reciting a static entity-comparison table. This is mechanism-first reasoning, matching the tone brief's explicit instruction.
- **The SEP framing** ("a foreign company can trigger a taxable presence... purely through the scale of its India-sourced digital transactions... independent of whether it has incorporated anything here at all") correctly explains *why* delay has a cost, rather than just asserting a section number. Precision on distinguishing SEP (direct tax) from OIDAR/GST (indirect tax) as "different tax, different trigger, different consequence" mirrors the same correct distinction Page A makes — good evidence of actual command of the two regimes, not confusion between them (which Stage 5 specifically flagged as a realistic error risk).
- **Honest, once-stated hedges** on genuinely uncertain/judgment-dependent points (SEP thresholds, FC-GPR window durability, tax-election suitability, FDI sector edge cases) read as a professional flagging real uncertainty, not anxious AI-generated qualifier-stacking. This is calibrated correctly per Stage 5's explicit tone instruction to avoid "it is generally advisable to consider potentially exploring..." language.

## Where It Falls Short

**1. FAQ/body inconsistency on the OIDAR-to-GST transition (found and corrected during this review).**
Stage 9 required the writer to stop implying the OIDAR registration "automatically converts" into the new entity's GST registration, since these are two separate registrations under two separate legal persons requiring an affirmative wind-down step. The writer's notes claimed this was "fixed throughout the page," and the main body (GST-after-incorporation section, lines ~93–95) and the Scenario 3 framework text were correctly reworded. However, the **FAQ answer to "How does GST work once we've incorporated, versus the OIDAR rule we were under before?"** had retained the original, unfixed language: *"Your existing OIDAR obligation doesn't just disappear; it transitions into the entity's regular GST filing cadence (GSTR-1/GSTR-3B replacing GSTR-5A) as part of the incorporation process."* This is exactly the overstated-certainty framing Stage 9 flagged — it reads as an automatic, seamless changeover "as part of the incorporation process," directly contradicting the correctly hedged body copy two sections above it. A careful reader (or a competitor/regulator) comparing the FAQ to the body would notice AU Corporate's own page contradicting itself on a real compliance mechanic. This is precisely the kind of "reads more certain than verification status supports" risk this review was asked to specifically watch for, given the site's precedent of a fabricated case citation being caught only at fact-check. **I corrected this FAQ answer directly** to match the properly hedged body language (two separate registrations, affirmative wind-down required, no automatic conversion) — see the updated `07-draft-page-b.md`.

**2. Byline format inconsistency with Page A (minor, not a trust risk).**
Page B: "*Last updated: 15 September 2026 — prepared by AU Corporate's cross-border tax and India business-setup practice.*" Page A: "*Last updated 2026 · Compliance guide for foreign digital-services providers.*" Page B's byline is more specific (exact date, named practice area); Page A's is vaguer on both counts. Not a trust failure on either page individually, but the pair should read as one coherent editorial product — recommend the orchestrator standardize both to the fuller Page-B format (specific date + named practice attribution) before publish.
