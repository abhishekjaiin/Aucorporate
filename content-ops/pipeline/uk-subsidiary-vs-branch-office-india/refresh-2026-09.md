## New Authoritative Information Found
1. **India's Section 115A royalty/FTS withholding rate was *increased*, not cut, by the Finance Act, 2023** — doubled from 10% to 20% (plus surcharge/cess) for non-residents, effective 1 April 2023, unless a valid DTAA claim is made. Verified via TaxGuru, AZB Partners, BDO Global, and MBG Corp coverage of the Finance Act 2023 amendment to Section 115A. (Note: this corrects a direction error present on the sibling AU page, which states Finance Act 2023 "cut" the domestic royalty/FTS rate to 10% — that AU page was not in scope for this task and was left untouched, but the correct direction was used when adding the new royalty/FTS row and FAQ to this UK page.)
2. **India-UK DTAA Article 13 (royalties/FTS) has no "make available" clause**, unlike the India-US treaty — so the 15%/10% treaty cap applies to all managerial, technical, and consultancy fees regardless of whether know-how transfers. Verified via VJM Global, Croner Navigate (Article 13 commentary), and Tax2win's DTAA guide. This was a genuine content gap: the page had a dividend-withholding DTAA row but nothing on royalty/FTS withholding, which is directly relevant to intercompany management-fee flows from an Indian subsidiary back to a UK parent.
3. **UK CFC rules — Part 9A, Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010** — a wholly UK-owned Indian subsidiary is generally a CFC under this regime; actual CFC charges depend on Part 9A's gateway/exemption tests (not asserted as automatic either way). Verified via legislation.gov.uk (Finance Act 2012 explanatory notes introducing Part 9A) and corroborated via Beacon Filing's CFC-rules-for-India-subsidiaries explainer. This closes the "home-country tax treatment" gap the US sibling page already covers (Subpart F/NCTI) but the UK page previously did not, for the UK's own regime.
4. **UK foreign branch profits exemption — Chapter 3A, Part 2, Corporation Tax Act 2009** — a UK company can make an irrevocable election to exempt foreign branch profits/losses from UK corporation tax; without it, worldwide taxation applies with DTAA Article 24 credit relief. Verified via legislation.gov.uk (CTA 2009 Part 2 Chapter 3A) and gov.uk's "Corporation Tax — reform of the foreign permanent establishment exemption" consultation page.
5. **HMRC's 2026 technical consultation proposing to make the Chapter 3A branch exemption *mandatory*** (rather than elective) for accounting periods from 2027, consultation closed 7 September 2026. Verified via the gov.uk consultation listing and corroborated by independent commentary (Tanous & Co., "Mandatory Foreign PE Exemption From 2027"). **Not yet enacted** — added as a forward-looking, explicitly-dated note, mirroring how the existing RBI-draft callout is framed (draft/consultation, not current law), not as settled fact.
6. **RBI's October 2025 draft branch/liaison office regulations** (already on the page from the August refresh) — re-checked for any notification since; found no evidence of notification as of the most recent available coverage (confirmed still-draft status through December 2025 sources, no later notification found). Rewrote the callout's date-qualifier from "As of August 2026" to "As of the most recently available information" to avoid re-asserting a specific cutoff date each refresh cycle without fresh verification of that exact date, while keeping the substance (still a draft) unchanged and accurate.
