## Claims That Could Not Be Verified — MUST FIX

| Claim | Issue |
|---|---|
| "A manufacturing or contract-manufacturing arrangement... is more typically benchmarked using a cost-plus or resale-price method" (US Parent's Own Tax Exposure section) | This appears to overstate cost-plus/resale-price relative to actual Indian TP practice. Multiple sources indicate the **Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM)**, applied using operating profit/total cost for a contract manufacturer, is the **most widely applied method in Indian transfer-pricing practice**, specifically because traditional transactional methods (CUP, RPM, Cost Plus) require granular comparable data that is rarely available. Cost-plus and resale-price are the theoretically "cleaner" textbook methods for manufacturing/distribution fact patterns, but asserting they are "more typically" used is not well-supported and could mislead a reader who then expects their transfer pricing study to default to cost-plus. Recommend revising to something like: "in Indian TP practice, contract-manufacturing arrangements are most commonly benchmarked using TNMM (operating margin on total cost), though cost-plus or resale-price methods may apply depending on the specific fact pattern" — this is a correction to make before publish, not just a hedge. |
| "Environmental clearance specifically can take anywhere from roughly a month to several months depending on the plant's pollution category and whether it requires a full Environmental Impact Assessment" | Research found that industrial projects requiring a **full EIA (Category A projects)** commonly take **12 to 18 months** end-to-end for environmental clearance — materially longer than "several months." The draft's range likely holds for simpler Category B/exempted cases (Consent to Establish/Operate without a full EIA) but understates the upper end for projects that do require a full EIA, which the draft's own conditional clause explicitly contemplates. Recommend widening the upper bound (e.g., "a few weeks for simpler consent processes up to 12-18 months where a full Environmental Impact Assessment is required") rather than leaving "several months" as the ceiling. |
| PLI incentive-rate upper bound stated as "high teens to mid-20s percent of incremental sales" | Could not find any source — primary or secondary — supporting incentive rates in the "mid-20s" range. Government/secondary sources found: a commonly-cited overall range of roughly 1%-20% (drug intermediates/APIs cited at the ~20% high end), specialty steel at 4-12%, mobile/electronics at 4-6%. Recommend revising the upper bound downward to "up to roughly 20%" unless a specific sector notification supporting a higher figure can be produced — as written, "mid-20s" is not a figure this check could substantiate and should not be published without a specific citation. |

## Regulatory Currency Check

- **PLI → ECMS/successor-scheme transition**: Confirmed as a real, actively moving target. ECMS was notified in April 2025 and its outlay was already revised upward (₹22,919 crore → ₹40,000 crore) within roughly ten months, in the Union Budget 2026-27. The draft's explicit "don't structure your investment case around original PLI terms as settled" framing is well-supported and should not be softened.
- **Section 115BAB / 15% concessional manufacturing rate**: This is the one place this check found a genuine, unresolved regulatory-currency gap the draft does not surface at all. The commence-manufacturing sunset condition (originally 31 March 2023, extended once to 31 March 2024) appears — on the bulk of evidence found — not to have been further extended for new entrants, which would mean the incentive is not currently open to a company only now planning its India entry, unless a subsequent extension exists that this session's tools could not confirm. Combined with the underlying provision being renumbered under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), this section of the draft is the one area of the page most likely to be stale or materially incomplete as currently written. See the "Needs Human Professional Verification" table above — this should be treated as the top-priority open item from this fact-check, ranking above the Form 5472 point (which checked out cleanly) in terms of remaining publish risk.
- **Press Note 3**: current and correctly reflects the March 2026 relaxation; no outdated content found.
- **Factories Act, 1948**: still the governing central statute for factory registration; note that several states have their own Factories Rules with local variations on procedural detail (though not, to this check's knowledge, on the core Section 2(m) worker-count threshold itself). The draft does not claim national uniformity beyond the threshold and notice-period points, which is appropriate, but a one-line note that "state rules may add procedural detail beyond the central Act" would close a small residual gap.
- **GST RCM on import of services**: settled law, no currency concerns identified.
- **Form 5472 / Form 5471**: settled, well-established IRS framework; no currency concerns identified.

## Overall Fact-Check Verdict

**Needs human professional sign-off before publish.**

Reasoning: two items are genuine Must-Fix corrections the writer can address directly (the transfer-pricing benchmarking-method overstatement, and the environmental-clearance timeline's understated upper bound for full-EIA projects) — these do not require a licensed professional's judgment call, just a text revision grounded in the sourcing above. However, the 115BAB eligibility-window finding is exactly the kind of time-sensitive, judgment-dependent regulatory question this pipeline's guardrails require escalating to a qualified human tax professional at AU Corporate rather than resolving via web research alone — the evidence found is directionally concerning (possible closed window for new entrants) but not conclusive (one ambiguous, unconfirmed 2026 extension reference was found and could not be resolved either way). Every other flagged item in the Writer's Notes (PLI sector count, rate ranges, investment thresholds, local value-addition, ECMS terms, Factories Act thresholds/notice period, Form 5472 direction) checked out as either fully verified or appropriately still-hedged and does not block publication on its own. The page should not go live until (a) the two Must-Fix text corrections are made, and (b) a qualified AU Corporate tax professional has confirmed the current, correct framing of the 15%-concessional-rate eligibility window (both the sunset-date question and, ideally, the Income-tax Act 2025 renumbering) — consistent with how the page already, correctly, handles the PLI/ECMS transition as an explicitly time-bound caveat rather than a settled fact.
