This page can *reference* that real capability (with a link, not a re-pitch) as evidence AU Corporate's existing process already extends naturally to AI billing structures, without re-running the sibling page's 3-step / software-comparison content as if it were new.

## Explicit Guidance for the Writer

- **Do not re-pitch the 3-step data-export/sort/file process or the software-vs-managed-service comparison table as if inventing them here.** They already exist, verbatim in spirit, on the sibling page. If referenced at all, reference them as "how this gets operationalized once you know your position" with a link — one or two sentences, not a re-run section.
- **Do not assert that India's CGST s.9(5) deemed-supplier mechanism applies to AI API marketplaces.** Per Stage 1/4 research, it currently covers only passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping, and restaurant services. State this as "not yet extended here — worth monitoring," matching the honest-uncertainty tone already modeled on the OIDAR page.
- **Do not fabricate specific rate/threshold/registration mechanics for any jurisdiction outside the verified 13** (US, Canada, UK, EU/OSS, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Serbia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, China, India, Turkey). "87 jurisdictions" may appear once as a headline coverage claim (consistent with the sibling page's positioning); do not imply detailed mechanics exist on-page beyond the 13.
- **Do not claim AU Corporate is a Merchant of Record or offers MoR services.** The MoR-vs-deemed-supplier section should draw a clean, factual distinction between a commercial arrangement (MoR) and a regulatory liability assignment (deemed supplier) — it should read as clarifying two already-documented concepts accurately, not as a pitch against MoR vendors. Avoid competitor-bashing language (e.g., "unlike MoR platforms that just...") — state the distinction, let the reader draw the commercial conclusion.
- **Do not claim AU Corporate offers tax-automation software or an API/integration product.** AU Corporate's real model is advisory + managed filing from exported data, already established on the sibling page. Nothing in this topic's research supports (or should invent) a software capability.
- **Avoid resolving genuinely unsettled law with false confidence.** EU ViDA scope, evolving US state rulings (Indiana/Illinois), and any "how will regulators eventually treat AI marketplaces" question should be framed as developing, with a visible last-reviewed date, consistent with the OIDAR page's existing pattern and Stage 4's table-stakes item #5.
- **Write the decision framework and the seller/exporter-side section as genuine advisory judgment, not a sales funnel.** These are the two areas where AU Corporate has real differentiated standing (per the Right-to-Win section above) — keep the tone at "here is how to think about your specific situation," matching the OIDAR page's `ScenarioSelector`-style decision-support pattern, rather than "contact us to find out." CTAs belong in dedicated lead-form/inline-CTA sections, not woven into the technical explanation itself — mirror the restraint the OIDAR page already shows (technical sections stay technical; CTAs are visually separate).
- **Cross-link explicitly and early**, not just in a closing "related resources" block: to `/global-vat-compliance-ai-saas-companies` for general jurisdiction/filing mechanics, to `/oidar-gst-registration-india` for inbound India OIDAR registration mechanics, and to `/india-entry-for-saas-companies` for readers whose actual next question is entity setup rather than billing-tax classification. This is both an internal-linking/SEO hygiene point and a genuine reader service — a reader on the wrong page for their situation should be routed, not stranded.
- **Overall register**: professional, precise, slightly technical (matches the OIDAR page's citation-heavy, statute-referencing style) — not a generic "AI is changing everything" marketing voice. The differentiation the whole pipeline has converged on is depth and resolution of genuinely unresolved questions, not breadth or brand enthusiasm. If a section reads like it's persuading rather than explaining, cut the persuasion.
