## The Framing Problem With the Brief (read first)
The brief's internal phrasing — "AI, SaaS and technology companies" tax compliance — describes AU Corporate's *client segment*, not a query pattern. Nothing in Stage 1's research shows anyone searching "AI and SaaS companies tax compliance," "technology companies international tax," or similar bundled phrasing. Real demand in this SERP fractures into two structurally distinct, separately-competed clusters with different searcher jobs-to-be-done:

- **Cluster A — GST/OIDAR compliance obligation** ("do I need to register, what do I file, what's the penalty") — triggered by having Indian customers, not a physical presence.
- **Cluster B — India market entry** ("how do I set up an entity/subsidiary in India") — triggered by a decision to establish a formal presence.

These are asked by overlapping but not identical searchers, at different points in the buyer journey, and are answered by different (though overlapping) competitor sets in Stage 1's data. This split is flagged explicitly in the "Single Page vs. Two Pages" section below — it is a strategic question for the orchestrator/Stage 5, not resolved here.

A third, weaker cluster — **global VAT/GST on digital services** (EU OSS, etc.) — is real search volume but SERP-dominated entirely by VAT-tech software vendors (Fonoa, Anrok, Numeral, Avalara) with zero India-advisory-firm presence observed. AU Corporate has no demonstrated authority signal to compete there; treat as supporting/context material only, not a targeting cluster.

## Foreign/International Framing Check

**Evidence found for international-audience phrasing:**
- Stage 1 confirms Treelife's title is literally "OIDAR Registration in India for Foreign SaaS Providers" and IndiaFilings' title is "GST Registration for Foreign Companies in India" — both are direct competitor page titles containing "foreign," and both recurred across multiple query clusters. This is the one piece of real evidence that "foreign company/foreign SaaS provider" framing has SERP presence, not just audience relevance.
- A Stage 1 PAA-proxy question is phrased "How does GST apply to foreign SaaS companies selling to India" — again, "foreign" appears in the query itself, not only in AU Corporate's internal description of its client base.

**Conclusion:** Unlike a generic "foreign investor" framing (which the standing rule correctly warns against inserting without evidence), here there IS direct evidence — two independently-recurring competitor titles and one PAA-proxy question — that "foreign SaaS company/provider" and "foreign companies" are real query fragments in this specific niche, likely because the regulatory hook (OIDAR, NRTP) is itself defined around non-resident/foreign-supplier status. This justifies **using "foreign" as a natural modifier within body copy, a secondary keyword, or an FAQ answer** (e.g., "OIDAR registration in India for foreign SaaS providers," "GST registration for foreign companies in India") — but it should still not replace the plainer, higher-recurrence primary phrasing ("SaaS company GST registration India," "India entry for SaaS companies") in the title/H1, since those plain forms recurred more broadly and don't presuppose the searcher already thinks of themselves using regulatory/foreign-status language.
- Do NOT extend this evidence to "international" or "cross-border" broadly — those specific words were not observed in any recurring title or PAA-proxy question in Stage 1; only "foreign" (tied specifically to the non-resident/OIDAR regulatory context) has direct support.

## Single Page vs. Two Pages — Flag for Orchestrator / Stage 5

This is a strategic decision, not resolved here, but the keyword evidence is directional enough to flag clearly:

- **Cluster A (OIDAR/GST compliance)** and **Cluster B (India entry)** are asked by different searcher intents (obligation-driven vs. decision-driven), answered by a *mostly* overlapping but not identical competitor set, and Treelife — the single closest structural analog identified in Stage 1 — itself runs these as **two separate pages** ("India Entry for SaaS and Tech Companies" and "OIDAR Registration in India for Foreign SaaS Providers"), not one combined page.
- A single combined page under a brief-style primary keyword ("AI and SaaS companies tax compliance") has **no search-demand evidence at all** and risks diluting both clusters' on-page relevance signals against competitors who are single-topic-focused.
- If the orchestrator needs one page now for timeline reasons, "GST registration for SaaS companies in India" is the more defensible single primary (broader plain-language recurrence than "OIDAR"), with India-entry content demoted to a secondary section/CTA rather than a co-equal H1 target — but this is a compromise, not the ideal SEO structure per the evidence.
- Recommend Stage 4 (content-gap-analyst) and Stage 5 (au-corporate-strategist) weigh in explicitly on this before Stage 6 (content-architect) locks a single H1/URL.
