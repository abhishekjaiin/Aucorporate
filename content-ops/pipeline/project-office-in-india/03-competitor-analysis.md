## Per-Page Analysis

### setindiabiz.com/project-office-registration-india
**Does well:** Covers the core registration journey — an eligibility framing (foreign company awarded a project by a government or private Indian entity), a documented AD-bank/RBI approval step, and a downstream ROC filing (Form FC-1, 30 days) — in a sequence that plausibly matches how a founder or in-house counsel would actually move through the decision (can I even do this → how do I apply → what happens after approval). States plainly that RBI issues a UIN to the Project Office upon approval, without hedging.
**Does poorly:** No search evidence surfaced of dedicated tax/Permanent Establishment treatment, no visible closure/wind-up section specific to PO (closure content found in search results was for the sibling Branch Office page, not confirmed present on the PO page itself), and no primary-source citation (no link to or quote from the FEMA 22(R)/2016 text or the RBI Form FNC PDF) turned up in any synthesis — the regulatory claims read as asserted, not sourced. No FAQ block or dated "last reviewed" signal surfaced.
**Why it likely ranks:** Domain-level authority and topical breadth (setindiabiz ranks across the whole BO/LO/PO/company-registration cluster, not just this one page) plus a URL and title that exactly match the head query, rather than page-level content depth — this looks more authority/footprint-driven than uniquely comprehensive-content-driven, though this can't be confirmed against real traffic/backlink data this run (Semrush unavailable).
**What would beat it:** A page that actually cites its regulatory source (link the FEMA regulation and the specific RBI form), explicitly answers what happens if the PO doesn't get RBI's general-permission conditions met (specific-approval route vs. general-permission route — a distinction this page's synthesis didn't surface at all), and adds the closure and PE-risk sections this page appears to lack.

### taxguru.in/rbi/process-establishment-project-offices-india-foreign-entities.html
**Does well:** Walks the application mechanics more specifically than the commercial pages — names Form FNC, describes the AD Category-I bank's role and discretion "subject to Regulation 5," and includes a concrete operational detail the others miss: the six-month window to actually open the office after approval, with a possible further six-month extension "for reasons beyond the applicant's control." This is a genuinely useful, specific, decision-relevant fact (what happens if you're delayed) that a founder/CFO would want and that the commercial pages in this set didn't surface.
**Does poorly:** As an explainer/news-article format rather than a firm-authored guide, it carries no visible author credential, byline, or firm identity in what surfaced — a trust-signal gap relative to a page written and stood behind by a named advisory firm. No search evidence of FAQ formatting, no CTA (expected, since it's not a service page, but also means it doesn't help a reader who's ready to act). No PE/tax-risk content surfaced. taxguru.in's own footprint is split across 5+ separate URLs on this exact topic cluster (per Stage 1), which suggests fragmented rather than consolidated coverage — a reader has to piece together the process page, the comparison page, and the closure page separately rather than getting one authoritative page.
**Why it likely ranks:** Domain-level trust built from a very large, long-running library of tax/regulatory explainers (the site ranks for thousands of adjacent queries), plus a title that closely matches "process ... project offices ... foreign entities" search phrasing — again reads as footprint/domain-authority driven rather than this specific page being unusually deep.
**What would beat it:** Consolidate what taxguru.in spreads across five URLs (process, eligibility, comparison, closure, FAQ) into one page, keep taxguru's genuinely useful specific detail (the six-month/extension mechanic) but add what it lacks — visible authorship/credentials, a real FAQ block, and the PE/tax angle.

### vjmglobal.com/blog/rbi-approval-liaison-office-branch-office-project-office (+ companion closure post)
**Does well:** Splits the topic into a registration post and a dedicated closure post — the closure post specifically is the most operationally detailed closure walkthrough found in this set: CA certificate in the RBI-prescribed format, ROC deregistration, AD-bank submission with the CA certificate and ROC closure certificate, AD-bank compliance verification, and AD-bank reporting to RBI with a closure declaration. That is a real, step-level sequence a CFO planning a project wind-down would need, and none of the other four pages analyzed matched that level of closure specificity.
**Does poorly:** The registration-side synthesis on the UIN point was the least coherent of any page checked — the search summary produced a garbled, seemingly self-contradictory statement about AD banks and UIN for Project Office that did not read as a clean, confident position either way. If that reflects genuine ambiguity or hedging in the source text, it's a missed opportunity to be the page that actually resolves the question; if it reflects a badly-worded original, that's a clarity problem competitors should be judged on either way. No PE/tax content surfaced for the PO angle specifically (VJM's site does cover PE elsewhere, per Stage 1's ahlawatassociates-style entries, but not integrated into this page).
**Why it likely ranks:** A CA firm with an active blog covering the full BO/LO/PO lifecycle including two India-entry-adjacent posts (registration + closure) gives it topical completeness signals search engines reward, plus internal linking between the registration and closure posts that keeps users (and likely crawl equity) within the same content cluster.
**What would beat it:** Keep the closure-process specificity as the bar to match (not copy), but pair it with a clean, confidently-sourced answer on the UIN question and fold the PE-risk angle into the same page rather than leaving it in a separate, unlinked piece of content.

### kbcca.com/project-office-in-India.php
**Does well:** Positioned explicitly as a full-lifecycle page (title: "Setup, Compliance & Closure Guide") and the synthesis confirms it does cover all three phases in one place — general-permission vs. RBI-approval framing at setup, ongoing ROC/PAN/TAN/GST/audit/ITR compliance, and closure documentation for both RBI and ROC. Structurally, this is the closest match in the set to "one page that covers the whole PO journey" rather than a fragmented cluster.
**Does poorly:** The compliance section as surfaced reads generically — GST "if applicable," "corporate tax planning," "ensuring compliance with Indian tax laws" — without naming the specific recurring compliance items Stage 1 identified as central to this topic (Annual Activity Certificate filed with the AD bank and DGIT by 31 March, the FLA return, ITR-6 specifically). No PE/Permanent-Establishment framing surfaced despite PO carrying materially higher PE risk than LO. No UIN position surfaced either way in the synthesis — a real gap on the topic's single most contested fact.
**Why it likely ranks:** Full-funnel structural completeness (setup + compliance + closure named explicitly in the title and delivered in the body) is a genuine content strength here, not just domain authority — this looks like one of the more organically content-driven rankings in the set, though again unconfirmed against real traffic data.
**What would beat it:** Replace the generic compliance language with the specific named forms/deadlines (AAC/31 March, FLA, ITR-6), add the PE-risk section entirely, and take an explicit, sourced position on the UIN question rather than leaving it unaddressed.

### nishithdesai.com — FAQs on Setting Up of Offices in India (PDF)
**Does well:** The one page in this set carrying genuine legal-authority weight — a large, well-known India law firm's own FAQ document, which is the kind of source that gets cited by other advisors rather than just competing with them. The synthesis surfaced real procedural specificity not found elsewhere: it names the applicable annex of Form FNC by letter for liaison offices, distinguishes it from the project-office route, and describes AD-bank due-diligence criteria (promoter antecedents, nature/location of activity, source of funds, KYC) in more institutional language than the commercial pages use.
**Does poorly — and this is the load-bearing finding of this report:** this document is the one source in the entire set that states flatly, with no hedge, that **no UIN is allotted to a Project Office** — directly contradicting taxguru.in, setindiabiz.com, and masllp.com, which all state (also with no hedge) that RBI does issue a UIN for the Project Office upon approval. Neither position, on either side, is accompanied by a citation to the actual regulation text or a link to the RBI form in what surfaced. That is the real, citable competitor-vs-competitor contradiction the brief asked me to try to resolve.
**Why it likely ranks (for the adjacent/indirect query set, not necessarily this exact page one-for-one):** Firm reputation and citation-worthiness — the kind of authority that produces backlinks from other law firms and publishers even without aggressive on-page SEO optimization. Its ranking strength, if any exists specifically for this query, is very plausibly authority/backlink-driven rather than content-freshness-driven — no PDF "last updated" date surfaced in the synthesis, and Big-law FAQ PDFs of this kind are typically republished infrequently.
**What would beat it:** A page that has equivalent institutional-grade precision (naming specific form annexes, describing AD-bank due-diligence criteria properly) but resolves the UIN contradiction transparently — either by actually reading and citing the primary Regulation 5/Form FNC-1 text and stating the answer with a citation, or, if the primary text genuinely can't be pinned down, by explicitly naming that other reputable sources disagree and explaining why (general-permission/reporting route vs. specific-approval route may be the actual distinction being conflated) rather than picking a side with no sourcing at all, the way every page in this set — including Nishith Desai's — currently does.

## Cross-Page Patterns

**What most strong pages share:**
- A three-part backbone — eligibility/who-qualifies, application mechanics (AD bank → RBI), and what happens after approval (ROC/PAN/TAN) — appears in every page analyzed. This is the structural spine the whole ranking cluster has converged on, not a differentiator by itself.
- Every page frames the topic through the lens of "foreign company already has a specific contract/project" as the entry condition — the eligibility gate is universally treated as the natural opening, matching how Stage 2 read the primary keyword's intent.
- None of the five pages cited a primary source directly (no visible link to or quote from the FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB text, the RBI Master Direction, or the actual RBI form PDF) in what the search syntheses surfaced. Regulatory claims across the board read as assertions the reader has to take on the firm's/publisher's word.
- None of the five pages visibly showed an author name, professional credential, or a "last reviewed/updated" date in what surfaced — a trust-signal gap across the whole set, not just one competitor. (This is a search-synthesis limitation as much as a confirmed absence — but its consistency across five independently-checked domains is itself notable.)

**What's uniformly weak across all of them:**
- The single most contested, decision-relevant fact on this entire topic — whether RBI issues a UIN to a Project Office — is answered with total confidence and zero sourcing on both sides of a real contradiction, and not one of the five pages acknowledges that other reputable sources disagree. This is the clearest, most concrete gap in the entire competitive set.
- PE/Permanent-Establishment risk — which Stage 1 and Stage 2 both flagged as especially significant for POs specifically (construction/installation PE, the shorter treaty thresholds that apply to project sites) — is essentially absent from the commercial/service-page cluster (setindiabiz, vjmglobal, kbcca) and only shows up in a separate, disconnected tier of tax-journal content (per Stage 1's bcajonline.org/taxsutra/ahlawatassociates.com findings), meaning no page in this set integrates "how do I register a PO" with "what tax exposure does a PO specifically create" in one place.
- Compliance detail, where present at all, is generic ("ensure compliance," "corporate tax planning") rather than naming the specific recurring items (AAC deadline of 31 March, FLA return, ITR-6) that Stage 1 identified as the actual recurring compliance entities across the topic.
- No page in this set surfaced content on the sectoral/geographic prior-approval triggers (Pakistan-origin applicants; Bangladesh/Sri Lanka/Afghanistan/Iran/China/Hong Kong/Macau applicants seeking NE India/J&K/Andaman locations; Defence/Private Security/Telecom/I&B sectors) that Stage 1 flagged as a real gating condition — a founder relying on any of these five pages alone could miss that their specific situation requires RBI Central Office approval rather than the AD-bank general-permission route.
- No MCA (mca.gov.in) primary source appeared to be cited by any of the five pages despite Section 380/Form FC-1/FC-2 being central to the ROC side of the process — consistent with Stage 1's Open Question #5.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely better AU Corporate page needs to do four specific things none of the five analyzed pages do together:
1. **Take a sourced, transparent position on the UIN question** — either cite the actual Regulation 5/Form FNC-1 text and state the answer with that citation, or explicitly name that sources disagree (Nishith Desai's FAQ vs. the taxguru.in/setindiabiz.com/masllp.com consensus) and explain the likely reason for the disagreement (general-permission/reporting route vs. specific-approval route), rather than asserting either answer with no sourcing the way every competitor page checked here does.
2. **Integrate PE/tax risk into the core page**, not as a separate, disconnected article — construction/installation PE thresholds and why a PO carries materially higher PE exposure than a Liaison Office specifically, tied to the registration/eligibility content on the same page.
3. **Name the specific recurring compliance items with their actual deadlines** (AAC as at 31 March, FLA return, ITR-6) instead of generic "ensure compliance" language, and cover the sectoral/geographic prior-approval triggers that gate whether a given applicant even qualifies for the general-permission/AD-bank route versus needing RBI Central Office approval.
4. **Show real sourcing and authorship** — link to the primary FEMA regulation and the actual RBI form, and carry visible author/credential and last-reviewed-date signals — closing the trust-signal gap that was consistently absent across every page in this set, commercial and informational alike.

None of this requires more words than the competitors already have — kbcca.com's page is already structured as a full lifecycle guide and still leaves the compliance section generic and the PE section absent entirely. The bar is precision and sourcing on the specific facts a founder or CFO actually needs to act on, not additional length.
