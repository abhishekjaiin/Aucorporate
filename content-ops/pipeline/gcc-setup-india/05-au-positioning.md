# AU Corporate Positioning: GCC / Global Capability Center Setup in India

## Relevant Service Lines for This Page
Only service lines that map to a real reader need at each stage of the GCC lifecycle (decide → incorporate → operate) belong here.

- **India Entry Strategy** — directly relevant: the reader's core question is "should we set up a GCC here, and how." This is the frame for the whole page, not a bolt-on service mention.
- **Company Incorporation / Business Setup** — directly relevant: entity choice (Pvt Ltd WOS vs. branch vs. LLP vs. BOT-JV) and the MCA/SPICe+ mechanics are exactly what the entity-choice decision framework (Stage 4's top differentiator) needs to be grounded in. AU Corp already publishes SPICe+-level incorporation content at `app/india-business-setup/company-formation/page.tsx` — this page should be consistent with, not a rewrite of, that content.
- **FEMA & RBI Compliance** — directly relevant, and AU Corp's strongest right-to-win angle here. The site already commits to naming FC-GPR (share allotment), FC-TRS (transfer), and the annual FLA return on multiple live pages (`india-entry-for-singapore-companies`, `india-entry-for-australian-companies`). The GCC page should extend this exact claim into a fuller post-incorporation compliance calendar (deadlines, not just form names) rather than introduce a different or inconsistent set of forms.
- **FDI Advisory** — directly relevant: automatic vs. government route, sector conditions. Ties straight into the entity-choice framework and is already covered generally at `app/india-business-setup/fdi-channels/page.tsx`.
- **GST Advisory & Compliance** — relevant but secondary: address as a standard post-incorporation registration/compliance item for a GCC entity (this is a genuine gap per Stage 4 — competitors barely touch GST for a standard, non-IFSC GCC). Do not expand into a full GST advisory pitch; one clear paragraph/section is enough.
- **Transfer Pricing** — relevant and a genuine differentiator: a GCC is definitionally a captive cost/service center for a foreign parent, which makes intercompany pricing and safe-harbour margin treatment a first-order concern, not a footnote. This is where Stage 4's "worked numeric example" opportunity lives most naturally.
- **International Tax** — relevant at a lighter touch: DTAA/withholding treatment on any cross-charge or royalty/service-fee arrangement between the GCC and its parent. Keep this concise and point to the region-specific DTAA pages (e.g., `india-uk-dtaa-withholding-tax`) rather than re-explaining each treaty here.
- **Accounting & Outsourcing / Virtual CFO** — relevant for the "Operate" phase only: once a GCC entity exists, it needs bookkeeping, MIS reporting, and finance-function support exactly as described on `app/outsourcing/page.tsx` and within `app/india-entry-for-*-companies` pages. Frame this as "what a GCC needs after it's live," not as a service pitch layered onto the setup guide.
- **Payroll / HR Compliance** — relevant for the same reason, tied to `app/hr-services/page.tsx`: a GCC's whole reason for existing is headcount, so payroll/statutory-compliance mechanics (PF, ESI, professional tax, labour law registrations) are a legitimate, expected post-incorporation topic — not filler.
- **Audit & Assurance** — mention only briefly as an ongoing statutory obligation (all Indian Pvt Ltd entities require statutory audit); do not expand into a pitch.

## Service Lines Deliberately Excluded
- **Business Valuation** — no natural connection to a GCC setup guide; forcing it in would look like service-shelf padding.
- **Risk Advisory / Internal Controls** as standalone named services — the underlying substance (governance, controls) is legitimately part of "what makes a GCC succeed operationally" (the NASSCOM-owned angle Stage 4 flags as worth bridging into legal-setup content), but this should be discussed as operational guidance, not branded as an "AU Corp Risk Advisory service" pitch. Naming the service line here would misrepresent AU Corp's actual role (advisory/compliance, not GCC governance implementation).
- **Transaction Advisory** — no connection; a GCC is not typically an M&A/deal event. Do not include.
- **Managed-GCC / Build-Operate-Transfer / GCC-as-a-service** — explicitly not an AU Corp service line. AU Corp must not be positioned as competing with ANSR/Zinnov/Wisemonk-style GCC operators, real estate/workspace providers, or staffing-at-scale vendors. Any mention of the BOT model must stay descriptive (explaining what it is, as one entity-structure option) and never read as "AU Corp can build and run your GCC for you."
- **Coworking/managed-office space** — outside AU Corp's lane entirely (this is wework.co.in/JLL territory per Stage 1). Do not address beyond a passing acknowledgment that location/office choice is a separate workstream from the legal/tax/compliance one AU Corp advises on.

## International-Investor Considerations Relevant Here
This topic is squarely international-investor-facing (a GCC is by definition a foreign parent's captive entity), so more of the checklist applies here than on a generic topic — but still scoped to what a GCC-specific reader needs, not a repeat of every india-entry page:
- **Indian entity structures** — yes, central: Pvt Ltd (WOS) is the default recommendation for most GCCs; branch office and BOT/JV are the real alternatives worth naming with criteria (headcount at launch, IP sensitivity, sector FDI limit, timeline pressure, exit flexibility) per Stage 4's decision-framework opportunity. EOR-first as a pre-incorporation bridge option is worth a short mention since it's a real, distinct reader question ("do we need to incorporate before we hire anyone").
- **FDI route** — yes, needs to be explicit: automatic route covers the large majority of GCC-relevant sectors (IT/ITES, R&D, shared services); flag that sector-specific conditions can change this and point the reader to get sector-specific confirmation rather than presenting automatic-route access as universal.
- **FEMA/RBI angle** — yes, this is the differentiator: name FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA return deadlines explicitly and consistently with what's already live elsewhere on the site (see overlap section below) — do not introduce new/different form names or deadlines without verifying they match what AU Corp already commits to publicly.
- **Tax treatment** — yes: corporate tax rate applicable to the entity, safe-harbour rules for IT/ITES-type captive service margins (the 2026 Union Budget's unified 15.5% safe-harbour figure surfaced in Stage 1/2 research must be verified against a primary IT Department source before publishing — do not publish a specific rate without primary-source confirmation), transfer-pricing documentation obligation, and DTAA relief on any parent-company cross-charge.
- **GST** — yes, briefly: registration obligation, and (only if GIFT City/IFSC is in scope — see below) the distinct IFSC GST/indirect-tax treatment.
- **Accounting/compliance obligations** — yes: statutory audit, ROC annual filings, board/AGM requirements — standard Pvt Ltd obligations, framed specifically for a GCC entity's context (multiple stakeholders: local board plus foreign parent reporting).
- **Post-incorporation requirements** — yes, this is Stage 4's single clearest gap (month-by-month compliance calendar) and should be a core section, not an afterthought.
- **Practical India-entry considerations** — yes, lightly: city selection should be addressed (it's table stakes per Stage 4), but AU Corp's genuine expertise is the legal/tax/compliance layer, not real-estate/infrastructure comparison — keep the cities section factual and short, not a real-estate pitch.
- **GIFT City / IFSC** — address only as a distinct structuring note (different regulator — IFSCA instead of fragmented RBI/SEBI oversight — and different tax treatment), not folded in as if it were the default path. **Flag for human confirmation**: has AU Corp actually advised on a GIFT City/IFSC GCC structure? If not confirmed, keep this section informational/citation-based (what the rules say) rather than implying hands-on AU Corp experience there.

This page's job is to go one level deeper (actual deadlines, a worked example) on a claim AU Corp is already standing behind, which is a stronger, lower-risk differentiator than introducing brand-new claims.
2. **Cross-border reporting fluency, evidenced by named practitioners.** Utsav Dogra (FCA) has directly managed outsourcing/Virtual CFO engagements for Indian subsidiaries of German and Chinese corporate groups — real experience reconciling a foreign parent's reporting expectations with Indian statutory requirements, which is precisely the "Operate" phase of a GCC. Rimpi Jain (Company Secretary) leads FEMA/RBI filing work — the named specialist behind the FC-GPR/FC-TRS/FLA claim. Gulshan Grover (CA, US CPA) is the credible named voice for US-parent GCC readers specifically. **Use named, credentialed attribution on this page** (byline or reviewed-by line) — this directly closes Stage 4's "no page in the analyzed set has an on-article named author" gap, and AU Corp has real people to attribute it to, not a generic "our team."
3. **Neutral, non-BOT entity-choice advice.** Because AU Corp doesn't sell a build-operate-transfer or managed-GCC product, it has no structural incentive to steer readers toward BOT/JV over a straightforward WOS — unlike ANSR-style competitors. 4. **A compliance calendar and worked example grounded in actual filings AU Corp handles.** This is the single most differentiated asset the page can produce (per Stage 4), and it's credible specifically because FEMA/RBI compliance and transfer pricing/safe-harbour work are real, named AU Corp service lines — not adjacent-to-the-service content the way an EOR platform's (Wisemonk's) entity content is.

## Explicit Guidance for the Writer
- Write as a practitioner explaining mechanics to a CFO/founder who already knows what a GCC is in outline but needs to know exactly what filing happens when, and exactly how to decide between entity options — not as a firm introducing itself or its capabilities.
- Every named form, deadline, or rate (FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA return date, safe-harbour percentage, corporate tax rate) must be checked against a primary source (RBI FED Master Direction, Income Tax Department notification, MCA) before publishing — several of these (notably the 15.5% safe-harbour figure and any 2026 Budget claim) are currently sourced only from Stage 1/2 search snippets, not verified primary text. **Flag for human confirmation before publish.**
- Do not open or close the piece with a service pitch ("AU Corporate is your trusted partner for..."). Let the depth of the compliance calendar, the decision framework, and the named-author credibility do the positioning work. A single, clearly-scoped consultation CTA at the end is appropriate; a value-proposition paragraph is not.
- Do not claim AU Corp experience the firm doesn't have. Specifically: do not imply AU Corp has managed a GIFT City/IFSC GCC structuring engagement, has "set up X number of GCCs," or has any GCC-specific case-study/client-count claim — no such data exists in the inputs provided, and Stage 3's brief explicitly warns against inventing AU Corp-attributed statistics. Any market-size number (GCC count, revenue, employment) must be attributed to NASSCOM/Zinnov by name, in-text, never presented as AU Corp's own figure.
- Avoid implying AU Corp builds, staffs, or operates GCCs (BOT/managed-GCC territory) — every mention of AU Corp's own role should stay inside legal entity setup, FEMA/RBI/FDI compliance, tax/transfer pricing, and post-incorporation accounting/payroll/audit support.
- When distinguishing GCC from BPO/outsourcing, be precise about a real internal-consistency point: AU Corp's own outsourcing/accounting-BPO service is something a GCC (or any company) can use for its finance/HR back office — it is not itself a "GCC," and the page should not blur that distinction while explaining the GCC-vs-BPO concept to readers.
- Match competitors' decomposition discipline (named cost categories, phase-level timeline, sub-intent-scoped sections) but do not pad the page with generic "why India" content that belongs on `doing-business-in-india/why-india` — link to it instead of re-writing it.
- Use the named-author/reviewer signal deliberately: attribute the FEMA/RBI section to Rimpi Jain's practice area, the transfer-pricing/tax section to the international tax practice, and the post-incorporation Operate section to Utsav Dogra's outsourcing/Virtual CFO experience — but only using the credentials already established on the live site; do not invent additional credentials, case counts, or engagement numbers for these individuals.
