## Minimum Coverage List
(what the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes; skipping any of these makes the page look thinner than the field, regardless of how good the differentiation content is)

1. **The no-threshold, first-transaction OIDAR/NTOR registration rule for foreign suppliers**, stated unambiguously and explicitly distinguished from the domestic turnover threshold (INR 20 lakh / 10 lakh). This is not optional — 4 of 8 competitors get this right and at least one prominent competitor (Kanakkupillai) appears to get it wrong, so precision here is both table stakes and an easy correctness win.
2. **NTOR vs. reverse-charge (B2B) distinction** — who is liable, the provider or the Indian recipient, and why.
3. **GSTR-5A filing cadence** — monthly, due the 20th of the following month, nil-return obligation even with no transactions.
4. **Registration mechanics at a basic level** — Form GST REG-09, requirement for an Indian authorized representative, DSC/EVC submission.
5. **GST rate and SAC code basics** — 18%, SAC 9983, what the invoice needs to show.
6. **Equalisation levy status stated correctly and only in the past tense** — 2% levy withdrawn August 2024, 6% digital-advertising levy withdrawn April 1, 2025 (Finance Bill 2025). Getting this wrong (describing it as current) is the single clearest "this page is stale" tell in the niche; getting it right is necessary but, per the tally, not sufficient — see Differentiation List.
7. **A credible entity-structure menu** (WOS, branch office, liaison office, LLP, private limited) if the page (or page-pair) covers India entry at all — every entry-focused competitor has some version of this list.
8. **FEMA/RBI compliance named as part of the post-entry lifecycle**, even briefly — every entry-focused competitor at least name-drops FEMA/FDI compliance as a service line.

## Differentiation List
(ranked by genuine opportunity — traced to specific observed gaps in Stage 3, not generic "add more depth" filler)

1. **An explicit decision framework connecting "compliance-only" and "entity-triggering" scenarios** — e.g., "you're billing Indian customers directly, no entity: here's your OIDAR-only obligation" vs. "you're hiring locally / opening a bank account / raising India-specific capital: here's when that same fact pattern should trigger entity setup, and which structure." Stage 3 confirms this exact gap explicitly: not one of the 8 pages reviewed does this, and even Treelife — the closest structural analog, which runs two separate cross-linked pages — solves the problem by splitting the topics apart rather than integrating them. This is the single highest-value, most defensible differentiation opportunity in the set.
2. **The AI-specific extension of OIDAR** — the October 2023 amendment explicitly broadened OIDAR's definition to cover AI-delivered services (dropping the "minimal human intervention" qualifier), and zero of the 8 competitors reviewed engage with "AI" as a framing at all; every one of them frames the topic purely as "SaaS." This is real, citable white space that also happens to match AU Corporate's own stated client segment — a genuine and low-risk differentiation angle because it is grounded in an actual regulatory text change, not invented positioning.
3. **Correcting the no-threshold-vs-domestic-threshold conflation** that Kanakkupillai's GST-SaaS page appears to create. Stating this distinction with more precision than the weakest widely-ranking competitor is a concrete, checkable accuracy win, not a vague "be more thorough" gesture.
4. **A full compliance-lifecycle narrative in one place**: scope-in trigger → registration mechanics (Form REG-09, representative appointment) → GSTR-5A cadence → Section 122/50 CGST Act penalty and interest exposure for non-compliance. No single page in the reviewed set carries a reader through all of these steps end-to-end; each does one or two pieces well and stops.
5. **Bridging the withdrawn equalisation levy to what's still live** — Significant Economic Presence (SEP) exposure and ongoing OIDAR/GST obligations. Beacon Filing gets the withdrawal fact right but stops there, leaving readers who search "equalisation levy India" without an answer to their real underlying question ("so is there still some digital tax I owe?"). Zero of the 8 pages make this bridge.
6. **A statutory-citation depth beyond what any single page assembles**: IGST Act §13(12) (reverse-charge carve-out — currently only on Treelife-OIDAR), CGST Act §24 (mandatory registration — currently only on IndiaFilings), and CGST Act §§122/50 (penalty/interest — currently on none of the 8). No competitor combines all three; doing so in one page is a genuine authority differentiator, not duplicated ground.
7. **A foreign-provider-scoped compliance checklist**, as distinct from Kanakkupillai's generic domestic-startup checklist — scoped explicitly to "compliance-only" vs. "entry-plus-compliance" journeys.
8. **A clear default recommendation with reasoning**, not just a menu, on entity structure (KNM does the "clear default" move but only 1 of 8 pages does it, and even KNM doesn't tie the recommendation back to GST/OIDAR status as an input).
9. **Visible authorship/credential/last-updated signal** — zero of the 8 competitors show this. Low effort, real trust-signal differentiation in a topic area where "did they update this after the Oct 2023 amendment / the 2024-25 levy withdrawal" is exactly the kind of thing a cautious founder or their counsel would want reassurance on.

## Opportunities for Authoritative Sourcing
(where citing MCA/RBI/Income Tax/GST primary sources would beat what's currently ranking)

- **CGST Act Section 24** (mandatory registration for non-resident taxable persons) — currently cited by only 1 of 8 competitors (IndiaFilings); citing it directly alongside the OIDAR-specific framing (which IndiaFilings does not do — its OIDAR content is a subsection of a generic foreign-company page) is real differentiated authority.
- **CGST Act Sections 122 and 50** (registration-failure penalty; 18% p.a. interest on unpaid IGST) — cited by none of the 8 analyzed pages with primary-source precision; a direct citation here closes a gap no competitor fills.
- **IGST Act Section 13(12)** (place-of-supply rule underlying the B2B reverse-charge carve-out) — currently only on Treelife-OIDAR; citing this precisely, tied to the NTOR/RCM distinction, matches the field's single sharpest citation and extends it.
- **The specific CBIC notification underlying the October 2023 OIDAR definition amendment** — every competitor references the amendment's effect (broadened scope, dropped "minimal human intervention" language) but none, per Stage 3's available evidence, appears to cite the actual notification number. Naming and linking the primary notification (verify exact number/date against gst.gov.in/cbic.gov.in before publishing) would be a concrete authority upgrade over the entire reviewed field.
- **CBDT's Significant Economic Presence threshold notification** (Finance Act 2018; INR 2 crore / 300,000-user figures) — not sourced to a primary citation by any of the 8 pages; also the basis for a possible worked numeric example (Potential Unique Angle).
- **Finance Act 2016 Chapter VIII and Finance Bill 2025** for the equalisation levy's two-phase withdrawal — Beacon Filing states the outcome correctly but a page that cites the specific provisions/dates precisely (rather than describing the outcome only) is a modest but real upgrade.
- **gst.gov.in's own Non-Resident Taxable Person registration user guide** — ranks directly for procedural queries per Stage 1; citing/linking it directly (rather than only paraphrasing registration mechanics secondhand, as most competitors do) both adds authority and is defensible practice (pointing to the primary source rather than competing to re-describe it worse).
- **RBI/FIRMS portal primary references for FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA, APR filings** — no competitor in the reviewed set details these beyond naming them as a service line (KNM); direct RBI Master Direction/FIRMS citations would out-authority the entire entry-focused competitor set on this specific point.

## One-Page vs. Two-Page: Gap-Analysis Input for the Orchestrator

Weighing in explicitly, from the coverage-diff evidence (not repeating Stage 2's keyword-cluster argument or Stage 3's SERP-occupancy argument, which both already lean toward a two-page split):

**The minimum-coverage bar argues for two pages.** Cluster A (compliance) and Cluster B (entry) each carry a substantial, non-overlapping table-stakes list (8 items combined above, split roughly 5 compliance-side / 3 entry-side plus shared items). The one competitor in this set that tried to cover both in a single page's worth of attention — KNM — is the clearest cautionary evidence: Stage 3 explicitly flags that KNM treats GST/OIDAR as "a light pairing... rather than engaged with at the mechanism level," i.e., trying to serve both clusters from one page caused the compliance side specifically to fall below the bar the dedicated OIDAR pages clear. If AU Corporate's page tried to hit every Minimum Coverage item above for both clusters in one page, it risks either the same dilution KNM shows, or a page so long it undermines on-page relevance signals for either keyword cluster (per Stage 2's same concern).

**But the differentiation opportunity is fundamentally a between-cluster synthesis problem, and two separately-published pages — even cross-linked — don't automatically solve it.** Differentiation List item #1 (the decision framework) is the single highest-value gap identified in this analysis, and by definition it requires connecting Cluster A and Cluster B content, not just placing them near each other. Treelife's own two-page, cross-linked approach — the closest analog in the reviewed set — does not achieve this integration either, per Stage 3's verdict.

**Recommendation for Stage 5:** two pages, but do not let the split become an excuse to skip the integration work. Whichever structure is chosen (two pages, or one integrated page), the decision framework/flowchart described in Differentiation #1 should exist as one shared, consistently-built asset — either duplicated in full on both pages (not just a "see also" link, the way Treelife does it) or, if truly one page is chosen instead, built as the page's organizing spine rather than a bolt-on section, exactly as Stage 3's verdict concludes. The two-page structure is the safer choice for clearing each cluster's Minimum Coverage bar without dilution; the differentiation value only materializes if the connective framework is treated as first-class content on both sides of the split, not an afterthought — which is precisely the failure mode KNM and Treelife both illustrate in different ways.
