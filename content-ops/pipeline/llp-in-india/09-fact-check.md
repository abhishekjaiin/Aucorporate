Form URC-1 is prescribed under **Rule 3(2) of the Companies (Authorised to Register) Rules, 2014**, filed together with SPICe+ (INC-32), which incorporates the resulting company. The draft's claim that URC-1 is filed "alongside the SPICe+ incorporation filing" is accurate. |

| 5 | FC-GPR vs. Form FDI-LLP(I)/LLP(I) as genuinely distinct filings | **Confirmed.** Under the Single Master Form (SMF)/FIRMS portal framework (FEM (Mode of Payment and Reporting of Non-Debt Instruments) Regulations, 2019), there are nine distinct reporting forms — FC-GPR, FC-TRS, LLP-I, LLP-II, CN, DRR, ESOP, DI, InVi — with FC-GPR covering equity-instrument allotment by a company and LLP-I/LLP-II covering LLP capital contribution and profit-share transfers as their own separate category ("capital participation in LLP" is defined as its own class of non-debt instrument, distinct from "capital instruments" issued by companies). This corroborates the draft's core distinction. One AI-search-summarized result (from a generic FC-GPR explainer) asserted LLPs are also subject to FC-GPR — this was checked against every detailed/specific source found and contradicted by all of them; it is treated as a summarization artifact, not a real finding, and rejected. |

The draft's explanation of the gate, and its distinction from the broader company-side test, is accurate. |

Corroborated by Taxmann, Lawrbit, and academic/practice materials, but not fetched from primary Act text. The draft's deliberately vague framing ("permitted... carries its own eligibility conditions," no schedule number stated) is the safe, correct call and doesn't need a specific citation added unless AU Corporate wants to name Sections 56/57 — see (b) for suggested hedge if so. |
| 8 | AMT rate ~18.5%, Section 115JC | **Confirmed as the consistently cited current-law figure**, with a threshold detail the draft doesn't yet include: AMT under **Section 115JC** applies to an LLP only where **adjusted total income exceeds ₹20 lakh**, at a rate of **18.5%** (effective ~19.24% with cess). Convergence across many independent, non-competitor tax-content sources (Axis Max Life, Quicko, Taxguru, Startupflora, incometaxmanagement.com, DisyTax, CallMyCA). Still not primary-source-fetched — keep the existing hedge, but the ₹20 lakh trigger threshold is a genuinely useful, well-corroborated addition if Stage 9/writer wants to strengthen this section. |

Mechanically: Form 11 is due within 60 days of financial-year close (30 May); Form 8 is due within 30 days of the expiry of six months from FY close (30 October). This is about as strong as non-primary-source convergence gets. |
| 10 | Audit threshold ₹40 lakh turnover / ₹25 lakh contribution | **Confirmed, with a specific citable rule now available**: **Section 34(4), LLP Act, 2008** and **Rule 24(8), LLP Rules, 2009**, "whichever is crossed first" (Patron Accounting, ComplianceCalendar). ## (b) Items Requiring a Hedge or Softened/Strengthened Wording (with suggested wording)

1. **Section 10(2A) — Income-tax Act 2025 transition.** Current-law citation (Section 10(2A) of the 1961 Act, exempting a partner's share of firm/LLP profit from further tax) is correct *as currently in force*. But research indicates the transition is not a simple renumbering: under the incoming **Income-tax Act, 2025** (in force from 1 April 2026), exemptions presently under Section 10 are restructured into **Schedule II ("Income Not To Be Included In Total Income")**, referenced by a table entry/serial number rather than a section-and-subsection citation. This is a structural change, not just a new number — the draft's current footnote ("the corresponding section number under the new Act needs confirming") understates this. Suggested replacement wording:
   > *"Note: for tax years beginning on or after 1 April 2026, this exemption is expected to move out of Section 10 of the 1961 Act entirely and into Schedule II of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (referenced by table entry, not a section number) — confirm the exact Schedule II entry with a tax professional before citing this for any assessment year 2026–27 return or later."*
   This finding is search-synthesized, not primary-fetched — flag for professional confirmation before publishing the revised footnote.

2. **AMT section** — can be strengthened with the ₹20 lakh adjusted-total-income trigger (well corroborated, see (a)#8) while keeping the existing "needs primary-source confirmation" hedge, and should get the same Income-tax Act 2025 caveat as Section 10(2A), since Chapter XII-BA (Sections 115JC–115JF) will almost certainly be renumbered/relocated under the new Act and this pipeline could not confirm the new location.

3. **Audit threshold** — suggest strengthening from "market-consensus figure" to:
   > *"...commonly cited as ₹40 lakh in annual turnover or ₹25 lakh in partner contribution, whichever is crossed first, under Section 34(4) of the LLP Act, 2008 and Rule 24(8) of the LLP Rules, 2009. We're citing this rule reference as it's consistently reported across professional sources, but it was not independently verified against the primary Rules text in this pipeline — confirm with your advisor before relying on it for a compliance decision."*
   Keep the "confirm with advisor" hedge; only the specificity of the citation is being upgraded, not the confidence level.

4. **FDI-LLP(I) reporting window** — given the broader-than-expected corroboration found (including an ICSI document), suggest adding back a hedged figure rather than leaving it fully silent:
   > *"...the LLP has to report that contribution to RBI, commonly cited as within 30 days of receipt of the capital contribution amount, under the FEM (Mode of Payment and Reporting of Non-Debt Instruments) Regulations, 2019 — we'd recommend confirming this window with your bank's authorised dealer branch before treating it as a fixed deadline."*
   This is a judgment call for the writer/AU Corporate: the underlying figure is now well-corroborated by independent secondary sources including a professional body, which is a materially stronger evidentiary basis than what the writer had when deciding to omit it — but it is still not primary-source-fetched in this environment.

5. **INC-23/INC-24 parenthetical** — now that these are confirmed *not* relevant (see (a)#4), suggest shortening the italicized flag from "including whether INC-23/INC-24-style approvals apply in a given case" to something like:
   > *"(Forms INC-23 and INC-24 — used respectively for shifting a registered office between states and for a company name change — are unrelated processes and don't apply to this Section 366 conversion route; the specific section numbers and forms above should still be verified against primary MCA sources before publication.)"*

6. **Reverse conversion** — if AU Corporate wants more specificity than the current vague sentence, this can safely be tightened to:
   > *"...permitted under the LLP Act's own conversion provisions — a private company converts under Section 56 and the Third Schedule, an unlisted public company under Section 57 and the Fourth Schedule; a listed public company cannot convert into an LLP."*
   This is well corroborated across secondary sources but not primary-fetched; keep as an option, not a required fix, since the current hedge is already safe.

7. **Company-side tax rate references in the comparison table/section (22% / 25% / 30%)** — these aren't cited to a specific 1961-Act section within *this* draft, but they describe rates (Sections 115BAA/115BAB-type concessional regimes) that are equally subject to the Income-tax Act 2025 transition. Recommend a single shared footnote near the top of the "How Is an LLP Taxed" section covering all rate/section references at once (LLP 30% flat rate, AMT, Section 10(2A), company concessional rates), rather than three scattered individual hedges — cleaner, and avoids the reader wondering why only two of four rate-adjacent claims are flagged.

## (c) Factually Wrong — Required Fix

1. **FDI-in-LLP liberalisation date — "10 November 2015" is likely incorrect; the correct date, per this research, is 24 November 2015.** Writer's Note #10 states Stage 2's Related Entities research treated **10 November 2015** as the liberalisation date. This pass found no corroboration for that date anywhere. Instead, multiple independent sources (Mondaq — twice, in two separate articles — plus NovoJuris and others) consistently identify the relevant policy as **Press Note 12 of 2015, dated 24 November 2015**, issued by the Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion (DIPP, now DPIIT), which introduced the automatic-route FDI-in-LLP framework described in the draft. This is not currently in the live copy (Stage 7/8 correctly chose not to state a date in body copy pending this verification), so **there is no live-copy error to fix right now** — but the underlying research record (Stage 2's date) should be corrected before it's used anywhere, and if a date is added to the page per Stage 4's "cite the actual policy" recommendation, it should be **"Press Note 12 of 2015, dated 24 November 2015"**, not 10 November. This is search-synthesized (not primary-fetched — DPIIT's own site was found in search results but not fetched), so treat as high-confidence-but-not-primary-confirmed before publishing a specific date claim.

No other claim in the live draft copy was found to be factually wrong; all other flagged items are unverified-but-plausible (require hedging) or now confirmed accurate.

## Regulatory Currency Check

- The draft already correctly anticipates the **Income-tax Act, 2025** transition (1 April 2026) for Section 10(2A) and flags AMT similarly — this is good practice and should be extended to the company-rate references per (d)#2.
- The **LLP (Amendment) Act, 2021** 120-day rule is current and correctly stated; no more-recent amendment was found that would supersede it.
- The **FDI-LLP Press Note 12 of 2015** framework remains the current policy basis; no subsequent liberalisation or restriction specific to LLP FDI was found in this pass (the general FDI policy has had various sector-specific updates since, e.g., insurance in 2021, but nothing altering the two-part LLP gate itself).
- **DPIN/DIN**: fully merged since 2011; no separate DPIN application process exists today for anyone who already holds a DIN. The draft's description is directionally correct and not describing an outdated process.
- No evidence found of any process described in the draft (Form 11/Form 8 filing regime, URC-1 conversion route, FC-GPR/FDI-LLP(I) split) having been superseded or replaced by a newer mechanism.
