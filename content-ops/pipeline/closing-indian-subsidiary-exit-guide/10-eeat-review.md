## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The margin-case judgment content is the single strongest EEAT signal on the page.** Every competitor page in Stage 3's research gave parallel eligibility gates ("strike-off requires X; liquidation requires Y") and left the reader to work out which applies. This draft names three specific margin scenarios (a single lingering liability, open-but-unenforced contracts, board risk tolerance beyond bare eligibility) and reasons through each one, including naming the failure mode (an STK-6 objection triggered by an undisclosed liability, forcing an unplanned pivot to voluntary liquidation "later and with less control over the timeline"). That's the kind of detail that only comes from having actually seen a filing get stuck, not from summarizing statute text.
- **The refusal to overstate figures it can't verify, paired with a concrete alternative, reads as professional judgment rather than as a content gap.** "We're deliberately not repeating a specific day-count figure here... ask us for the current C-PACE timeline when you're actually ready to file" is a materially different posture from either inventing a number or silently omitting the topic — it tells the reader why the number is unstable and what to do instead.
- **The India-side/US-side reconciliation framing (Form 5471 deconsolidation, Section 482 close-out, tying the liquidator's certificate to the US parent's Schedule M) is a genuine structural differentiator**, not a bolted-on cross-sell — Stage 3 confirmed no competitor connects these two sides of the same transaction, and the one competitor with adjacent expertise explicitly disclaims it. The page earns the claim to depth here rather than asserting it.
- **The capital-account vs. current-account remittance distinction and the two-regime Rule 21/Section 66 correction** (post-fact-check) show real technical precision — the kind of distinction a generic AI-generated overview would either miss entirely or state incorrectly as one undifferentiated rule, which is exactly what the original draft did before Stage 9 caught it.

## Where It Falls Short

- **No named reviewer/expert byline anywhere on the page**, despite the page explicitly invoking "our team... CA and US CPA-qualified" as a credibility claim. For a page this technical, an unnamed team reference reads slightly weaker than it could — see Attribution Recommendation below.
- **No inline links to primary regulatory sources** (RBI, IBBI, MCA, IRS) — the page names Section 248, RBI MD 13/2015-16, IBC Section 59, IRC Section 957/482 precisely, but a reader (or Google) evaluating trustworthiness has to take those citations on faith rather than being able to click through. This is a low-cost enhancement, best done once the RBI Master Direction text is human-confirmed (so the link target and the described mechanics are known to match).
- **The cost FAQ is fully qualitative** ("Strike-off is generally the lower-cost route... voluntary liquidation costs more") with no anchor number at all, not even a wide indicative range. This is the correct call given no verified figure exists in the research chain, but it is the one FAQ answer that gives a reader materially less to work with than the others — flagged as a content-owner decision (supply a real indicative range if AU has one), not a drafting defect.
- **The standing "needs human professional sign-off" items remain open** — RBI Master Direction 13/2015-16 full-text confirmation, the AD-bank document checklist's exact specificity, and the capital-vs-current-account mechanics as stated. These are disclosed honestly in the content itself (the page never overclaims procedural detail it can't fully back), but they are real gaps in what's currently confirmed, and this review does not independently resolve them — that determination sits with AU's own CA/CS and US CPA per the established pipeline pattern.

## Author/Expert Attribution Recommendation

This page warrants a named reviewer credit, more so than most pages in this cluster. It makes specific claims about a formal insolvency process (voluntary liquidation), a specific US tax filing obligation (Form 5471 final-year reporting), and cross-border remittance mechanics — all YMYL-adjacent subject matter where a reader and Google both weight named expertise more heavily than an unnamed "our team" reference. Recommend AU add, at minimum, a byline block near the top or bottom of the page in the pattern: "Reviewed by [Name], Chartered Accountant" and, given the page's dual-jurisdiction claim, ideally a second name or joint credit covering the US-CPA side ("India-side review by [Name], CA; US tax review by [Name], CPA"). This does not need to block publication — it's an enhancement the content owner can add once specific reviewers are assigned — but it is the single highest-leverage remaining EEAT improvement available on this page, and should be actioned before or shortly after go-live rather than treated as optional polish.
