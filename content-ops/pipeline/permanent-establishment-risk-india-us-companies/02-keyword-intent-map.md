## Foreign/International Framing Check
Evidence found for international-audience phrasing, and how it changes the map:
- **"Foreign companies" (not "foreign investors," "international," "overseas," or "cross-border") has real, repeated SERP-title evidence**: treelife.in ranks with the exact title "Permanent Establishment Risk in India: For Foreign companies," and this stage's follow-up WebSearch surfaced **two additional stratrich.com pages** using the identical pattern — "Permanent Establishment Risk in India for Foreign Companies" and "Permanent Establishment Risk for Foreign Companies in India." nexdigm.com also ranks with "Foreign Companies in India – Entry Options, Taxation & Regulatory Compliance." That's three independent domains, four page instances, using "foreign companies" as the audience qualifier in an actual ranking title — genuine evidence, not assumption.
- **Action taken on this evidence**: "foreign companies" is added to this map as a **natural/synonym variant** (used in body copy, alt phrasing of subheads, and meta description variation) alongside "US company/companies" — not as a replacement primary keyword. The primary keyword stays "…US company" because that phrasing has equal-or-stronger direct-title evidence (kaam.work, beaconfiling.com, anjusmriti.com) *and* matches the actual scoped brief and target URL (`/india-entry-for-us-companies/`). Using "foreign companies" as a secondary synonym, not a manufactured primary, keeps AU Corporate honest to its US-specific service line while still capturing the adjacent SERP real estate the evidence supports.
- **"Cross-border"**: one instance only (lexology.com's "Cross-Border Work: Understanding Permanent Establishment Risk in India") — this is the single most-recurring result across all of Stage 1's nine searches by domain frequency, but the "cross-border" phrase itself appears in only that one title. Weak, single-source evidence — **not** promoted to a targeted keyword; may appear naturally in copy about remote/traveling work, nothing more.
- **"International" and "overseas"**: no evidence found anywhere in Stage 1's research or this stage's two follow-up searches — a top-ranking page title, a PAA-style question, or a related-search variant using either word never surfaced. **Do not use either term as a keyword.**
- **Conclusion**: the audience is a US company (and the brief further specifies this), but the validated query language is plain — "permanent establishment risk India," "PE risk India," "US company"/"foreign companies" as a qualifier, not "cross-border tax risk" or "international PE exposure." Target the plain, evidenced phrasing; carry the "for US companies specifically, with the broader foreign-company principle" nuance in the content's framing and internal linking, not by inventing a keyword no one has been observed to type.
