| "2,100 centers... well over $70 billion in annual revenue... more than 2.4 million professionals... figures that have moved up meaningfully even from the FY2024 baseline" (Section: "The GCC Opportunity in India: 2026 Snapshot") | This is now **stale relative to the actual most-recent NASSCOM-Zinnov report** (July 2026, "GCC Value Orbit"), which puts revenue at **$98.4 billion** (not "well over $70 billion" — technically true but substantially understates the real figure by roughly $28B) and employees at **~2.36 million**, slightly below the draft's "more than 2.4 million." The draft's figures read as an older, more conservative estimate rather than the current report. Given this section explicitly claims to be citing figures more current than "the FY2024 baseline most competitor content still cites," it must be updated to the actual current report's numbers, exact report title ("GCC Value Orbit: From Delivery Engine to Enterprise Nerve Centre," NASSCOM-Zinnov, July 2026), and the attribution should name that specific report rather than a generic "most recent joint research" reference. |
| "GST registration is mandatory once the entity crosses the applicable turnover threshold, or immediately if the GCC is billing its foreign parent for services" | This overstates the mandatory-registration trigger. Under Section 22/24 of the CGST Act, a person exclusively exporting services (the standard GCC fact pattern — billing only its foreign parent) is **not** subject to compulsory registration purely by virtue of that export activity; the ordinary Rs 20 lakh turnover threshold still applies. Registration is commonly obtained *voluntarily* pre-threshold so the entity can file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) and export services without paying IGST/claim zero-rating and ITC refunds — but that is a practical/commercial reason to register, not a legal "immediate mandatory" trigger. As worded, the draft conflates the two and should be corrected to distinguish "legally mandatory at the threshold" from "commonly registered earlier, voluntarily, to access LUT/zero-rating." |

## Regulatory Currency Check

The most significant regulatory-currency issue found is **not** something the draft gets wrong, but something the draft should be aware of going forward: **the Income-tax Act, 2025 has replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 effective 1 April 2026**, and this transition affects essentially every section-number citation a tax/compliance page might want to add — including the corporate tax rate provision (115BAA → apparently Section 200) and the safe harbour rules themselves (now anchored to Rule 89/91 read with Section 167 of the 2025 Act, not the old 1961-Act rule numbering). The current draft avoids this trap by not naming section numbers at all — that caution should be preserved. If a future revision adds specific section citations, they must be checked against which Act/numbering applies to the relevant tax year, not just against pre-2026 practitioner content (a large share of GST/tax advisory content still online was written under the old numbering and has not been updated).

Separately: the safe harbour rules referenced are themselves very recently changed (notified ~March 2026, effective for Tax Year 2026-27) — a meaningful supersession of the prior Rs 300 crore threshold / broader margin-band regime that predates it. The draft correctly treats this as a live, dedicated-page topic rather than restating figures here, which is the right call given how recently it changed.

No other outdated process or superseded rule was identified in the draft. SPICe+, the FDI automatic route framework, IFSCA's unified-regulator role, and the FC-GPR/FC-TRS/FLA three-filing structure all remain current as far as could be independently confirmed.
