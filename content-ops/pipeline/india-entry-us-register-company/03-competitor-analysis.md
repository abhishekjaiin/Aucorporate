## Per-Page Analysis

### commenda.io/incorporation/register-a-company-in-india-from-the-usa
**Does well:** States the resident-director requirement as a sequencing problem, not just a fact — makes the point that the resident director needs to be lined up *before* filing, since SPICe+ requires director details at submission, which is a more practically useful framing than a flat "you need a resident director" statement. States the minimum-structure rule plainly (2 directors, 2 shareholders, can overlap) — a concrete number a self-serve searcher can act on. Names the FDI Automatic vs. Approval Route split and ties it to bank-account opening, connecting a compliance concept to a practical next step (a searcher can't always open a business account without a resident director in place).
**Does poorly:** Reads as one of at least 7 near-identical country-pair page variants (UAE, Canada, Australia, Singapore, Japan, France, USA all confirmed) — the "why India, from the USA specifically" angle is likely thin or generic, since the page structure has to work across nationalities with a swapped country name. No entity-comparison table (Pvt Ltd vs LLP vs Branch/Liaison/Project) surfaced in what's retrievable — entity choice appears to be mentioned but not resolved into a decision framework. Ends by recommending the reader consult a professional service provider rather than answering the harder mechanics itself — a soft punt on complexity rather than resolving it.
**Why it likely ranks:** A dedicated, keyword-matched country-pair URL (exact phrase in the URL and title) combined with a SaaS platform's likely broader domain authority and a real, if generic, content template that hits the core process points (resident director, SPICe+, FDI route) accurately enough to satisfy a basic "what do I need to know" query.
**What would beat it:** Resolve the entity choice into an actual comparison with selection criteria (not just naming the FDI-route split), and go beyond "consult a professional" by answering the resident-director gap question directly — what a solo US founder without an Indian contact actually does (nominee director arrangement, what that costs/requires) instead of deferring it.

### indiafilings.com/learn/how-to-register-your-own-company-in-india-from-usa
**Does well:** Structures the process end-to-end through to the compliance tail, not just incorporation — documents, submission, certificate of incorporation, bank account opening, PAN/TAN/GST, and FDI reporting to RBI are all named as sequential steps, which is a genuinely complete journey (most competitors stop at incorporation and treat FDI reporting as an afterthought). Sits inside a very large content platform (own dedicated "private limited company registration" guide, its own "USA company registration for Indian founders" reverse-direction page, etc.) — this gives it real topical breadth around the entity type itself that a standalone guide page can borrow authority from via internal linking.
**Does poorly:** Timeline framing looks internally inconsistent across the platform's own content ecosystem — one part of the site frames name approval and incorporation-application prep in 1-2 business days each, while a blended figure elsewhere on related pages suggests a 6-8 week (up to 10-week) total "operational" timeline; it's not fully clear from search-synthesis alone whether both figures live on the same page or whether this is the search tool blending multiple IndiaFilings-adjacent pages — but even the pattern of not scoping "registered" vs. "operational" clearly in one place is a real usability gap for a reader trying to plan.
**Why it likely ranks:** Domain-authority and platform-breadth driven more than page-specific driven — a ClearTax-owned registration platform with a large surrounding content library and (almost certainly, though unverified here) strong backlink profile from its core registration-service business, not primarily because this one page is uniquely deep.
**What would beat it:** A single, clearly scoped timeline (incorporation-only vs. incorporation + FDI reporting + bank account, stated together, not split across pages) with a stated basis, plus the same end-to-end completeness IndiaFilings already has — completeness alone won't be a differentiator against this page, since it already covers the full sequence; the win here is clarity and internal consistency, not more steps.

### enterslice.com/learning/register-company-india-from-usa
**Does well:** Gives the clearest three-way entity framing observed in this set for *why* a US founder would default to Private Limited over the alternatives — Pvt Ltd needs no Central Government/RBI pre-approval, LLP needs Central Government approval, proprietorship/partnership needs RBI approval and is generally discouraged for foreign nationals. This is a real "why," not just a list of entity names, and directly serves the entity-choice sub-intent Stage 1/2 flagged as the strongest content-gap signal.
**Does poorly:** Describes name reservation via a standalone "Form INC-1" / RUN step — Stage 1 already flagged RUN/INC-1 as a likely-legacy description of a process now largely folded into SPICe+ Part A; if this is presented as a live current step rather than superseded, it's a real currency/accuracy problem, not a stylistic one, and could mislead a searcher about what form they'll actually be filing in 2026. Doesn't appear (from what's retrievable) to extend the entity comparison to Branch/Liaison/Project Office in the same clear "why" framing it gives Pvt Ltd vs. LLP vs. proprietorship — so a US parent company evaluating a non-subsidiary structure doesn't get the same quality of guidance.
**Why it likely ranks:** A CA/CS/legal services firm with an established India-registration content library and a clear, correctly-reasoned (mostly) explanation of the single highest-value sub-question in this cluster — why Pvt Ltd is the default answer — which likely satisfies a large share of searchers who just want to know which structure to pick.
**What would beat it:** Get the current MCA process right (confirm SPICe+ Part A supersedes standalone RUN/INC-1 and say so explicitly, which also doubles as a freshness/trust signal), and extend the same quality of "why" reasoning Enterslice gives for Pvt Ltd/LLP/proprietorship to the Branch/Liaison/Project Office alternatives too, so the comparison is genuinely complete rather than strongest on one branch of the decision tree.

### masllp.com/how-to-start-a-company-in-india-from-the-usa-detailed-guide-for-us-businesses
**Does well:** Covers both incorporation paths in the same piece — SPICe+ for the Private Limited/Wholly Owned Subsidiary route, and Form FC-1 (via an Authorized Dealer bank, under FEMA) for Branch/Project/Liaison Office — which is a more complete entity-type spread than most of the set, which tend to default hard to Pvt Ltd and mention the others only in passing. **Does poorly:** The explicit "2026" title-stamping is a freshness *signal*, not freshness *substance* — nothing in what's retrievable indicates the underlying process detail was actually re-verified against current MCA/RBI procedure rather than just re-dated. The FC-1/branch-office coverage, while present, reads as bolted onto a subsidiary-focused piece rather than given equal structural weight — a US parent seriously evaluating a branch office over a subsidiary likely still needs to look elsewhere for a comparably deep treatment.
**Why it likely ranks:** Genuine topical completeness (both entity paths, scoped timeline framing) combined with an aggressive freshness-signaling title that Google appears to reward in this query cluster per Stage 1's freshness-signal pattern across multiple competitors.
**What would beat it:** Give the Branch/Liaison/Project Office path the same structural weight as the subsidiary path (not a bolt-on section), and back the "2026" framing with something concrete a reader can verify — a specific current fee schedule reference or an explicit "last verified against MCA guidance" type statement — rather than relying on the year in the title alone.

## Cross-Page Patterns

**What most strong pages share:**
- A near-identical underlying process skeleton across every page regardless of publisher type: entity-choice mention → SPICe+/DSC/DIN → PAN/TAN/GST → post-incorporation FDI reporting (FC-GPR). This skeleton is table stakes, not a differentiator — every page in the set has some version of it.
- Education-led framing even on pages that sit on service-selling firm domains: process/requirements content leads, consultation CTA follows at the end rather than being pushed throughout, consistent with Stage 1's mixed informational-commercial intent read.
- Where a page has a genuine edge, it's a specific, narrow thing (Commenda's sequencing point on resident-director timing, Enterslice's reasoned Pvt-Ltd-vs-LLP-vs-proprietorship "why," MASLLP's dual subsidiary/branch coverage and scoped timeline, VJM's Startup India tax-incentive angle) — not overall superior depth or length.

**What's uniformly weak across all of them:**
- No page in this set was confirmed (even allowing for the fetch limitation) to contain a genuine side-by-side decision table covering all five relevant structures (Pvt Ltd / LLP / Wholly Owned Subsidiary / Branch / Liaison / Project Office) with selection criteria tied to actual business activity. Entity types get named across different pages and sections, but not synthesized into one decision-support artifact.
- The resident-director requirement is stated as a rule everywhere but not treated as the practical gating question it is for a solo US founder — nobody observed addresses "what do I do if I don't have an Indian resident to appoint" with a real answer.
- Cost and timeline figures are soft, unscoped, and inconsistent across the whole set (confirmed again here: MASLLP's own figures split "5-10 working day" ROC approval from a much larger "6-8 week operational" figure without always making that distinction crystal clear elsewhere in the same content ecosystem) — nobody sources a number against a dated, defensible basis.
- No page in this set shows visible inline citation of primary sources (mca.gov.in forms, RBI FIRMS portal, specific Companies Act sections as clickable references) in what's retrievable through search synthesis — this can't be fully confirmed without direct fetch, but its consistent absence across every search result summary is itself a signal worth treating as a real gap, not just a measurement artifact.
- Freshness is signaled ("2026" in titles) more than substantiated — no page shows evidence of an actual "last verified against current MCA/RBI guidance" statement, and at least one competitor (Enterslice) appears to describe a specific step (standalone RUN/Form INC-1 name reservation) in a way that may already be superseded by SPICe+ Part A.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely better AU Corporate page on this topic needs to do five specific things none of the pages studied clearly do all at once:
1. **Resolve the entity choice into an actual decision table** — Pvt Ltd / LLP / Wholly Owned Subsidiary / Branch / Liaison / Project Office, with selection criteria tied to what the US parent is actually trying to do (revenue-generating operations vs. liaison-only vs. project-based), not a prose list of names.
2. **Answer the resident-director gating question directly** — what a solo US founder without an Indian contact actually does, since every page in the set states the rule but none resolve the practical follow-on problem it creates.
3. **Scope cost and timeline explicitly and consistently within the page** — incorporation-only vs. incorporation + FDI reporting + bank account, stated together in one place with a defensible basis, avoiding the split-figure inconsistency observed even within single competitor content ecosystems.
4. **Get the current process right and say so** — confirm SPICe+ Part A's relationship to the older standalone RUN/INC-1 step explicitly, turning freshness into a substantiated claim rather than a title-year stamp.
5. **Close the two proven technical gaps visible on AU's own sibling page** (meta description, canonical tag) and use theaucorp.com's stronger domain footprint with real internal links to the existing hub and subsidiary-deep-dive pages — the registercompanyinindia.com case is direct proof that reasonably complete content on this topic still won't rank without both the technical basics and the authority/internal-linking support this new page can supply that a smaller secondary domain can't.

None of this requires being longer than the competitor set — MASLLP's and Enterslice's narrowest, most specific claims (a scoped timeline distinction, a reasoned three-way entity "why") are exactly the kind of specific, defensible statements that beat generic completeness, and AU Corporate's own apostille-specificity instinct (already proven on the sibling page) is evidence this kind of precision is achievable.
