| "A manufacturing or contract-manufacturing arrangement... is more typically benchmarked using a cost-plus or resale-price method" (US Parent's Own Tax Exposure section) | This appears to overstate cost-plus/resale-price relative to actual Indian TP practice. Multiple sources indicate the **Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM)**, applied using operating profit/total cost for a contract manufacturer, is the **most widely applied method in Indian transfer-pricing practice**, specifically because traditional transactional methods (CUP, RPM, Cost Plus) require granular comparable data that is rarely available. Cost-plus and resale-price are the theoretically "cleaner" textbook methods for manufacturing/distribution fact patterns, but asserting they are "more typically" used is not well-supported and could mislead a reader who then expects their transfer pricing study to default to cost-plus. ## Regulatory Currency Check

- **PLI → ECMS/successor-scheme transition**: Confirmed as a real, actively moving target. ECMS was notified in April 2025 and its outlay was already revised upward (₹22,919 crore → ₹40,000 crore) within roughly ten months, in the Union Budget 2026-27. The draft's explicit "don't structure your investment case around original PLI terms as settled" framing is well-supported and should not be softened.
- **Section 115BAB / 15% concessional manufacturing rate**: This is the one place this check found a genuine, unresolved regulatory-currency gap the draft does not surface at all. The commence-manufacturing sunset condition (originally 31 March 2023, extended once to 31 March 2024) appears — on the bulk of evidence found — not to have been further extended for new entrants, which would mean the incentive is not currently open to a company only now planning its India entry, unless a subsequent extension exists that this session's tools could not confirm. Combined with the underlying provision being renumbered under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), this section of the draft is the one area of the page most likely to be stale or materially incomplete as currently written. See the "Needs Human Professional Verification" table above — this should be treated as the top-priority open item from this fact-check, ranking above the Form 5472 point (which checked out cleanly) in terms of remaining publish risk.
- **Press Note 3**: current and correctly reflects the March 2026 relaxation; no outdated content found.
- **Factories Act, 1948**: still the governing central statute for factory registration; note that several states have their own Factories Rules with local variations on procedural detail (though not, to this check's knowledge, on the core Section 2(m) worker-count threshold itself). The draft does not claim national uniformity beyond the threshold and notice-period points, which is appropriate, but a one-line note that "state rules may add procedural detail beyond the central Act" would close a small residual gap.
- **GST RCM on import of services**: settled law, no currency concerns identified.
- **Form 5472 / Form 5471**: settled, well-established IRS framework; no currency concerns identified.
