## Recurring Entities & Regulations
Observed by name across multiple ranking pages/snippets in this research:
- **India**: OIDAR (Online Information and Database Access or Retrieval), IGST Act Section 13(12) (place of supply for OIDAR = recipient's location), IGST Act Section 2(6) (conditions for export of services / zero-rating), Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM), GSTR-5A (monthly return for foreign OIDAR suppliers), Form GST REG-10 (simplified registration for foreign OIDAR suppliers), NTOR (Non-Taxable Online Recipient), CGST Act Section 9(5) (e-commerce operator as deemed supplier for notified services — passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping, restaurant services), GSTR-3B Table 3.1.1(i)/(ii), Input Tax Credit (ITC), 18% GST/IGST rate cited repeatedly for OIDAR/digital services.
- **EU**: One Stop Shop (OSS) / Import One Stop Shop (IOSS), "electronic interface" deemed-supplier rule (effective since July 2021 for certain B2C goods transactions), VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform (scope of deemed-supplier extension referenced), reverse charge for B2B cross-border services, €10,000 EU cross-border B2C distance-selling threshold (non-EU sellers get no threshold).
- **US**: State-by-state sales/use tax treatment of AI services (Indiana and Illinois cited as having ruled generative-AI chatbot access via web/API not subject to state sales tax), Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Agreement (SSUTA) referenced re: redemption-code/token characterization, ASC 606 (revenue recognition, adjacent to tax characterization for tokens).
- **Cross-cutting commercial concept**: Merchant of Record (MoR) — recurs heavily as the practical mechanism AI companies are being sold to offload VAT/GST/sales-tax collection; distinct from the regulatory "deemed supplier" term but frequently conflated in market messaging.
- **AI-specific technical vocabulary recurring across sources**: tokens, credits, compute, API calls, usage-based billing, prepaid top-ups (VAT taxed at top-up/purchase time per at least one source, not at consumption time), agentic commerce / AI agents as transaction intermediaries.

## Search Intent (page-level read)
- **Informational-dominant overall.** The overwhelming majority of ranking/appearing pages are explainer content (law-firm blogs, CA-firm blogs, tax-media) answering "does VAT/GST apply to AI services" and "how is OIDAR/RCM applied" — evidence: title patterns like "How GST Applies," "Tax Implications Of," "What's Taxable and What's Not," consistently framed as educational rather than "hire us" pages.
- **Commercial intent concentrated in one cluster**: the Merchant-of-Record / tax-automation-software vendors (Dodo Payments, Anrok, Sphere, Numeral, Quaderno, FastSpring, Comecero, Kelviq, 1stopVAT, Payments.ai) are selling a product/service directly off this exact pain point — these are genuinely transactional/commercial pages, and they are indirect competitors to AU Corp (software/platform solution vs. advisory firm), not direct ones.
- **Direct-competitor advisory content (India CA/law firms) is present but mostly framed from the "Indian buyer of foreign AI tools" side** (RCM on ChatGPT/Claude subscriptions), not the "AI company as seller needing global/India compliance" side that matches AU Corp's likely client (an AI company, not a consumer of AI tools). This is a notable intent mismatch/gap in the current SERP.
- **No page found that treats "AI companies" as categorically separate from "SaaS companies" on the basis of usage-based billing classification + marketplace deemed-supplier exposure, combined, as a single dedicated topic.** The closest is Anrok's "Sales tax for AI companies: what's taxable and what's not" (US-only) and TaxProf Blog's token-transaction-characterization piece (US-only, academic/legal, not client-facing India/global advisory). This appears to be genuine open ground for a differentiated page, especially with a global + India-inbound/outbound advisory lens rather than a US-state-tax or MoR-product lens.
