# AU Corporate Positioning: Branch Office vs Subsidiary in India

## Relevant Service Lines for This Page
Only the service lines that materially help a reader deciding between a branch office and a subsidiary — not a checklist of everything AU Corporate sells.

- **India Entry Strategy / Company Incorporation / Business Setup** — this is literally the page's subject. The decision framework, entity comparison, and conversion-mechanics content are a direct extension of AU Corporate's actual incorporation practice already evidenced in `app/india-business-setup/company-formation/page.tsx` (SPICe+ process, entity-type list, EntitySelectorTool). This service line earns its place because the reader's core question — "which structure do I choose" — *is* the entry-strategy question.
- **FEMA & RBI Compliance** — non-negotiable for this topic. Branch office approval runs through an AD Category-I bank under FEMA 22(R)/2016; the draft Establishment Regulations/EROI reform status; Annual Activity Certificate/DGP reporting/Form FC-3 for branches; FC-GPR/FLA for subsidiaries. AU Corporate's own company-formation page already demonstrates real command of this mechanics (FC-GPR compounding process, FOCC/Rule 23 indirect-FDI nuance), so this isn't a borrowed capability — it's demonstrably practiced.
- **FDI Advisory** — relevant at the level of "does your subsidiary need Automatic Route or Government Route clearance, and does that change the practical timeline" — genuinely affects the branch-vs-subsidiary decision (branch approval is a separate RBI gate regardless of sector; subsidiary FDI eligibility depends on sector). Do not expand into the full sector-by-sector FDI table here — that's `fdi-channels`'s job; link to it.
- **International Tax / Corporate Tax** — the tax-rate comparison (branch ~35% base/~36-38% effective as a foreign-company PE vs. subsidiary ~22%/~25.17% effective, or 115BAB concessional rates) is table-stakes content this page must get right, and it's squarely AU Corporate's tax practice's territory.
- **Transfer Pricing** — a light but genuine angle, not a bolt-on: branch profits require attributing income to a permanent establishment, which is a recurring, real point of dispute with Indian tax authorities (AU Corporate's own UK comparison page already states this — "profit attribution to the PE is a common point of dispute... typically needs its own supporting study"). This is a substantive practitioner detail almost no competitor page surfaces, and it's grounded in AU Corporate's existing published content, not invented for this exercise.
- **Accounting & Outsourcing / ongoing compliance support** — relevant specifically for the post-setup compliance-burden section (branch AAC/FC-3 vs. subsidiary RoC annual filings/AOC-4/MGT-7). AU Corporate's compliance roadmap already exists in the company-formation page and is genuine service depth, not a claim manufactured for this page.
- **Audit & Assurance** — relevant only as a single-line fact (subsidiaries carry a statutory audit obligation branches largely avoid) — not as a section, since a full audit pitch doesn't serve a reader still deciding on entity structure.

## Service Lines Deliberately Excluded
- **Virtual CFO** — no organic connection; a reader choosing between branch and subsidiary hasn't reached the "who runs my finance function" question yet. Forcing it in would read as upsell, not answer.
- **Payroll** — same reasoning; payroll only becomes relevant once an entity exists and hires, which is several steps past this page's decision point.
- **Business Valuation, Transaction Advisory** — irrelevant; nothing about choosing an entry structure touches M&A, fundraising, or valuation.
- **Risk Advisory, Internal Controls** — generic risk-governance language would dilute the page's specificity; the reader needs regulatory/tax mechanics, not a controls framework pitch.
- **Compliance & Regulatory Services** as a separate named block — already fully covered in substance via FEMA/RBI Compliance and Accounting & Outsourcing above; listing it again as its own section would be redundant, not additive.
- **GST Advisory & Compliance** — mention only in passing (GST registration is triggered by incorporation/turnover threshold regardless of structure chosen), do not build out LUT/voluntary-registration mechanics here — that's `company-formation`/`banking-taxation`'s job and repeating it dilutes this page's actual differentiator (the decision framework).

## Explicit Guidance for the Writer
- Match the tone and evidentiary discipline of `company-formation/page.tsx` and the UK/US/Australia comparison pages: named regulations and sections (FEMA 22(R)/2016, Companies Act 2013 provisions, Section 115BAB), dated figures ("as of [date]"), a visible last-updated date and named-practice attribution line — not generic advisory-firm boilerplate.
- State the RBI draft Establishment Regulations/EROI status precisely: draft, released for consultation, not yet notified as of the stated date, with the 2016 framework (FEMA 22(R)/2016) remaining current law. Do not treat it as settled, and do not omit it — this is the single highest-value accuracy point in the whole page per Stage 3/4.
- Do not invent country-specific DTAA percentages, treaty rates, or country-tailored timelines on this page. Stay at the generic-mechanism level for tax/repatriation and route UK/US/Australia readers to the existing deep pages by name and link.
- Do not re-teach the SPICe+ incorporation process step-by-step, the full sectoral FDI table, or GST voluntary-registration mechanics — link to `company-formation`, `fdi-channels`, and `banking-taxation` respectively rather than duplicating them.
- Build the decision framework as genuinely operationalized ("choose branch if... / choose subsidiary if...") around real parameters a founder/CFO weighs — this is the page's core differentiator; don't let it collapse into a restatement of the comparison table.
- Where AU Corporate's expertise appears, it should read as demonstrated practice (a specific mechanic explained correctly, a specific compliance form named, a specific dispute pattern flagged) — never as "AU Corporate is a leading/trusted provider of..." language. Avoid superlatives, avoid listing services as a sales menu, and avoid inserting a service mention where the reader hasn't yet reached that decision point (e.g., no payroll/CFO/valuation mentions).
- Keep promotional surface area to the same pattern already used sitewide: one mid-page lead form, a "Continue Your Journey" set of internal links at the bottom, and inline links to the deeper AU Corporate pages where genuinely relevant — not a "why choose us" section. If a section starts to read like a pitch for AU Corporate rather than an answer to the reader's question, cut it back to the factual/mechanical content and move any service framing to a link, not body copy.
- The Letter of Comfort mention and the worked ₹ tax example (per Stage 4's differentiation list) should be handled as genuine practitioner detail — brief, specific, sourced — not padded into a bigger section than the topic warrants.
