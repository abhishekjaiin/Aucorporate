## Minimum Coverage List
(what the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes)

These are the items where the analyzed set converges at or near 5/5, plus two items (PE/tax treatment, named AAC-equivalent compliance) that are included here even though no competitor currently covers them well — because Stage 1/2 confirm there is real distinct search demand for both ("project office permanent establishment India" and "project office India compliance requirements" are targeted keywords, not incidental), and because a page that omits them on a high-stakes regulatory topic would leave a reader as under-served as the current majority. Presence at a basic level is the bar here; depth is what separates Minimum from Differentiation below.

1. **Eligibility gate** — the page must state plainly that a Project Office is only available to a foreign company that already has a specific contract/project in India, and should name the Regulation 5 general-permission conditions (funded by inward remittance from abroad; or by a bilateral/multilateral financing agency; or the project cleared by an appropriate authority; or the awarding Indian entity has been granted a term loan by a PFI/bank for the project) rather than leaving eligibility vague, as most of the 5 do at the "specific conditions" level of detail.
2. **Application mechanics** — Form FNC(-1) submitted through an AD Category-I Bank; state which route (general permission vs specific RBI Central Office approval) applies depending on whether the Regulation 5 conditions are met.
3. **AD Category-I Bank's role** — as the routing/reporting authority for both the general-permission and specific-approval paths.
4. **Permitted activities** — must state explicitly that activities are restricted to executing the specific project (no broader commercial trading), since every analyzed page treats this as the implicit entry condition but few state the restriction as an operating rule in its own right.
5. **ROC registration** — Form FC-1 (within 30 days), PAN/TAN.
6. **Tax/PE treatment (baseline)** — the page must at minimum state that a Project Office, unlike a Liaison Office, can constitute a Permanent Establishment/taxable presence in India and briefly explain why (it conducts revenue-generating project activity). This is table stakes for the page to be considered complete on this topic, even though it is currently a differentiator relative to competitors (see below) — competitors' silence here is the gap, not a reason to also stay silent.
7. **AAC-equivalent compliance (baseline)** — the page must name, at minimum, that an Annual Activity Certificate, FLA return, and ITR filing are required on an ongoing basis, even before adding deadline-level specificity.
8. **Closure sequence (baseline)** — the page must describe, at minimum, that closure requires an AD-bank submission with supporting certification and ROC deregistration; the fully worked step sequence is a differentiator (see below), but *some* closure treatment is now close to expected given 2/5 already cover it and it is a named secondary keyword.

## Differentiation List
(what almost nobody covers well, or covers at all — the real opportunity)

1. **Transparent resolution of the UIN contradiction.** All 5 analyzed pages take a confident position (2 say "yes UIN," 1 says "no UIN," 1 is incoherent/garbled, 1 is silent) and *none* cites a source or acknowledges disagreement. This page should not silently pick a side either. It should name that reputable sources disagree, state the more robustly evidenced framing (Form FNC-1 is tied specifically to Regulation 5's general-permission route; the UIN question may hinge on which route — general-permission/reporting vs specific-approval — applies, a distinction every competitor conflates or ignores), and stop short of a flat factual claim pending primary-source confirmation. This directly traces to Stage 3's "load-bearing finding."
2. **PE/tax-risk content integrated into the core registration page, not bolted on separately.** Stage 3 confirms 0 of the 5 pages carry this; Stage 1 confirms it only exists in a disconnected tier of tax-journal content (bcajonline.org, taxsutra, ahlawatassociates.com) that never appears alongside registration/eligibility content. Given Stage 2 already targets this as a distinct keyword with real demand, this is the single clearest content-completeness opportunity in the set: construction/installation PE, shorter treaty thresholds specific to project sites, and why PO carries materially higher PE exposure than Liaison Office.
3. **Named, dated compliance items instead of generic language.** Every analyzed page that addresses compliance at all (kbcca most explicitly) uses generic phrasing ("ensure compliance," "corporate tax planning," "GST if applicable"). None names AAC's 31 March deadline, the FLA return, or ITR-6 specifically — despite these being the actual recurring compliance entities Stage 1 identified across the wider SERP.
4. **Sectoral/geographic prior-approval triggers.** Zero of the 5 analyzed pages mention that Pakistan-origin applicants, applicants from Bangladesh/Sri Lanka/Afghanistan/Iran/China/Hong Kong/Macau seeking NE India/J&K/Andaman locations, or Defence/Private Security/Telecom/Information & Broadcasting sector projects trigger RBI Central Office prior approval rather than the AD-bank general-permission route. A founder relying on any one of these 5 pages alone could miss that their situation is gated differently — this is a genuine, actionable gap, not a nice-to-have.
5. **Full closure sequence at vjmglobal's level of specificity, matched or exceeded.** Only vjmglobal documents the real step order (CA certificate in RBI-prescribed format → ROC deregistration → AD-bank submission with CA certificate + ROC closure certificate → AD-bank compliance verification → AD-bank reporting to RBI with closure declaration). kbcca gestures at "closure documentation for RBI and ROC" without the sequence; the other 3 don't cover PO-specific closure on the analyzed page at all. This is the current bar — match the step-level precision, don't just copy it, and pair it with primary-source citation which vjmglobal itself lacks.
6. **The six-month post-approval opening window (+ discretionary further six-month extension).** Currently isolated to taxguru.in among the 5 analyzed pages and absent from every commercial service page — a concrete, decision-relevant operational fact ("what happens if I'm delayed") that founders/CFOs would value and that no competitor surfaces prominently.
7. **General-permission vs specific-approval route as an explicit decision framework.** Most pages imply eligibility as binary (qualify or don't) rather than showing the two distinct pathways and what triggers each — kbcca gets closest but doesn't name the sectoral triggers that would push an applicant into the specific-approval route.
8. **AD-bank due-diligence criteria.** Only nishithdesai.com names concrete diligence factors (promoter antecedents, nature/location of activity, source of funds, KYC) — useful practical-prep content entirely absent from the commercial cluster.
9. **Visible sourcing and authorship.** Zero of the 5 pages show a primary-source link/citation (FEMA text, RBI form, RBI Master Direction) or visible author credential/last-reviewed date. This is a trust-signal gap across the whole analyzed set, not one competitor — closing it (citations + visible authorship) is a low-competition, high-credibility win.

## Unanswered PAA/Related Questions
Pulled from Stage 1's PAA-proxy and related-search list; cross-checked against what the 5 analyzed pages actually resolve.

- **"Does RBI issue a UIN for a project office?"** — addressed by all 5, resolved by none (direct contradiction, zero sourcing on either side).
- **"Is a project office liable to tax / does it create a Permanent Establishment?"** — not answered on any of the 5 analyzed pages; only answered elsewhere in a disconnected tax-journal tier per Stage 1.
- **"What activities can a project office undertake in India?"** — addressed only at the general "tied to one specific project" level; none of the 5 spell out the operating boundary (e.g., no independent commercial trading, no activity beyond the sanctioned project) with real precision.
- **"How is a project office closed / wound up in India?"** — only fully answered by 1 of 5 (vjmglobal); a reader landing on setindiabiz, taxguru.in's process page, or nishithdesai's FAQ would not get a workable closure answer from that page alone.
- **"What is the difference between a branch office, liaison office, and project office?"** — only 1 of 5 (vjmglobal) frames PO comparatively on the same page; the rest treat PO in isolation.
- **(Implicit related question) "When do I need RBI's special approval instead of just going through my bank?"** — no page in the analyzed set answers this with the sectoral/geographic trigger list, despite it being a real gating condition per Stage 1's regulation research.

## Potential Unique Angles
(grounded in the gaps above, not invented)

- **Resolve-transparently, don't assert, the UIN question** — state that sources disagree, explain the likely general-permission/specific-approval distinction as the probable source of the conflict, and hold back a flat claim pending primary-source confirmation (mirrors how AU Corporate's Branch Office sibling page already hedges its own unresolved validity-period question, per the pipeline's carried-forward convention).
- **A single page that connects registration mechanics to tax exposure** — no competitor currently does this; PO-specific PE risk (construction/installation PE, shorter treaty thresholds) sitting directly next to the eligibility/application content, rather than siloed in a separate tax-journal piece.
- **A decision framework distinguishing the general-permission (AD-bank) route from the specific-approval (RBI Central Office) route**, explicitly naming the sectoral/geographic triggers — this is implied by the gap, not invented, since Stage 1 already surfaced the trigger list and Stage 3 confirms zero pages present it as a decision aid.
- **A worked closure checklist** at or above vjmglobal's step-level specificity, with primary-source citation added (which even vjmglobal lacks) — turns the current best-in-class competitor content into a genuinely citable, more authoritative version.
- **A compliance calendar treatment** — AAC (31 March), FLA return, and ITR-6 named with their actual deadlines rather than generic "ensure compliance" language, closing a gap present even in kbcca's otherwise-strongest full-lifecycle page.
- **The six-month opening window + extension mechanic**, currently a taxguru.in-only fact, folded into the process section as a practical "what if I'm delayed" note.

## Opportunities for Authoritative Sourcing
(where citing MCA/RBI/Income Tax/GST primary sources would beat what's currently ranking)

- **FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB, Regulation 5** — none of the 5 analyzed pages cite the regulation text directly; linking/quoting it when stating the four general-permission funding conditions would immediately out-source every competitor page checked.
- **RBI Master Direction — Establishment of BO/LO/PO** — currently only mirrored on a private domain (fedcontech.com) among the sources Stage 1 found; no analyzed competitor page links to it directly.
- **RBI's own Form FNC-1 PDF** (`rbi.org.in/Upload/Forms/Pdfs/form-fnc1.pdf`, titled "FNC 1 (See Regulation 5)") — citing this specific, verifiable government artifact would give the page a sourcing edge no competitor currently claims, and could be used carefully to frame (not resolve) the UIN question.
- **RBI's own consumer-facing FAQ page** (rbi.org.in FAQs Id=1303) — this page itself ranks in the SERP; citing/aligning with it directly borrows authority from the top government source rather than competing against it blind.
- **Companies Act 2013, Section 380 + Companies (Registration of Foreign Companies) Rules 2014, Rule 3(3)/3(4)** on mca.gov.in — no MCA primary source appeared anywhere in the SERP or the 5 analyzed pages despite Section 380/Form FC-1/FC-2 being central to the ROC side of the process. This is an open lane: no competitor, direct or indirect, currently cites MCA directly for this topic.
- **Income Tax Act, 1961 PE provisions + specific DTAA Construction/Installation PE articles** — citing actual treaty article numbers and threshold periods (rather than the generic "may create a PE" language used in the tax-journal tier) would out-cite even the more technical indirect competitors (bcajonline.org, taxsutra) on the one angle they currently own.
