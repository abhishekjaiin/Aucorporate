## Where It Falls Short

### Required Fix 1 — Internal pipeline/production language has leaked into publish-ready copy (highest priority)
Four places inside the "## Page Content" section — the part of the draft meant to ship as-is — contain language that only makes sense as internal QA notes written by one pipeline stage to another, not as copy a reader would ever see on a live page. This is the exact failure mode this review exists to catch: it is the single clearest tell that a page was assembled by an automated process rather than written and reviewed by a professional firm, and it would visibly undercut trust for any reader (or editor at AU Corporate) who notices it before publish.

1. **Line 148** (shared tax-currency footnote): "...**None of this has been confirmed against primary text in this pipeline.** Treat the section numbers below as accurate under current law only..."
   → Remove "in this pipeline." Suggested: "...None of this has been confirmed against primary legislative text. Treat the section numbers below as accurate under current law only..."

2. **Line 156** (AMT hedge): "...haven't been independently confirmed against primary-source text **in this pipeline — see Writer's Notes.**"
   → "Writer's Notes" is an internal pipeline artifact that does not exist anywhere on the live page or site — this is a dangling, meaningless reference for a real reader. Suggested: "...haven't been independently confirmed against primary-source text — confirm with a qualified tax advisor before relying on this figure."

3. **Line 182** (audit threshold): "...but it hasn't been independently verified against the primary Rules text **in this pipeline** — confirm it with your advisor before relying on it for a compliance decision..."
   → Remove "in this pipeline." Note this exact claim is *also* stated in the FAQ (line 242) without "in this pipeline" — so the fix simply brings line 182 in line with how the same fact is already, correctly, worded elsewhere on the same page.

4. **Line 205** (LLP-to-company conversion parenthetical): "...the specific section numbers and forms above should still be verified against primary MCA sources **before publication.**"
   → "Before publication" is unambiguous pre-publish QA phrasing — on a live page this reads as the site telling its own readers the content hasn't cleared internal review yet, which is actively damaging to trust, not just awkward. Suggested: "...the specific section numbers and forms above should still be confirmed with your advisor against primary MCA sources."

These are not stylistic nitpicks — each one is a phrase that, left in, would read to an actual visitor (or to a competitor doing due diligence, or to Google's own content-quality signals) as unmistakable evidence this page was AI/pipeline-assembled and not properly reviewed before going live. All four are small, mechanical, low-risk edits — no factual content changes, only removal/replacement of three phrases and one dangling internal reference.

### Optional — Minor scope ambiguity in the tax-currency footnote (line 148)
Mid-paragraph, "None of this has been confirmed against primary text..." sits close enough to the preceding sentence (about the LLP flat rate, Section 10(2A), AMT, and company rates all being "current under the Income-tax Act, 1961") that a fast reader could momentarily read "none of this" as casting doubt on the current-law rates themselves, rather than on the 2025-Act transition mapping specifically (which is what it actually means, and which the final sentence — "treat the section numbers below as accurate under current law only" — does eventually clarify). Not required, since the footnote resolves itself by its last sentence, but tightening this one clause (e.g., "None of the 2025 Act mapping above has been confirmed against primary legislative text") would remove the ambiguity outright.

### Optional — Hedge phrasing repetition
The near-identical clause "hasn't been independently verified/confirmed against primary[-source] text" recurs close to verbatim four times (lines 148, 156, 182, and FAQ 242), plus a fifth variant at line 205. Once the required fixes above are applied, none of these individually reads as jargon — but the repetition across a single page can itself start to read as a copy-pasted boilerplate disclaimer rather than a claim-specific judgment, which is a milder version of the same "assembled, not written" signal. Consider varying the phrasing across at least two or three of these instances if there's a later polish pass. Not blocking.

### Optional — Editorial inconsistency in hedge weight
Some claims with essentially the same evidentiary basis as fact-check describes it (multi-source convergence, not primary-fetched) are hedged heavily in copy (AMT rate/threshold, audit threshold) while others with the same basis are stated close to flat fact with only a light "commonly cited around" (Section 366/URC-1 conversion mechanics, Sections 56/57 reverse-conversion). This is a defensible judgment call — figures with direct compliance/financial consequences (deadlines, tax rates, thresholds) arguably warrant heavier hedging than cross-references to which statutory section governs a process — but it's worth a conscious editorial decision rather than an implicit one, since a careful reader comparing the two could reasonably ask why one set of unconfirmed citations gets a disclaimer and the other doesn't.
