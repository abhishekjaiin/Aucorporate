## Claims That Could Not Be Verified — MUST FIX

| Claim | Issue |
|---|---|
| **Construction PE duration "commonly cited in the six-to-twelve-month range"** (draft, Construction PE subsection) | This does not match the India-US DTAA specifically. Multiple independent, converging sources — including a Tax Notes reference to the Joint Committee on Taxation's report on the treaty, and multiple independent tax-guide summaries — state the India-US DTAA Construction PE threshold is **120 days in any 12-month period**, not 6-12 months. One source explicitly frames the 120-day figure as "a middle ground" between the 12-month US Model threshold and the 6-month threshold typical of developing-country treaties — which is likely exactly where the "6-12 months" figure in Stages 1-4's source material came from: a generic/global range being applied to India-US specifically when the actual treaty figure is a distinct number in between. **Recommend correcting to "120 days in any 12-month period" (India-US DTAA)** rather than a vague range, since a specific figure is available and converges across independent sources. I could not fetch the primary treaty text directly (irs.gov blocked), so recommend final confirmation against Article 5 before publish, but the current "6-12 months" framing is very likely inaccurate as stated and should not ship as-is. |
| **Associated-enterprise Service PE threshold stated as "roughly 30 days"** (draft: framing section is fine, but appears in the Service PE subsection, the subsidiary/E-Funds worked example, and FAQ #4) | Multiple independent, converging secondary sources — including language attributed to the Treasury Department's own Technical Explanation of the treaty ("without regard to" the duration threshold) — indicate that under the India-US DTAA, services furnished to a **related/associated enterprise create a Service PE with no minimum day threshold at all** — i.e., the 90-day rule is an alternative trigger for *unrelated*-party services, and the related-party trigger has no day-count qualifier whatsoever. This is materially different from, and stricter than, "roughly 30 days" — the draft's current framing likely **understates** the actual risk (implying under-30-day related-party engagements are safe, when the rule described in the sources has no safe floor at all). This affects three places in the draft: the Service PE subsection's explanatory paragraph, the subsidiary section's worked person-days example (which currently reasons "already stacking meaningful person-days against the tightened, roughly 30-day... threshold" — this framing would need to change to something like "already sufficient, since there's no minimum day threshold at all for services furnished to a related enterprise"), and FAQ #4. **This is the single highest-priority correction in this fact-check** — I could not fetch the primary treaty text or Technical Explanation directly (irs.gov blocked all session), so recommend a tax professional confirm the exact Article 5(2)(l)-equivalent language before publishing, but the "roughly 30 days" figure should not ship without that confirmation given the direction of the error (risk-understating, not just imprecise). |
| **Section 271 cited as "the penalty provision for related non-compliance"** (draft's consequence section and FAQ) | Likely incorrect/outdated as the primary citation for PE-related profit non-disclosure. Multiple converging sources confirm that Section 271(1)(c) — the general concealment-of-income/inaccurate-particulars penalty — **stopped applying to assessment years from AY 2017-18 onward**, replaced by **Section 270A** (under-reporting and misreporting of income, Finance Act 2016), which is the provision that actually fits the PE fact pattern described in the draft (previously undeclared/underreported India-attributable profits following a PE finding). Section 270A has itself been renumbered to **Section 439** under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (with Section 440 carrying the related immunity provision) per [calcguru.in's renumbering breakdown](https://calcguru.in/income-tax-act-2025-penalties-prosecution-sections/) — consistent with the pattern the transfer-pricing sibling page already uses (271AA/271BA/271G, i.e., specific sub-sections, not a bare "Section 271"). **Recommend replacing "Section 271" with "Section 270A (Income-tax Act, 1961) / Section 439 (Income-tax Act, 2025) — the under-reporting/misreporting penalty"**, or the correct current equivalent once a tax professional confirms. This is not a citation I can fully verify against primary statute text this session (incometaxindia.gov.in was reachable via search snippet only, not fetchable), so final sign-off from a qualified professional is still required — but "Section 271" as currently stated appears to be the wrong provision, not merely an unconfirmed one. |

## Regulatory Currency Check

- **Income-tax Act, 2025 vs. 1961 Act**: The draft correctly uses the 2025 Act throughout and correctly states Section 9 was not renumbered — consistent with AU Corporate's own live incorporation page. No superseded-Act references found.
- **Section 271(1)(c) — outdated general reference risk**: As detailed above, Section 271(1)(c)'s concealment-of-income penalty stopped applying from AY 2017-18 onward (replaced by Section 270A, itself renumbered to Section 439 in the 2025 Act). If the draft's "Section 271" reference is read by any careful reader as invoking the general concealment penalty, it would be citing a provision that hasn't applied for years. This is the clearest "outdated process still being referenced" finding in this check and should be treated as a genuine currency problem, not just an unconfirmed citation.
- **PE tax-rate omission**: Correctly currency-proofed by design — the draft avoids stating a specific effective rate precisely because rate schedules shift with each Finance Act, and the mechanism-only framing means this section won't go stale even if surcharge/cess bands change before or after publish.
- **RBI liaison office framework**: The general statement in the draft doesn't cite a specific Master Direction number, so it isn't at risk of referencing a superseded direction. If a specific citation is later added, use the current Master Direction on Establishment of BO/LO/PO (No. 10/2015-16, as amended) rather than the older Master Circular series, which liaison-office guidance has moved away from citing directly in recent practitioner material.
- **Construction PE and associated-enterprise Service PE thresholds**: Both appear to be stated using generic/rounded figures that don't match the specific India-US treaty numbers found this session (120 days; no minimum for related-party services) — these read as if sourced from general cross-treaty practitioner commentary rather than the India-US treaty text specifically, which is exactly the kind of drift this page's whole value proposition (treaty-specific precision) is meant to avoid.
