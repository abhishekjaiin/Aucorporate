## Claims That Could Not Be Verified — MUST FIX

| Claim | Issue |
|---|---|
| "Rule 21 of the [NDI] Rules brings in a fair-market-value/**registered-valuer** requirement" for a capital reduction or buyback connected to closure (draft, "Rule 21 of the NDI Rules" subsection) | WebSearch on Rule 21's actual text indicates the FMV certification for FEMA pricing purposes is done by a **Chartered Accountant or a SEBI-registered Category I Merchant Banker** (a Cost Accountant in some cases) — not a "registered valuer" in the technical sense of an IBBI-registered valuer under Section 247 of the Companies Act / Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017. A registered valuer's report is a *separate* requirement that can apply to a capital reduction scheme under Section 66 of the Companies Act (an NCLT-approved process), not to Rule 21's own FMV certification. The draft appears to conflate two distinct professional-certification regimes. This is stated only "at a conceptual level" in the draft and explicitly deferred to a direct conversation — but as worded, the specific professional-certifier reference is not supported by what was found and should be corrected (or genuinely re-verified against the primary Rule 21 text and, if a capital reduction is involved, the Section 66/registered-valuer requirement stated as the separate thing it is) before publication. |
| Pre-closure checklist item: "**EPF/ESIC closure** — final contributions made and the establishment's registration closed out, if the subsidiary had employees" — framed as something to complete before filing for strike-off | Multiple secondary practitioner sources (not a primary EPFO/ESIC circular) indicate the opposite sequencing: **GST cancellation must precede or accompany the STK-2 filing**, but **EPF, ESI, PAN, TAN, and IEC registrations can typically only be formally surrendered/closed *after* the company is struck off** (ESI in particular can only be marked "inoperative" pre-strike-off; full deactivation follows the STK-7 dissolution notice). If this secondary-source pattern is correct, the draft's checklist item is procedurally backwards and could mislead a reader into delaying their strike-off filing while waiting for an EPF/ESIC closure that isn't actually available to them until after the ROC dissolves the company. This needs to be checked against a primary EPFO/ESIC source or a CA/CS who handles closures routinely, and the checklist wording revised (e.g., "final contributions and returns filed; formal registration surrender for EPF/ESIC typically follows the ROC dissolution order rather than preceding it — confirm current procedure with your compliance advisor") before publication. |

## Regulatory Currency Check

- **STK forms / Section 248 process**: No evidence found of a superseded process; C-PACE centralization (2023) is correctly reflected as the *current* processing model rather than the older ROC-by-ROC system, which the draft correctly treats as historical ("used to accumulate under the earlier... model").
- **RBI's branch/liaison office reform**: Not this page's direct subject, but note for consistency — the sibling page `us-subsidiary-vs-branch-office-india` (verified by direct read) already correctly flags the October 2025 draft RBI branch/liaison reform as *not yet in force*. This closing-subsidiary draft doesn't touch branch/liaison rules directly (it links out to the dedicated closure guides instead), so no currency issue here, but worth noting the two pages should stay in sync if that reform is notified before this page publishes.
- **IBBI Voluntary Liquidation Regulations, 2017**: One search result indicated these regulations have been amended as recently as mid-2026 (i.e., very recently relative to today). The draft's decision to avoid stating specific day-count figures is the correct defensive choice given this amendment cadence — but it also means anyone adding those figures later must re-check against the current regulation text at that time, not reuse the 90/270-day figures found in this session's research without re-confirming.
- **NCTI vs GILTI terminology**: The draft's "Subpart F or NCTI (formerly GILTI)" framing is currently accurate for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025 (OBBBA renaming). Given today's date, this is the correct, current terminology — not stale. No issue.
- **No outdated process identified as still being presented as current.** The one sequencing concern (EPF/ESIC closure timing relative to strike-off) is flagged above under "Could Not Verify," not as a stale-regulation issue but as a possible procedural-order inaccuracy.
