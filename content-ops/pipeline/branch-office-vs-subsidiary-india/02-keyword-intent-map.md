## Foreign/International Framing Check
Evidence found for international-audience phrasing (limited and specific — do not over-extend it):
1. **Confirmed-verbatim** Quora thread title: *"What are the parameters to be considered by a foreign company while deciding whether to have branch office in India or a wholly owned subsidiary?"* — real, on-topic, user-generated question. This justifies using "for a foreign company" language **inside one FAQ answer**, addressed naturally.
2. Several competitor titles use "for a foreigner" / "for a foreign national" framing (ebizfiling.com, businesssetup.in) and incorpx.io's title reads "Foreign Company Registration in India: Branch vs Subsidiary vs LO" — this confirms competitors sometimes use the framing in supporting content, but none of it appeared as the *dominant* title pattern (most competitor titles are plain "Branch Office vs Subsidiary [in India]" with no "foreign" qualifier), and no PAA/related-search/Semrush volume data confirms searchers typing "foreign" into the head query itself.

Conclusion: this is enough evidence to **address the foreign-investor angle in body copy and one FAQ answer**, but not enough to put "foreign," "international," "overseas," or "cross-border" into the primary or secondary keyword strings. Target the plain-language queries ("branch office vs subsidiary," "branch vs subsidiary," "...in India"); carry the foreign-investor audience through framing/context/FAQ, not through the keyword itself — consistent with the GSC ground truth, which shows zero impressions for any "foreign"-qualified variant.
