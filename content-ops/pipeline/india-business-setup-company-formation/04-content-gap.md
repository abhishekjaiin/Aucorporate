## Minimum Coverage List
(What the page MUST address to be competitive — omitting any of these will make it look thin next to indiafilings.com, cleartax.in, vjmglobal.com, treelife.in, companiesnext.com, and incorpx.io.)

1. The full procedural skeleton, in order: DSC → DIN (Form DIR-3) → SPICe+ Part A (name reservation) → SPICe+ Part B (incorporation, PAN, TAN, GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC) → RoC issues CIN and Certificate of Incorporation. Numbered/sequential formatting, since this format is near-universal and appears to be built for a featured-snippet win.
2. Entity-type definitions at minimum — Private Limited Company, LLP, OPC, WOS, Branch Office, Liaison Office, Project Office, JV — even if the deep comparison lives elsewhere (see Recommendation #2).
3. FDI route distinction: Automatic Route vs. Government Route, with reference to the Consolidated FDI Policy / DPIIT.
4. A documents checklist (identity/address proof for directors and shareholders, registered-office proof) — plain-vanilla coverage is table stakes; specificity (apostille/notarization) moves this into Differentiation.
5. Resident-director requirement stated plainly — every foreign-investor page in the set states it in some form.
6. A cost/fee figure and a timeline figure, even a simple one — every strong competitor has some version of both; the reconciled, honest version is the differentiator (see below), but having none at all reads as incomplete.
7. GST registration mentioned as a distinct, threshold-triggered post-incorporation step, not conflated with incorporation itself.
8. A short FAQ block covering the recurring generic PAA questions: is registration fully online, minimum capital requirement, registration vs. incorporation distinction, how long it takes.
9. A visible, genuine last-updated/freshness signal (not just a "2026" claim — an actual update date), since this specific competitive field republishes annually and a stale-looking page will read as behind.
10. Coverage of both "company registration in India" and "company formation in India" phrasing on-page (per Stage 2's primary/secondary keyword decision) — the SERP overlaps heavily but not completely between the two terms.

## Differentiation List
(What almost nobody covers well, or covers at all — traced directly to Stage 3's six named gaps and the tally above. This is the real opportunity, not a generic "add more detail" list.)

1. **A single, internally reconciled cost-and-timeline range, with the variance explained.** Directly from Stage 3: even vjmglobal.com contradicts itself across its own pages (10–18 working days on one page vs. 15–30 on its Singapore-specific page), and the official 7–15 day figure repeated across the field is never reconciled against the 6–8 week real-world experience one outlying source (ahlawatassociates) notes. No page in the analyzed set does this honestly. This is a genuine, buildable authority signal, not filler.
2. **Primary-source citations.** Zero of the six analyzed pages cite specific Companies Act sections, specific RBI Master Directions or FEMA notification numbers, or the current SPICe+ form version. Topical accuracy without citation is the norm; citing chapter and section (e.g., the specific Companies Act provisions governing incorporation and resident-director requirements, the specific FEMA/Non-Debt Instruments Rules provisions governing FDI reporting) is unclaimed ground.
3. **One consolidated post-incorporation compliance roadmap.** FC-GPR, the annual FLA return, ongoing RoC annual filings, and GST registration threshold logic are treated as scattered, separately-published sub-topics even by the deepest competitors (Dezan Shira, IncorpX). No page in the set assembles them into a single "what happens after incorporation, in order, with deadlines" roadmap. This is a structural gap, not a depth gap — the individual facts exist across competitor content, but nobody sequences them.
4. **CFO-level FAQ, not founder-level FAQ.** Every FAQ block in the analyzed set stops at "is it online / how long / how much." None address repatriation mechanics (dividend, buyback, capital reduction routes), DTAA interaction with the new entity, the practical sequencing of opening a bank account relative to incorporation (a real operational blocker foreign parents hit), or what actually happens — beyond a generic penalty citation — if an FC-GPR filing is missed. This is the sharpest, most defensible gap in the whole field because it requires real technical command, not just better formatting.
5. **Procedural precision matching IncorpX, paired with credentialed authorship IncorpX lacks.** IncorpX is the only page with genuinely operator-grade detail (the apostille-vs-notarization distinction for a foreign director's documents, the exact 120-day resident-director threshold), but it has no named-author or firm-credential trust signal. VJM Global and Dezan Shira have the credential layer but (per Stage 3) inconsistent figures / template-risk respectively. Nobody combines both — this is Stage 3's explicitly named "combine two competitors' strengths" opportunity.
6. **FOCC (Foreign Owned or Controlled Company) coverage.** Only vjmglobal.com explains this more technical FEMA concept. Genuinely useful for a foreign-investor audience and essentially unclaimed elsewhere in the analyzed set.
7. **Niche but real foreign-investor pain points india-briefing.com surfaces and nobody else does:** demat account requirements for foreign companies, and trademark/brand registration considerations for a newly-incorporated foreign entity. Both are realistic post-incorporation questions a foreign parent's finance/legal team would actually ask, and both are effectively single-source right now.
8. **An Australia-specific entry point.** VJM Global explicitly claims 250+ Australian clients as social proof but — unlike its Singapore/US/UK country-specific landing pages — has apparently not built a dedicated "from Australia" entry point (unconfirmed with full certainty per Stage 3's own caveat, but flagged as a specific, literal opening). Given AU Corporate's actual positioning as an Australia-facing specialist, this is the single most direct, ownable gap in the set and should be treated as a real content angle, not just an SEO nice-to-have.
9. **An original data point, tool, or estimator.** No competitor in the analyzed set has one — every differentiation attempt found is a slightly more specific written figure, not a genuinely different asset (e.g., a simple timeline/cost estimator or a downloadable post-incorporation compliance calendar would be structurally unmatched in this field).

## Unanswered PAA/Related Questions
(Pulled from Stage 1's PAA/related-search list; cross-checked against what the six analyzed pages actually resolve — not just mention.)

- **"Can a foreigner be a director of a company in India?"** — a recurring, cross-source PAA question (Quora, Accorp Partners, ClearTax per Stage 1) but not substantively resolved by any of the six pages given full analysis in Stage 3. It surfaces only as a separate, siloed article elsewhere in the wider field. A genuine, direct answer (eligibility, DIN process for a foreign national, resident-director pairing requirement) is open ground.
- **"What is the difference between company registration and company incorporation?"** — a recurring disambiguation question per Stage 1; not confirmed as clearly and cleanly answered by any of the six analyzed pages (most use the terms interchangeably without addressing the distinction head-on).
- **"What is the minimum capital required to register a company in India?"** — a recurring PAA question; not confirmed as explicitly answered within the Stage 3 core set (likely addressed generically elsewhere in the wider field per Stage 1, but not visibly on the strongest pages analyzed here).
- **The CFO-level questions that never surface as PAA-style questions at all but are clearly real, per Stage 3's cross-page pattern finding:** how and when profits can be repatriated; how DTAA relief interacts with the new entity; the practical bank-account-opening sequence; the real operational consequence of a missed FC-GPR deadline. These aren't "unanswered PAA questions" in the literal sense — they're the *next* layer of question a real reader has after the PAA-level questions are answered, and nobody in the field goes there.
- **"Is GST registration mandatory after company incorporation in India?"** — technically addressed (companiesnext, accorppartners) but only as a disconnected side-page, not integrated into a single compliance narrative — functionally under-resolved for a reader trying to see the whole picture in one place.

## Opportunities for Authoritative Sourcing
(Where citing MCA/RBI/Income Tax/GST primary sources would beat what's currently ranking — zero of the six analyzed pages do this.)

- **Companies Act, 2013** — cite the specific governing sections rather than a bare "as per the Companies Act, 2013": incorporation requirements, private company definition/share-transfer restrictions, registered-office requirements, and the resident-director requirement, by section number.
- **MCA / SPICe+** — name the current SPICe+ form version and the MCA V3 portal explicitly, since competitors reference SPICe+ generically without version-pinning it — a small, easy, and currently-unclaimed credibility signal.
- **FEMA, 1999 and the Non-Debt Instruments Rules / Consolidated FDI Policy** — cite the specific regulatory instrument governing the Automatic vs. Government Route distinction and FDI sectoral caps, rather than the generic "as per FDI policy" phrasing used across the field.
- **RBI FIRMS portal / FC-GPR reporting** — cite the specific RBI Master Direction on reporting under FEMA and the actual filing deadline (30 days from allotment) with the compounding/penalty consequence for late filing named specifically, not just alluded to.
- **Annual FLA (Foreign Liabilities and Assets) return** — name the specific RBI requirement and its annual deadline; no analyzed competitor surfaces this in the core registration guide even though it's a direct, evidenced foreign-investor obligation.
- **GST (CGST Act)** — cite the specific registration-threshold provision rather than the generic "GST registration may be required" phrasing common across the field.
- **DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement) / Income Tax Act** — cite the relevant Income Tax Act provision governing treaty relief (Section 90) when addressing repatriation/tax questions in the CFO FAQ — genuinely unclaimed territory per the tally above.

## Decisions on the Two Open Items from Stage 2

### 1. "India subsidiary" — include as a dedicated section within this pillar (not reserved for a future standalone page)
**Recommendation: Include.** Build a dedicated H2 ("Setting Up an India Subsidiary (Wholly Owned Subsidiary)") within this pillar, using "india subsidiary" as an on-page secondary keyword.

Reasoning: This pillar's stated purpose is explicitly "for foreign companies establishing a subsidiary/company in India" — a WOS is the default vehicle that audience uses, so "india subsidiary" is not a tangential topic bolted on for keyword coverage, it is close to synonymous with the page's core mission. The seasonality gap Stage 2 flagged (mid-March vs. late-January peak) is more plausibly explained by India's March 31 fiscal-year-end driving a distinct wave of parent-company subsidiary-establishment planning than by a genuinely different searcher intent — that's a timing signal to write into the section (e.g., a line noting FY-end-driven planning timelines), not evidence the term needs a separate URL. Splitting it into its own future page now would risk cannibalizing an identical audience and closely adjacent intent with two competing on-site pages, and this pillar currently has zero organic footprint to protect. Revisit a standalone "India Subsidiary" page only if, post-launch, Semrush/GSC data shows this phrasing specifically underperforming relative to demand within the combined page — that is a data-driven follow-up call, not a launch-time one.

### 2. Entity-type comparison sub-cluster (WOS vs. branch vs. liaison vs. LLP vs. JV) — summary + link out, not a full section
**Recommendation: Summary table + link-out to the existing dedicated pages** (app/blog/wholly-owned-subsidiary, the country-specific subsidiary-vs-branch comparison pages), not a full decision-framework section within this pillar.

Reasoning: Stage 3 confirms this is a dense, separately-contested sub-cluster where the strongest treatments (india-briefing.com's multi-article depth, treelife's use-case framing) exist as dedicated, standalone content — not as a section inside a broader registration-process page. This pillar's primary keyword ("company registration in India") is process-oriented, and a full decision-framework section here risks diluting that focus and duplicating content AU Corporate already owns elsewhere. The right move: a concise comparison table on this pillar that (a) satisfies the recurring PAA question ("difference between liaison office, branch office, and wholly owned subsidiary") directly on-page so the pillar doesn't read as thin, (b) gives 2–3 sentences of basic steering (which structure typically suits a market-testing services company vs. a full operating entity vs. a pure cost-center office), then (c) links out to the dedicated pages for the deeper comparison. 

One flag for whoever owns those dedicated pages going forward: Stage 3 found that even the best existing entity-comparison content in the market (treelife, india-briefing) stays mostly at the definitional or use-case-labeled level and stops short of a genuine "if your business looks like X, choose Y" decision framework tied to real business models. That upgrade — the actual differentiation opportunity in the entity-type cluster — belongs on AU Corporate's dedicated entity-comparison pages, not on this pillar. Flagging it here so it isn't lost, but it is explicitly out of scope for this page.
