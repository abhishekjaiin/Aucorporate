## Foreign/International Framing Check
Evidence found for international-audience phrasing:
1. **"Can a foreigner be a director of a company in India?"** — recurring, cross-source PAA-style question (Quora thread, Accorp Partners, ClearTax). Justified as a direct-targeted question.
2. **FC-GPR / RBI FIRMS portal reporting** — a foreign-investor-specific compliance step with multiple dedicated competitor articles (Beacon Filing, EquityList, CertificationsBay, CA Club India), i.e., real evidenced sub-topic demand, not an assumption. Justified as a supporting/long-tail section.
3. **"What are the sectors under 100% FDI automatic route in India?"** — recurring theme across ranking content and government sources (DPIIT). Justified as a direct question/section.
4. **Competitor page titles only** (not confirmed searcher phrasing): "company registration in India for foreigners" (Vakilsearch, CompaniesNext), "register a company in India for foreigners" (commenda.io), "GST registration for foreign-owned companies in India" (Accorp Partners). These prove competitors *believe* this framing converts, not that it is the dominant searcher phrasing — flagged as long-tail candidates only, explicitly NOT promoted to primary/secondary.
5. **Prior-engagement Google Trends finding (context, not re-verified via Semrush this round):** "Company Registration in India" (plain) strongly outperforms "Foreign Company Registration in India" as a search phrase. This directly confirms the guardrail: do not target "foreign company registration in India" as primary or secondary — it is real evidence *against* inserting "foreign" into the core keyword, not for it.

Net conclusion: the "foreign/international" framing belongs in the **audience definition, page intro, and specific evidenced FAQ/sections** (director eligibility, FDI route, FC-GPR) — not in the primary or secondary keyword/H1. The page should read as answering "company registration in India," written *for* a foreign-investor reader, rather than searching for "foreign company registration in India" as a phrase.

## Stage 1 Flags — Resolutions & Handoff Notes

**1. "Company registration" vs. "company formation" — resolved above under Primary Keyword.** Recommend primary = "company registration in India," secondary = "company formation in India," both covered on-page (H1/meta on registration; formation used in a subheading, intro synonym pass, and alt meta description variant). This is a recommendation for the strategist/architecture stage to ratify given it also affects the existing page's current title — flagging explicitly rather than deciding unilaterally, since it's a live page change with SEO-equity implications (though Stage 1 also found the existing page currently has zero observable organic footprint for either term, which lowers the risk of this change).

**2. "India subsidiary" — flagged, not decided here.** Not currently targeted anywhere on the site as a page title (per pipeline brief). Prior-engagement Trends data shows it is a strong complementary keyword with a materially different seasonal peak (mid-March vs. the main term's late-January peak) — this seasonality gap suggests it may serve a distinct search occasion (e.g., fiscal-year-end planning) rather than being a simple synonym pass within this page. Two options for the next stage:
   - (a) Include "India subsidiary" as a secondary keyword / dedicated section within this pillar (e.g., "Setting Up an India Subsidiary (WOS)"), accepting some cannibalization risk with a possible future dedicated page; or
   - (b) Address it only lightly/naturally here and reserve exact-title optimization for a future standalone "India Subsidiary" page timed to its March peak.
   No Semrush volume was available to break this tie. **Recommend the content-gap/architecture stage decide**, informed by the seasonality data point above.

**3. Entity-type decision cluster (WOS vs. branch vs. liaison vs. LLP vs. JV) — flagged, not decided here.** Stage 1 confirmed this is a dense, competitively contested sub-cluster with dedicated pages across companiesnext.com, incorpx.io, kdpaccountants.com, dugainadvisors.com, ahlawatassociates.com, taxguru.in, lexology.com, and india-briefing.com. From a pure keyword/intent standpoint, the relevant queries here ("best business structure in India for foreign investors," "liaison office vs branch office vs subsidiary India," and the related PAA question) are legitimate, audience-aligned, and evidenced — but given the competitive density, this map recommends **address naturally** (a summary/comparison section with clear internal links) rather than attempting to fully target and rank this sub-cluster within the same page as the core registration process. Whether it becomes its own linked cluster page or stays as an on-page summary is a content-architecture call, not a keyword-intent call — flagging per the brief's instruction, not deciding.
