## Minimum Coverage List
(What the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes. Absence of any of these will make the page look thinner than the field, even though none of them is the differentiator.)
1. State plainly, early, that VAT/GST/sales-tax obligations apply to AI services sold on a usage/token/API/compute basis — not exempt, not a gray area on the "does this apply at all" question.
2. Acknowledge the no-threshold / register-even-without-local-presence reality for foreign digital-service providers (the pattern nearly every competitor states, in India via OIDAR and globally via country-by-country thresholds).
3. Cover India OIDAR mechanics (place of supply under IGST s.13(12), RCM for B2B, GSTR-5A/REG-10 for foreign suppliers) at a level of specificity that matches the CA-firm cluster — but pointed at the seller, not the buyer (see Differentiation List — this is where the page must go further than "cover it," it must reframe it).
4. Name Merchant of Record (MoR) as a known commercial approach readers will have already encountered in their research, since a 5+ page cluster in the wider market sells directly against this pain point — ignoring it entirely would look like an information gap even though AU Corporate is not an MoR.
5. Match the honest "this area is still settling" credibility signal used by the strongest pages (thetaxadviser, anrok, 1stopvat) rather than asserting confident rules that don't exist yet — particularly around emerging AI-specific usage taxes, US state rulings still developing, and EU ViDA scope.
6. Use current 2026 regulatory references and visible recency signals (last-reviewed date), since every page that ranks well in this space carries 2026 dates and this is an actively moving topic.
7. Ground jurisdiction-specific mechanics only in verified jurisdictions — per the constraint carried into this stage, detailed per-country mechanics should be limited to US, Canada, UK, EU/OSS, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Serbia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, China, India, and Turkey (AU Corporate's existing verified sibling-page jurisdictions). A "coverage across 87 jurisdictions" headline claim is fine; do not fabricate mechanics for the other ~74.

## Differentiation List
(What almost nobody covers well, or covers at all — the real opportunity. Every item below traces to a specific observed gap in Stage 3's per-page analysis or Stage 1's corpus-wide pattern, not a generic "go deeper" suggestion.)

1. **Carry token/usage-based taxable-event classification through to actual VAT/GST consequence, globally.** taxprofblog and anrok both do the classification reasoning well (tokens as a wrapper around underlying rights/functionality, usage measured by prompt size/compute/output) but stop at US sales/use tax. No page connects that reasoning to VAT place-of-supply, registration thresholds, or invoicing treatment in the EU or India. This is the single largest, most specific gap in the corpus.

2. **Resolve the AI API marketplace/deemed-supplier liability question instead of only posing it.** 1stopvat raises "who collects — the agent, platform, or seller" but never answers it, and frames it around shopping agents, not API/model aggregators. No page in either pool answers this for an AI API marketplace specifically, and no page cites how a real platform handles it today. AU Corporate can resolve this with reference to documented current practice (e.g., OpenRouter's own published tax-ID/invoicing documentation showing VAT/GST calculated and added at invoice time via its payment processor) and by being explicit that India's CGST s.9(5) deemed-supplier mechanism is currently notified only for passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping, and restaurant services — it has not been extended to AI API marketplaces in any source found, so any claim that it applies must be framed as "not yet extended here, monitor," not asserted as current law.

3. **Draw the MoR-vs-deemed-supplier distinction explicitly.** A large commercial cluster (dodopayments and the 5-page MoR vendor set) sells Merchant of Record as *the* answer, while a separate cluster discusses "deemed supplier" as a regulatory liability concept — and Stage 1/3 confirm these get conflated in market messaging. No page anywhere in the corpus draws the line: MoR is a contractual/commercial arrangement a company opts into; deemed supplier is a regulatory liability assignment that exists (or doesn't) independent of any vendor relationship. This is a clean, defensible differentiator that requires no invented facts, only accurate framing of two already-documented concepts.

4. **Treat prepaid-credit-purchase-vs-consumption VAT timing as its own named issue.** No page treats this as a standalone practical question, even though it sits just under the surface of thetaxadviser's real-time-reporting point and taxprofblog's usage-measure analysis. AI billing structurally invites this ambiguity (buy $500 of credits now, consume tokens over months) in a way flat-fee SaaS subscriptions don't — this is a genuinely AI-specific timing question, not a generic "add more detail" ask.

5. **Cover India from the seller/exporter side, not just the buyer/importer side.** Confirmed absent across the entire researched corpus (6 deep-analyzed pages and the wider 18-page scan): every India-focused piece found addresses either the Indian recipient importing AI tools (RCM on ChatGPT/Claude subscriptions) or the Indian AI company's own input-side ITC position (cggst). None addresses a non-Indian AI company's OIDAR registration obligation to sell into India, or an Indian AI company's export-of-services treatment (IGST s.2(6)) when billing token/API usage to customers abroad. This is open ground with real search-and-content evidence of demand (the "foreign digital service provider" phrasing pattern documented in Stage 2) and no direct competitor currently occupying it.

6. **Give the reader a decision framework, not just awareness.** No page in the deep-analyzed set gives a founder/CFO a "if your billing model is X and your customer is in Y, here is what changes for you" structure — every one is descriptive. Given how fragmented the corpus is (each competitor stakes out exactly one narrow lane per Stage 3's cross-page pattern), a structured framework spanning billing-model type × customer jurisdiction × seller jurisdiction is something literally no competitor page attempts, not an incremental improvement on an existing structure.

7. **Address the bundled AI+non-AI invoice edge case.** Only anrok raises this (bundled offerings complicating tax treatment) and does so in passing. Worth a dedicated, brief treatment given how common bundled AI-feature/non-AI-platform pricing has become.

## Unanswered PAA/Related Questions
Caveat carried forward from Stages 1–2: no literal Google PAA box or related-search chips were ever confirmed by the tooling in this pipeline. The items below are Stage 2's inferred question-shaped topics (built from recurring title/snippet phrasing across independent domains), cross-checked here against what the six deep-analyzed pages actually resolve. None should be treated as verified verbatim PAA text.

- **Who collects VAT on an AI API marketplace — the platform or the seller of the underlying model?** — Posed by 1stopvat, resolved by no page in either pool.
- **Is VAT/GST charged on AI credits at purchase (top-up) or at consumption (token usage)?** — Not directly answered by any page; only tangentially implied by thetaxadviser/taxprofblog.
- **Does usage-based billing change VAT place-of-supply timing compared to flat-fee SaaS?** — Not answered anywhere; this is the natural pairing of gap #1 and #4 above.
- **Do foreign OIDAR/digital-service providers have to register for GST in India even without a local office?** — Partially answered, but only from the framing of "yes, foreign providers selling to Indian consumers" (buyer-facing pieces); never answered for an AI company specifically deciding whether/how to register as a seller.
- **How are AI tokens and compute credits characterized for tax purposes (goods vs. services vs. rights)?** — Answered well for US sales/use tax (taxprofblog) but not translated into a VAT/GST characterization anywhere.
- **Merchant of Record vs. deemed supplier — are these the same thing?** — Not asked or answered as a distinct question by any page; inferred as a real point of confusion from the co-occurrence of both content clusters in the same search space (Stage 1).

## Potential Unique Angles
(Grounded in the gaps documented above — none of these is invented; each maps to a numbered item in the Differentiation List.)

- **"Look through the token" reasoning, globalized.** Adopt taxprofblog's precise characterization method (what is the token actually a wrapper for — compute access, output rights, a functional service) and apply it to VAT/GST place-of-supply and India OIDAR classification, not just US sales/use tax. → Maps to Differentiation #1.
- **A resolved answer on marketplace liability, anchored to real current practice.** Rather than posing the "who collects VAT" question the way 1stopvat does, answer it with reference to documented aggregator practice (OpenRouter's public tax-ID/invoicing docs) and an explicit, correctly scoped statement on India's s.9(5) deemed-supplier mechanism not yet reaching AI marketplaces. → Maps to Differentiation #2.
- **A named "MoR is not the same as deemed supplier" explainer.** A short, clearly labeled section distinguishing the commercial mechanism from the regulatory one — genuinely differentiated because no page does this despite both concepts co-occurring constantly in this search space. → Maps to Differentiation #3.
- **A standalone "when does the taxable event actually happen" timing section** for prepaid credits vs. consumption — an AI-specific version of a question that doesn't arise the same way for flat-fee SaaS. → Maps to Differentiation #4.
- **A foreign-seller-into-India / Indian-exporter-of-API-access section** — the one clearly open India angle nobody has taken, addressed from the seller's registration and export-of-services position rather than the buyer's RCM position. → Maps to Differentiation #5.
- **A billing-model × jurisdiction decision framework** (e.g., a simple matrix or flowchart: usage-metered vs. prepaid-credit vs. flat-fee billing, crossed with seller jurisdiction and customer jurisdiction, showing what changes for registration/place-of-supply/invoicing) — the clearest "decision support, not more awareness" opportunity identified in Stage 3's verdict. → Maps to Differentiation #6.
- **A brief bundled-invoice edge-case note** (AI features bundled with non-AI platform functionality on one invoice) as a short FAQ-style item. → Maps to Differentiation #7.

Per the jurisdiction constraint carried into this stage: the decision framework and any per-country mechanics referenced within these angles should be populated only with the verified jurisdictions (US, Canada, UK, EU/OSS, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Serbia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, China, India, Turkey). A broader "87 jurisdictions" figure may appear as a headline coverage claim but should not be used to imply detailed mechanics exist on-page for jurisdictions outside that verified list.

## Opportunities for Authoritative Sourcing
(Where citing primary sources would beat what's currently ranking — none of the analyzed competitor pages were confirmed via WebFetch to cite primary legal text directly; several read as blog-level paraphrase of secondary sources.)

- **India — GST/IGST Act provisions and CBIC materials**: IGST Act s.13(12) (place of supply for OIDAR), IGST Act s.2(6) (export-of-services conditions — the exact provision the seller-side gap in Differentiation #5 turns on), CGST Act s.9(5) (e-commerce operator deemed supplier — notified categories), CGST Act s.52 (TCS collector mechanism, for the s.9(5)-vs-s.52 distinction Stage 1 flagged as well-explained elsewhere but not applied to AI), GSTR-5A/REG-10 procedural requirements, and any GST Council/CBIC notification governing OIDAR rate/classification — citing the primary provision number rather than paraphrasing a CA-firm blog's summary of it would be a direct authority upgrade over the entire India-focused cluster in the broad set.
- **EU — the "electronic interface" deemed-supplier rule and OSS/IOSS mechanics**: citing the EU VAT e-commerce package / Council Implementing Regulation text directly (rather than a secondary vatcalc.com/cashflo-style explainer) for the deemed-supplier scope question, plus flagging ViDA's current (not yet finalized) status accurately rather than treating it as settled.
- **US — actual state Department of Revenue guidance**: Indiana and Illinois rulings on generative-AI chatbot/API access are cited by anrok and taxprofblog only as facts, not as documents; linking or citing the underlying ruling/letter directly (where verifiable) would be a step up in rigor those two otherwise-strong pages don't take.
- **Primary-source evidence of real marketplace practice**: OpenRouter's own published tax-ID/invoicing documentation is the strongest available piece of "how does a real AI API marketplace actually handle VAT/GST today" evidence found across both research pools — no competitor page cites it; using it to resolve the marketplace-liability question (Differentiation #2) would be citing verifiable primary evidence rather than speculation, which is exactly what 1stopvat's otherwise-good piece is missing.
- **General framing discipline**: given the jurisdiction-scope constraint, all specific rate/threshold/registration-mechanic claims on the page should trace to the verified jurisdiction list (US, Canada, UK, EU/OSS, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Serbia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, China, India, Turkey) and cite the actual statute/regulation/notification for that jurisdiction where possible — this alone would exceed the sourcing rigor of every page in the deep-analyzed set, none of which was confirmed to cite primary legal text directly.
