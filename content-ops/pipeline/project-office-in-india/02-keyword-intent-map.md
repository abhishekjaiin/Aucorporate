## UIN Question — Hedged Treatment Required (do not target as a flat FAQ)

**"Does a project office need a UIN?" must not be treated as a confidently-answered keyword/FAQ at this stage.**

Stage 1's SERP research found directly contradictory sources: one synthesis states "No UIN is allotted to a PO" (a claimed PO/BO-LO distinguishing feature); another states RBI issues a UIN for the PO upon approval. The orchestrator's follow-up research leaned toward POs *do* receive a UIN via Form FNC-1/Regulation 5 — contradicting a premise this pipeline had been carrying forward from the published Branch Office and Liaison Office pages — but explicitly labeled this **still not primary-source confirmed** (WebFetch to rbi.org.in remained blocked).

Given this, the query "does a project office need a UIN" / "does RBI issue a UIN for a project office" should be:
- **Routed to a hedged treatment**, not a flat-answer FAQ or optimized H2/meta target — directly analogous to how the Branch Office page hedges its own unresolved validity-period question (per topics.csv: "Branch Office validity/renewal period — no fixed numeric cycle found against a primary RBI source, page states non-numeric framing instead of guessing").
- Addressed naturally in body copy only if it can be framed around the more robustly evidenced fact (Form FNC-1 as the PO-specific application tied to Regulation 5's general-permission conditions) without asserting a firm UIN yes/no.
- **Not** used as page title, H1, meta description, or a title-tag-optimized FAQ answer until Stage 9 fact-check resolves it against a primary source.
- Flagged again here for Stage 9: the live Branch Office and Liaison Office pages currently carry a "PO doesn't need a UIN" line in their own comparison sections that this pipeline's fresh research now casts doubt on — Stage 9 should decide whether that needs correcting on the sibling pages, per the orchestrator's explicit instruction not to silently edit live pages from this pipeline.

## Foreign/International Framing Check

Evidence found for international-audience phrasing: **weak/indirect only.** Several ranking page *titles* use "for Foreign Companies" or "by Foreign Entities" as descriptive framing (taxguru.in "...By Foreign Entities," setindiabiz.com "...for Foreign Companies," legalbabu.com "Open a Project Office of Foreign Company in India") — this reflects that a Project Office is *legally* only ever opened by a foreign company (unlike, say, generic company registration, which serves both domestic and foreign founders). That is a structural/legal fact about the entity type, not confirmed evidence of how searchers phrase their query. No PAA, related-search, or Semrush data (Semrush unavailable) was found using "foreign," "international," "overseas," or "cross-border" as a modifier *within* the core query itself (e.g., no evidence for "foreign company project office registration India" as a distinct higher-intent query vs. plain "project office registration India").

**Call:** Do not insert "foreign," "international," "overseas," or "cross-border" into the primary or secondary keywords. The audience (foreign/overseas parent companies undertaking a specific project in India) should be addressed through page content — eligibility criteria, "who this is for" framing, examples — not through the targeted keyword phrasing itself. This matches the plain-language pattern the SERP researcher found dominant across the ranking set (nearly every competitor's URL slug and H1-equivalent title uses bare "project office in India" / "project office registration," reserving "foreign" as a secondary descriptive word in the title, not the lead phrase).
