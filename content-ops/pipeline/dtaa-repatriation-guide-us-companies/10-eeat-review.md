- The MLI/PPT-absence section is the clearest non-obvious insight on the page: it correctly identifies that the India-US treaty's anti-abuse posture is the *opposite* of what a reader familiar with India's other treaties (e.g., the UK) would expect, explains why (the US hasn't signed the OECD MLI), and does this without overclaiming immunity from scrutiny generally (GAAR remains a separate backstop, stated plainly). - The dual CA/US-CPA honesty boundary in the closing FTC section is a genuine, non-manufactured differentiator: it correctly scopes what AU Corporate does and doesn't do across a jurisdictional line, which is precisely the kind of clarity a foreign client needs and that most competitor content — which either ignores the US side entirely or implies broader capability than it has — doesn't provide.
- The "make available" FIS standard is applied correctly and specifically to the management-fee scenario, distinguishing it from the more commonly seen "Fees for Technical Services" framing used under India's other treaties — a precise, treaty-specific point rather than a copy-pasted generic explanation.

## Where It Falls Short

1. **Management-fee/Business Profits hedge is softer than the underlying evidence quality warrants.** The claim carries real audit exposure per fact-check (single, unsourced competitor as the only basis) but reads in the body copy with more confidence than that evidence quality supports. Tighten the in-copy hedge to match the strength already used elsewhere on the page for comparably uncertain figures.
2. **Buyback section is silent on non-resident promoter status**, stating the favorable 12.5% rate without scoping it to non-promoter shareholders or flagging that promoter status produces a materially worse (22%/30%) outcome. This is an omission that could mislead a specific class of reader even though every stated figure is individually accurate — worth a one-line fix.
3. **No named individual expert attribution**, only a practice-level byline. Given the page's regulatory depth and the standing human-sign-off requirement already built into this pipeline's process, this is a missed opportunity to convert an internal quality-control step into a visible, reader-facing trust signal (see recommendation below).
4. **Long, dense single-paragraph callouts** (the PPT-absence callout; the FTC section's §245A paragraph, both 150–200+ words) were flagged by Stage 8 as a soft readability note and not fixed. Content is accurate, but a page working this hard to earn trust through precision should not risk losing a skimming reader in a wall of text at exactly its two most sophisticated points. Not a blocking EEAT issue, but a genuine one worth a light pass.
