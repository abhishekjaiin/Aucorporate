## Minimum Coverage List
(what the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes; every serious page in this field has some version of these)

1. The core process sequence end-to-end: entity selection mention → SPICe+ (Form INC-32, Part A/B) → DSC (Class 3) → DIN → PAN/TAN/GSTIN via AGILE-PRO-S → post-incorporation FDI reporting (FC-GPR on the RBI FIRMS portal).
2. Named entity-type options (Private Limited Company, LLP, Wholly Owned Subsidiary, Branch Office, Liaison Office, Project Office) — at minimum named and briefly characterized, even before any differentiated decision framework is layered on.
3. The resident-director requirement stated as a hard rule (Companies Act 2013, Section 149(3), 182-day test) — this is a genuine qualifying question that gates whether a US founder can self-serve, and every competitor names it.
4. A documents checklist (passport, proof of address, registered-office proof, board resolution/authorization).
5. FDI Automatic Route vs. Government/Approval Route named as a sector-dependent split.
6. Some stated cost figure and some stated timeline figure — even though Stage 1/2/3 all confirm these are inconsistent and unscoped across the field, their presence (in some form) is now a category expectation; absence would make the page look incomplete relative to competitors, even a bad or unscoped number is currently "normal" in this SERP.
7. An FAQ section addressing the recurring proxy-PAA questions (can a US citizen register; do I need a resident director; how long; how much; entity choice).
8. Education-led framing with a consultation CTA at the end, not a hard-sell opening — this matches the observed intent pattern across the whole category (informational-commercial, not transactional).

## Differentiation List
(what almost nobody covers well, or covers at all — the real opportunity, each traced to an observed gap above)

1. **A full 5-way entity decision table** (Pvt Ltd / LLP / Wholly Owned Subsidiary / Branch Office / Liaison Office / Project Office) with selection criteria tied to actual business activity (revenue-generating operations vs. liaison-only vs. project-based). Zero of the six analyzed pages resolve this into one synthesized artifact — entity types are named across different pages/sections but never brought together with selection logic. This is the single strongest, independently-confirmed signal across Stages 1-3.
2. **A direct answer to the resident-director gating question**: what a solo US founder with no existing Indian contact actually does (nominee director arrangement — mechanics, safeguards, indicative cost). Every page in the analyzed set states the rule; none resolve the practical follow-on problem it creates for the exact searcher this page targets.
3. **Cost and timeline scoped together, in one place, on a defensible basis** — incorporation-only vs. incorporation + FDI reporting + bank-account-operational, stated as distinct figures rather than blended or split across a site's own content ecosystem (the failure mode observed even within single competitors like indiafilings and masllp). The underlying pieces exist across the field; nobody assembles them coherently.
4. **Branch/Liaison/Project Office (FC-1 route) given equal structural weight**, not a bolt-on paragraph appended to a subsidiary-focused piece. Only one of six analyzed pages (masllp) covers it at all, and even that treatment is explicitly flagged as subordinate to the subsidiary path. A US parent seriously evaluating a non-subsidiary presence currently has nowhere well-built to go.
5. **A substantiated currency claim**: state explicitly that SPICe+ Part A has superseded the standalone RUN/Form INC-1 name-reservation step, rather than either omitting the point or (as at least one competitor does) describing the legacy step as still live. This converts a checkable accuracy edge into a trust signal, since "2026" title-stamping across the field is a freshness signal with no substance behind it on any analyzed page.
6. **Primary-source inline citation** — naming and linking mca.gov.in's SPICe+ form/FAQ resources, the RBI FIRMS portal, and specific Companies Act sections (149(3), 164) as identifiable references rather than generic mentions. No analyzed page shows this.
7. **US-specific document mechanics (apostille vs. embassy legalisation)** — observed on zero of the five true competitor pages in this analyzed set (it exists only on AU's own sibling registercompanyinindia.com page, which is a data point on the field, not a competitor threat). This is a real, non-generic differentiator opportunity for a US-audience-specific page, distinct from the generic "proof of address" documentation most competitors give.

**Caution flagged, not recommended without Stage 5 sign-off**: the Startup India tax-incentive angle (Section 80-IAC/56 exemptions), observed on only 1 of 6 pages (vjmglobal), is a genuine differentiator where it appears — but STATUS.md scopes this page to registration process, not a tax-incentive deep-dive, and the existing AU hub page already owns adjacent tax material (transfer pricing, Section 482, Form 3CEB). Flagging as a possible light-touch mention rather than a structural section; Stage 5 should decide whether it fits this page's scope or belongs elsewhere in the cluster.

## Unanswered PAA/Related Questions

Pulled from Stage 1's PAA-proxy list (question-phrased proxy queries, not verbatim-captured Google PAA text — see Stage 1/2 caveats) and Stage 2's Foreign/International Framing Check, checked against what the six analyzed pages actually resolve:

- **Do I need an Indian resident director to register a company in India?** — Answered as a rule everywhere; **not resolved** as a practical problem (what to do without one). Genuinely unanswered at the level a self-serve searcher needs.
- **Private limited company vs. LLP vs. branch office — which should a US parent company use in India?** — Named on multiple pages; **not resolved** into an actual decision framework anywhere in the analyzed set.
- **How much does it cost to register a company in India from the USA?** — Answered inconsistently; no analyzed page gives a scoped, sourced figure that would hold up to scrutiny.
- **How long does it take to register a private limited company in India for foreign investors?** — Answered inconsistently and often unscoped (registered vs. operational conflated); genuinely unresolved as a clear, single answer.
- **Does the process differ for a US citizen vs. other foreign nationals?** — Flagged in Stage 2's Foreign/International Framing Check as a legitimate edge-case qualifying question; not addressed by any analyzed page (reasonable FAQ-only addition, not a heading-level target per Stage 2's keyword guidance).
- **Can a US citizen register a company in India?** — Reasonably well answered across the category at a basic level (yes, via various entity structures); not a strong unresolved gap on its own, but the "how" behind the yes is where the real gaps above live.
- **What documents are required to register a company in India from the USA?** — Reasonably well answered by the field (checklist-style coverage is common); the sharper unresolved layer is the apostille-vs-embassy-legalisation specificity, not the base checklist.

## Potential Unique Angles
(grounded in the gaps above, not invented)

1. **A genuine decision-support artifact, not a prose list**: build the entity comparison as an actual table with selection criteria mapped to what the US parent is trying to do (sell into India vs. representative presence vs. time-bound project) — implied directly by the confirmed absence of this artifact across every analyzed competitor.
2. **A worked resolution of the resident-director problem**: rather than restating Section 149(3), walk through the nominee-director arrangement as a real mechanism (what it is, what it costs in broad terms, what governance safeguards apply) — implied by the observed pattern of every competitor stating the rule and stopping there.
3. **A single cost/timeline framework presented once, scoped explicitly**: incorporation-only figures and full operational-readiness figures (FDI reporting + bank account) shown side by side with the scope boundary stated in the same place they're presented — implied by the observed failure mode of even single competitors blending these across their own content ecosystems.
4. **A "process currency" statement**: an explicit note that SPICe+ Part A has replaced standalone RUN/Form INC-1 name reservation, positioned as a factual correction/clarification rather than a generic freshness claim — implied by at least one analyzed competitor's apparent description of the legacy step as current, and by the field-wide pattern of title-year-stamping without substantiation.
5. **Equal-weight treatment of the non-subsidiary path (Branch/Liaison/Project Office via FC-1)** as a first-class alternative for US parent companies not looking to set up a subsidiary — implied by the observed pattern of every analyzed page defaulting hard to the subsidiary path and treating the alternative, where it appears at all, as an afterthought.
6. **US-specific document mechanics (apostille vs. embassy legalisation)** treated as its own clearly stated point rather than folded into a generic "proof of address" document list — implied by its complete absence across every true competitor page in the analyzed set.

## Opportunities for Authoritative Sourcing
(where citing MCA/RBI/Income Tax/GST primary sources would beat what's currently ranking — grounded in the confirmed absence of inline primary-source citation across every analyzed page)

- **mca.gov.in** — SPICe+ (Form INC-32) portal and its official FAQ/instruction resources, plus the linked forms AGILE-PRO-S and INC-9, cited by name/form number rather than described only in prose.
- **Companies Act 2013** — Section 149(3) (resident director, 182-day test) and Section 164 (director disqualification), cited as specific sections rather than paraphrased as a general rule — directly resolves the "state the rule but don't cite it" pattern observed across all six analyzed pages.
- **RBI FIRMS portal / FC-GPR filing** — the specific post-incorporation FDI reporting requirement (within 30 days of share allotment), citable to RBI's own FIRMS portal guidance rather than a generic "report to RBI" statement.
- **FEMA framework** — the Automatic Route vs. Government/Approval Route split, citable to the applicable FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules / sector-specific notification rather than the generic split-mention every competitor gives.
- **FC-1 registration** — for the Branch/Liaison/Project Office path, citable to the specific RBI Authorized Dealer bank approval mechanism under FEMA, which would substantiate the equal-weight treatment recommended above rather than leaving it as an unsupported alternative.
- **Income Tax Act / Startup India portal** — only if the Startup India tax-incentive angle (Section 80-IAC, Section 56) is greenlit by Stage 5 within this page's scope; citable to CBDT notifications or the official Startup India recognition criteria rather than a general "tax benefits exist" mention.

Note: DTAA/treaty-level sourcing (India-US treaty text, Form 1118) is intentionally not listed here as a target for this page — per STATUS.md this page is registration-process-scoped, and that material is already owned by AU's existing hub page. Flagging its exclusion explicitly so Stage 5/6 don't duplicate scope.
