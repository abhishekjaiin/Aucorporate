# Competitor Reverse-Engineering: Branch Office vs Subsidiary in India

## Method note (read before using this report)
Direct `WebFetch` of every candidate page was blocked by the network egress proxy again this stage (setindiabiz.com, ebizfiling.com, incorpx.io, india-briefing.com, treelife.in, kanakkupillai.com all returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`) — the same condition Stage 1 hit for every domain tested, including neutral sites. `mcp__Semrush__organic_research` and `mcp__Semrush__backlinks_research` both returned "insufficient API units," the same failure Stage 1 and Stage 2 hit — **no organic-traffic, keyword-footprint, or backlink-profile data was obtainable for any competitor domain this session.** The "organic-content-driven vs. domain-authority-driven" sanity check called for in the process could not be run quantitatively; the qualitative judgment in each entry below is reasoned from indirect evidence (domain type, number of competing articles per site, presence/absence of visible authority markers) and is flagged as such, not presented as Semrush-verified.

In place of full-page fetch, this stage ran multiple targeted `WebSearch` queries per page (beyond Stage 1's broader discovery queries) to pull the search tool's synthesized content summaries for each specific URL. This surfaces more per-page detail than Stage 1 had, but it is still a synthesized secondary read of each page's content, not a verified capture of literal on-page text, structure order, FAQ wording, author bylines, or CTA copy. Any claim below about what a page *does not* cover is a statement about what didn't surface in available evidence, not a confirmed absence — flagged inline where that distinction matters.

## Per-Page Analysis

### setindiabiz.com — "Branch Office vs Subsidiary: Key Differences Explained"
**Does well:** Names the specific governing regulation (FEMA Establishment-of-Branch-Office Regulations, 2000) rather than just citing "FEMA" generically. Gives a genuinely useful, itemized list of what a branch office is actually permitted to do (export/import trading, consultancy, research, technical/financial collaboration, buying/selling agency, IT/software services, parent-company technical support) — this answers the real founder question "what can I actually do with a branch office" rather than staying abstract. Cites a specific effective tax rate (41.60–43.68%) rather than a rounded figure. Separately covers the branch-to-subsidiary conversion path (re-incorporation under Companies Act 2013) and gives setup-timeline figures (subsidiary ~15–20 business days vs. branch/liaison ~30–45 days due to RBI approval).
**Does poorly:** Timeline figures are not sourced/dated, and conflict with other competitors' figures for the same claim (see Cross-Page Patterns). No evidence surfaced of coverage of the RBI 2025 draft Establishment Regulations / EROI reform, Letter of Comfort, or DTAA — real gaps if true, though not fully confirmable without page fetch. Structure reads as a standard difference-by-category walkthrough (legal status → activities → control → tax) rather than being organized around actual decision triggers (e.g., "choose X if...").
**Why it likely ranks:** Highest apparent presence across Stage 1's 13 query variants, on a domain running many overlapping registration/comparison articles — reads as topical-coverage-volume and on-page specificity (naming exact regulations, exact tax percentages) rather than an obvious backlink/authority signal; not Semrush-verified either way.
**What would beat it:** Match its specificity on permitted-activities and tax rate, then add the conversion-mechanics, EROI-reform status, and DTAA/repatriation context it appears to be missing, with cited timeline figures instead of unsourced ranges.

### ebizfiling.com — "Branch office or Indian subsidiary — what is best for a foreigner"
**Does well:** Frames the comparison explicitly around the foreign-founder decision ("what is best for a foreigner"), which matches the actual search intent better than a neutral "key differences" framing. Covers the same core categories (structure, activities, control, taxation, liability) with reasonably clear separation between "what a branch can do" and "what a subsidiary can do."
**Does poorly:** Tax and compliance claims stay generic ("different tax treatments apply," "subsidiaries enjoy local tax benefits") without citing an actual percentage, section number, or example — noticeably vaguer than setindiabiz or treelife on the exact same point. The domain runs at least four separate, overlapping articles on this same comparison (this one, "Branch Office vs Indian Subsidiary," "Opening Indian Subsidiary or Branch Office... Advantages & Disadvantages," "Difference between Branch Office and Wholly Owned Indian Subsidiary") — a sign of SEO-driven content proliferation rather than one authoritative, maintained page, which likely dilutes rather than strengthens any single URL's depth.
**Why it likely ranks:** Title/intent match to a real query pattern ("for a foreigner," "which is best") plus sheer count of near-duplicate URLs increasing the odds any one of them ranks for a given phrasing — not depth of any single page.
**What would beat it:** Keep the decision-oriented "what is best for a foreign founder" framing but back every claim with the specific figures ebizfiling leaves generic, in one single, maintained page rather than four fragmented ones.

### incorpx.io — "Subsidiary vs Branch vs Liaison Office India (2026)"
**Does well:** Widens the comparison to three structures (subsidiary, branch, liaison office) rather than two, which matches real search behavior — Stage 1/2 found several competitors and a Quora thread treating this as a 3-or-4-way decision, not strictly branch-vs-subsidiary. Gives a clear, specific restriction for liaison offices (cannot conduct any commercial, trading, or manufacturing activity; funded entirely by inward remittance from the parent) that other pages treat more vaguely.
**Does poorly:** Despite the "(2026)" title signaling freshness, no coverage of the RBI 2025 draft Establishment Regulations / EROI framework surfaced in available evidence — a real freshness gap if confirmed, since this is precisely the kind of 2025–26 regulatory development a "(2026)"-dated page should be expected to cover, and other sources (india-briefing, taxguru, EY, KPMG, Lexology) are actively covering it. The "(2026)" in the title functions as a freshness *signal* without confirmed freshness *substance* on the one live regulatory story that matters most right now.
**Why it likely ranks:** Explicit year-tagging in the title is a plausible CTR/freshness signal, combined with broader (3-way) topic coverage than most 2-way competitor pages.
**What would beat it:** Keep the 3-way framing (it's genuinely more useful) but actually cover the EROI/draft-regulation status accurately and explicitly, since that is the one place a dated title creates an expectation this page appears not to meet.

### india-briefing.com — "Representative Office, Branch Office, Project Office vs LLP's and Subsidiary in India"
**Does well:** Broadest and most precise structural scope of any page reviewed — covers representative/liaison office, project office, LLP, subsidiary, and (by cross-reference) branch office as five distinct options, and draws a specific distinction most competitors blur: a project office can conduct commercial activity tied to its specific contract, while a liaison office cannot conduct any commercial activity at all. Closes with an actual decision framework (sector, business type, desired control, funding mode) rather than stopping at a static comparison table — closer to real decision support than most of the set.
**Does poorly:** The specific comparison sub-page (as opposed to the annual "Introduction to Doing Business in India" flagship guide it sits alongside) did not show a visible last-updated date in available evidence — freshness of this particular URL is uncertain even though the brand's flagship guide is confirmed to be updated annually. Function is explicitly a guide-and-lead-gen hybrid for Dezan Shira & Associates' advisory services (confirmed dual role, not resolved by this pass), so despite editorial framing it isn't a neutral third party either.
**Why it likely ranks:** Publisher-level authority as an established, multi-country "Doing Business" content brand (Asia Briefing/Dezan Shira network) — this one plausibly is authority/brand-driven rather than purely content-driven, though not Semrush-confirmed.
**What would beat it:** Match the precision on project-office-vs-liaison-office activity rights and the decision-framework close, in a page that also states its own freshness clearly (visible date) and doesn't require the reader to know it's tied to a separate paid-guide product.

### treelife.in — "Indian Subsidiary vs Branch Office — Key Differences, Taxation, Compliance"
**Does well:** Clearly the most decision-useful page reviewed. Uses a worked numeric example (₹10 crore net Indian profit → branch pays ~₹3.82 crore tax vs. a subsidiary under Section 115BAA paying ~₹2.52 crore) instead of stopping at a bare percentage — this is the kind of concrete, apply-it-yourself detail a CFO actually needs and no other page in this set provides. States a specific effective branch tax range tied to an assessment year (AY 2026-27: 36.4%–38.2%). Flags a compliance-burden point almost nobody else raises: using a branch office for pre-revenue activity creates disproportionate compliance obligations (Annual Activity Certificate, DGP reporting, Form FC-3) relative to the actual commercial activity happening — a real, practical warning, not a generic pro/con list. Confirmed via search metadata as recently updated (June 2026), the freshest maintenance signal found across the set.
**Does poorly:** No author byline or named credential surfaced in available evidence despite being a legal/finance advisory firm with real CA/CS expertise to display — a missed trust-signal opportunity even Stage 1's competitor set generally doesn't exploit. Scope stays two-way (branch vs. subsidiary); no evidence of liaison/project-office context that other pages provide.
**Why it likely ranks:** Specificity and currency of the tax and compliance detail (worked example, AY-tagged rate, named compliance forms) is a genuine content-quality differentiator, not just a relevance-match — the strongest evidence in this set that a page can win on substance rather than volume or authority.
**What would beat it:** Match this level of concrete, worked, AY-dated tax detail and the named-compliance-form specificity, add visible authorship/credentials, and extend to the liaison/project-office context this page leaves out.

### kanakkupillai.com — "Wholly Owned Indian Subsidiary Vs Branch Office"
**Does well:** States a specific, useful operational restriction — a branch office is "strictly prohibited" from manufacturing — more decisively than pages that only imply it via an activities list. Gives a setup-timeline estimate (subsidiary ~4–6 weeks vs. branch up to ~8 weeks) as a second, independent data point on timing.
**Does poorly:** Its timeline figures (4–6 vs. up to 8 weeks) directly conflict with setindiabiz's (15–20 business days vs. 30–45 days) for the same claim — neither page appears to cite a source for its number, which is a trust problem visible across the SERP, not just on this page. Tax treatment is described only in relative terms ("significantly higher... than domestic subsidiaries") with no percentage, section reference, or example — thinnest tax treatment of the set reviewed. Sits inside a large "/learn/" content-hub structure characteristic of high-volume SEO content production rather than a single maintained authority piece.
**Why it likely ranks:** High-volume CA/CS registration-platform domain running a broad "/learn/" library — plausibly ranking on breadth of topical coverage across many adjacent queries (site-wide topical relevance) rather than this individual article's depth; not Semrush-confirmed.
**What would beat it:** Cite an actual source for setup-timeline claims (AD-bank/RBI processing benchmarks, not an unsourced range) and replace the relative tax language with the specific rate/section detail this page is missing.

## Cross-Page Patterns

**What most strong pages share:** A consistent category skeleton (legal status/identity → permitted activities → regulatory approval route → taxation → liability/control) that maps reasonably well to the real decision journey, and at least some grounding in named regulation (Companies Act 2013, FEMA, specific FEMA regulation years) rather than vague "Indian law" references. Pages that rank across many query variants (setindiabiz, ebizfiling) tend to run *multiple* competing URLs on the same core comparison rather than one definitive page — volume-of-content substitutes for depth-per-page in several cases.

**What's uniformly weak across all of them:** No page in this set showed clear, visible authorship or named professional credentials in available evidence, despite every one of these being a CA/CS/legal advisory firm with real expertise to put a name to — a genuine, checkable E-E-A-T gap across the category. Setup-timeline claims conflict between competitors with no cited source for either figure. No page in this set was confirmed (via available evidence) to state clearly and accurately where the RBI 2025 draft Establishment Regulations / proposed "Entity Resident Outside India" framework currently stands (still draft, not notified, as of the most recent evidence found this session) — several either omit it or (per Stage 1) reference the reform without the "still draft" qualifier being confirmed present. None of the pages reviewed link out visibly to RBI/FEMA/Invest India primary sources in a way that was surfaced by search snippets — consistent with Stage 1's finding that government sources are cited *in spirit* but the primary documents themselves aren't observed ranking or being visibly linked. Conversion mechanics (branch → subsidiary), Letter of Comfort, and DTAA/repatriation detail appear thin or absent across most of the set, despite being recurring entities Stage 1 flagged as topically expected.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely better AU Corporate page does not need to be longer than these — it needs to close four specific, checkable gaps none of the six pages reviewed close together: (1) state the RBI 2025 draft Establishment Regulations / EROI reform status precisely and correctly (draft, not yet notified, 2016 framework still in force as of the most recent evidence available), rather than treating it as settled or omitting it; (2) replace unsourced setup-timeline and tax-rate claims with figures tied to a specific, named source or assessment year, the way treelife.in does for tax but no page does for timelines; (3) cover the mechanics a real founder or CFO will actually ask about and this set leaves thin — branch-to-subsidiary conversion, DTAA/repatriation treatment, and (only where genuinely relevant, per Stage 2's foreign-framing guidance) the "which parameters should a foreign company actually weigh" decision framework india-briefing gestures at but doesn't fully operationalize; (4) put a real, credentialed author/reviewer on the page — something zero pages in this set were confirmed to do. None of this requires more words; it requires more accountability for the specific claims already being made across the category.
