## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The UIN section is the strongest EEAT signal on the page.** It states a conclusion ("does not appear to be allotted a UIN"), gives the reader the actual reasoning for landing there (a recurring "BO/LO"-only phrasing pattern versus "BO/LO/PO" phrasing elsewhere), and is explicit that this is convergent secondary evidence rather than a primary-text read — in both the body (line 85) and FAQ #6 (line 168), matching Stage 9's own recommended language almost verbatim. This is a page showing its work in a way that reads as an actual practitioner's judgment call, not a confident-sounding guess with a citation bolted on. Very few competitor pages in this space would risk stating a hedge this precisely rather than either flattening it into a false certainty or burying it in a disclaimer.
- **The construction/installation PE section is real technical depth, correctly bounded.** It names the concept, explains why a Project Office carries materially higher PE risk than a Liaison Office, raises profit attribution as "a genuine, recurring point of engagement (and at times negotiation)," and deliberately declines to state a universal day-threshold — with the reasoning for declining spelled out (treaty-specific, and stating one treaty's number as if universal "is the kind of error that causes real disputes with Indian tax authorities"). That's a judgment-call explanation, not a hedge for hedging's sake.
- **The eligibility section corrects a real, named category error in published guidance** — that the BO/LO net-worth/profitability test gets conflated with PO eligibility, when PO eligibility actually turns on the underlying contract. Calling this out by name signals the writer/reviewer actually knows the comparative framework, not just this one entity type in isolation.
- **Internal linking is substantive, not decorative.** The page links into AU Corporate's own established claim on `/services/taxation-regulatory` (that branch/project offices are taxed "differently, and generally less favourably" than an incorporated subsidiary) and extends it specifically for Project Offices, rather than restating it — this is the kind of cross-page consistency that reads as one firm's coherent body of expertise rather than disconnected landing pages.
- **The four-authority coordination framing in the closing section is a real operational insight** (AD bank, ROC/MCA, DGIT-International Taxation, and the PE/profit-attribution assessment as a fourth distinct thread), not a generic "we handle everything" close — it names the actual seams a client would otherwise have to manage themselves.

## Where It Falls Short

- **Hedging discipline has one visible gap: the restricted-country/sensitive-sector list.** Every other genuinely uncertain item on this page (UIN, Regulation 5 numbering, FNC vs FNC-1, the six-month window, FC-2 closure mechanics) carries a reader-facing hedge. The restricted-country/sensitive-sector list (Pakistan/Bangladesh/Sri Lanka/Afghanistan/Iran/China/Hong Kong/Macau; NE states/J&K/Andaman & Nicobar; Defence/Private Security/Telecom/I&B) is stated as flat fact in the body and in FAQ #12, even though Stage 9 explicitly flagged it as carried forward from the sibling pages without independent PO-specific verification and as "genuinely time-sensitive/policy-dependent" (RBI updates sensitive-sector lists periodically). This is a minor inconsistency rather than a factual problem — the list itself isn't contradicted by anything Stage 9 found, and matching the sibling pages' existing (unhedged) treatment is a deliberate consistency choice per the Writer's Notes — but a genuinely careful reviewer should notice that this is the one place where the page's otherwise-excellent "say what we don't know" discipline quietly drops. Recommend either a one-line hedge ("subject to periodic RBI review; confirm current list with your AD bank") or an explicit editorial decision to leave it as-is for cross-page consistency, made consciously rather than by default.
- **The page is dense enough that a first-time reader could lose the thread on why the UIN point matters at all.** It's stated correctly and carefully, but nothing in the surrounding prose tells the reader *why* they should care whether a UIN is issued (i.e., what practical consequence follows from a PO not having one — e.g., whether it affects contract execution, bank account opening, or subsequent filings). As written, it reads as a fact for its own sake rather than a decision-relevant fact. This doesn't undermine trust, but it's a missed opportunity to make the page's most carefully-sourced section also its most obviously useful one.
- **Nothing else reads as generic or AI-flattened.** No stock phrases, no unearned superlatives, no vague "our experts" language standing in for named mechanics. This page clears the bar the checklist is most worried about (reading as generic AI content with a logo on it) comfortably.
