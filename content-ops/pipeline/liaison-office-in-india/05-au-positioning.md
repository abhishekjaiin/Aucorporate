# AU Corporate Positioning: Liaison Office in India

## Relevant Service Lines for This Page

- **FEMA & RBI Compliance.** This is not an add-on for this page — it *is* the page. Everything from Form FNC filing through the AD Category-I bank, to eligibility (net worth/profit track record), to the Annual Activity Certificate (AAC), to closure (Form FC-2, Section 380(3)) sits squarely inside FEMA & RBI Compliance. This should read as the page's structural backbone, not a bolted-on service mention.
- **Compliance & Regulatory Services.** The recurring, multi-authority compliance calendar an LO carries once established (AAC to the AD bank + DGIT by 30 September, renewal of the 3-year/2-year validity cycle, the eventual FC-2 closure) is a genuine, ongoing engagement — distinct from FEMA & RBI Compliance's one-time approval work, and worth naming as its own thread in the "how AU supports" section, mirroring how the Branch Office page separates the AAC/validity section from the initial approval section.
- **International Tax / Corporate Tax (light touch).** The PE-risk section (Section 9(1)(i), DTAA Article 5) is squarely tax-technical and is the single biggest differentiation opportunity Stage 4 identified. It belongs on this page because it directly answers a real, independently-searched question ("Is a liaison office a PE for tax purposes?") that every analyzed competitor left unaddressed — but it should stay a well-sourced explainer section, not a pitch for a broader tax engagement. AU Corporate's existing `/services/taxation-regulatory` page already carries "International Taxation Litigation & Advisory" and "DTAA Advisory & Compliance" as named service lines, so this section has a real, live capability behind it — it is not invented for this page.
- **GST Advisory & Compliance (light touch).** Same treatment as PE risk: a brief, accurate "form vs substance" callout (an LO doesn't register for GST by default because it isn't supplying anything, but drift into taxable-supply territory changes that) closes a confirmed content gap and connects to a real, live AU service (`/services/taxation-regulatory` already runs a full GST practice, including the RCM-on-import-of-services point that's directly relevant if an LO's cost-reimbursement arrangement with its parent is ever mischaracterised). Keep this to a short paragraph, not a GST-compliance-calendar walkthrough — that belongs on the GST service page, not here.
- **India Entry Strategy.** The LO vs Branch Office vs Project Office vs Wholly Owned Subsidiary decision section (scoped narrow, per the keyword map's Comparison Scope Note) is a genuine India Entry Strategy function — helping a reader self-select the right vehicle before they commit to the FNC filing. This should stay a brief "at a glance" framing section, matching the Branch Office page's `structureComparison` treatment almost exactly, not a second full comparison pillar.

## Service Lines Deliberately Excluded

- **Company Incorporation / Business Setup (as a framed service).** An LO is explicitly *not* an incorporation — it has no separate legal identity, no MOA/AOA, no ROC-issued Certificate of Incorporation. Framing this page around "company setup" or "business setup" services would misstate what an LO actually is and contradicts the very first thing the page needs to establish (a representative presence, not a company). Where MCA/ROC involvement is real (Form FC-1 foreign-company registration), name it precisely as that — a registration, not an incorporation — rather than reaching for the Company Incorporation service framing.
- **Virtual CFO and Payroll.** An LO cannot generate revenue and typically runs a small coordination/liaison headcount. Pitching Virtual CFO (a service built around running finance operations for a trading entity) or a dedicated Payroll service onto this page would misread the reader — someone choosing an LO has explicitly chosen the lightest-touch, most restricted structure precisely because they don't yet need that operational depth. A single factual line on headcount (LO can hire local staff for liaison/coordination activity, subject to standard labour-law obligations) is appropriate; a service pitch around managing that headcount is not.
- **FDI Advisory.** An LO involves no equity capital and no FDI route — there are no shares to subscribe to, so FEMA 20(R)'s Automatic/Government route framework (which is what FDI Advisory actually addresses) doesn't apply here at all. The Branch Office page already draws this exact distinction explicitly (FEMA 22(R) establishment approval vs FEMA 20(R) equity-investment approval are "different mechanisms," not the same routing question under different names) — this page should carry the same distinction for consistency, and should not use "FDI route" framing anywhere in the LO context. Readers evaluating actual equity investment belong on `/india-business-setup/fdi-channels`, which this page can link to for that different question, not absorb.
- **Business Valuation and Transaction Advisory.** No plausible reader researching "liaison office in India" is at the valuation or deal-structuring stage — these belong nowhere near this page.
- **Audit & Assurance (as a distinct service pitch).** The AAC is CA-certified, but it is a FEMA/RBI compliance certificate, not a Companies Act statutory audit — an LO has no share capital or board in the Companies Act sense to audit. Naming "Audit & Assurance" as a service here would overstate what's actually happening; keep the AAC framed as FEMA/RBI compliance work (see above), not audit work.
- **Risk Advisory / Internal Controls.** These serve a materially bigger, more operationally complex entity than an LO ever is by design. Forcing them in here would read as generic cross-selling rather than something this specific reader needs.
- **Transfer Pricing (as a named dedicated section).** Unlike a Branch Office — which is genuinely revenue-generating and therefore has real related-party transactions with the parent that TP rules bite on — an LO by definition has no commercial dealings, only expense funding from the parent. TP exposure only becomes live if the LO's actual activity drifts into something commercial (at which point it's really a PE-risk problem, not a standalone TP problem). Don't give TP its own section; a single clause inside the PE-risk discussion, if it comes up naturally, is enough.

## International-Investor Considerations Relevant Here

- **Indian entity structures** — yes, via the brief LO vs BO vs PO vs WOS decision section (narrow, at-a-glance, not a rebuilt comparison pillar — link out to the Branch Office page and the three country-specific Subsidiary vs Branch Office pages for deeper comparison rather than duplicating them).
- **FDI route** — explicitly *not* relevant to LO establishment itself, and the page should say so plainly (no share capital, no FEMA 20(R) route), the same way the Branch Office page distinguishes FEMA 22(R) from the FDI equity route. Do not use "FDI route" language to describe how an LO is approved.
- **FEMA/RBI angle** — central to the entire page: FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB as the statutory basis, AD Category-I bank as routing authority, Automatic vs Government route distinction (tied to sector and restricted-country status, not a flat process), Form FNC.
- **Tax treatment** — narrow and specific, not a full corporate-tax walkthrough (there's no corporate tax computation to walk through since an LO can't earn income): PE risk under Section 9(1)(i)/DTAA Article 5 is the one tax topic that genuinely belongs here, treated as a boundary-of-activity question, not a tax-planning pitch.
- **GST** — one short, accurate "not required by default, but here's when it could be" paragraph. Not a GST compliance calendar.
- **Accounting/compliance obligations** — yes, central: AAC (who it's filed with, when, what it certifies), the 3-year/2-year validity/renewal cycle (with the pending, unnotified 2025 draft flagged the same way the Branch Office page flags it), and closure (Form FC-2, Section 380(3)).
- **Post-establishment requirements** (reframed from "post-incorporation," since an LO isn't incorporated) — Form FC-1 registration with ROC within 30 days of RBI approval, UIN allotment, and the ongoing AAC cycle.
- **Practical India-entry considerations** — the route-dependent timeline range (not a flat number), restricted-country/government-route handling, the Nepal-only-LO rule, and the J&K/North-East/Andaman & Nicobar police-registration point. These are narrow but genuinely citable and match Stage 4's differentiation list — include them as a compact reference block, not scattered throughout.

## AU Corporate's Right-to-Win Angle

1. **Honest hedging over false precision, carried over from the Branch Office page's precedent.** The single most checkable gap Stage 4 found across every analyzed competitor is a flat, unqualified timeline figure. The Branch Office page already set AU Corporate's house style for this (explicitly declining to state a fixed Branch Office renewal period it couldn't verify against a primary source, and explaining *why* timelines vary by route rather than asserting a number). This page should apply the identical discipline to the LO timeline question — state the route-dependent reasoning, not a cherry-picked single figure.
2. **Multi-authority coordination framed accurately, not vaguely.** An LO application/lifecycle genuinely touches RBI (via the AD bank), MCA/ROC (Form FC-1, later Form FC-2), and the DGIT (AAC routing) — four distinct authorities with no shared filing system. AU Corporate's actual, demonstrated depth here (visible in how precisely the Branch Office page names each form, section number, and authority) is a real right-to-win: generic competitor pages blur "register with the government" into one step; this page can name exactly who does what, when, in what order — the same execution standard as its sibling page.
3. **Consistency with AU Corporate's own existing answer on LO-to-subsidiary "conversion."** `/doing-business-in-india/incorporation` already has a live FAQ answering "Should we set up a liaison office first and convert to a subsidiary later?" with a specific, non-generic position: an LO doesn't literally convert — there is no legal conversion mechanism — it must be wound down and a subsidiary separately incorporated, and an LO is a better fit for genuinely time-bound research/coordination than as a staging step toward a subsidiary you already expect to need. This page's FAQ on the same question must repeat that exact position, not a vaguer or contradictory one. Getting this right (and matching AU's own existing public position) is itself a differentiator, since Stage 4 found this question unaddressed by any analyzed competitor.
4. **Compliance-boundary-first framing, not benefits-first framing.** Stage 4 flagged vjmglobal as leading with generic setup benefits ("low cost," "local hiring") ahead of the activity restriction that actually matters to a risk-aware reader. AU Corporate's version should lead the activity section with the boundary (what triggers PE exposure, what triggers GST registration) before or alongside the permitted-activity list — this is a framing choice available to any writer, but it only reads as credible if it's paired with the specific statutory citations (Section 9(1)(i), DTAA Article 5) rather than a generic caution.
5. **Genuine primary-source citation depth.** Naming Form FNC by its Annex B/Annex-1 reference and FED Master Direction No. 10/2015-16, citing FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB directly, and citing the 2025 draft by its actual title, publication date (3 October 2025) and comment deadline (24 October 2025) — clearly labeled as not notified as of the page's publish date — is achievable, low-cost, and matches precisely what the Branch Office page already did for the same draft. This is a repeatable AU Corporate execution standard, not a one-off.

## Explicit Guidance for the Writer

- **Match the Branch Office page's register exactly:** plainspoken, precise, form-and-section-number literate, comfortable saying "we could not confirm X against a primary source, so here is the honest answer" rather than repeating an unverified figure. These two pages will be read as a matched pair — divergent tone between them (e.g., one hedging carefully, the other overclaiming) would undermine both.
- **Lead the activity-restriction section with the boundary, not the benefits.** Don't open with "low-cost way to test the market" language before establishing exactly where permitted activity ends and PE/GST exposure begins — that ordering is itself part of the differentiation, per Stage 4.
- **Keep the PE-risk and GST sections short, cited, and boundary-focused.** A worked example (e.g., contrasting "relaying a customer inquiry to the parent" with "negotiating contract terms on the parent's behalf") is good and grounded in the gap analysis — a general tax-advisory pitch is not. These sections exist to answer a real, independently-searched question accurately, not to sell a tax engagement.
- **Do not use "FDI" language to describe how an LO gets approved.** LO establishment runs under FEMA 22(R) via the AD bank/RBI, not the FDI equity route under FEMA 20(R) — conflating the two (a mistake the Branch Office page specifically corrects) would be a real accuracy regression on the sibling page.
- **The "How AU Corporate Supports" section should stay proportional and specific** — mirroring the Branch Office page's approach: name the actual authorities/filings AU coordinates (AD bank/Form FNC, ROC/Form FC-1, AAC's dual routing to the AD bank and DGIT, and eventual FC-2 closure), not a generic "we help with compliance" statement. One paragraph, not a features list, and it should come near the end of the page (after the substantive content), exactly where the Branch Office page places it — not woven earlier into the how-to sections.
- **Do not introduce Virtual CFO, Payroll, Business Valuation, Transaction Advisory, Risk Advisory, or Internal Controls anywhere on this page.** If a reviewer sees any of these mentioned, that's a signal the page has drifted from "answer this reader's question" toward "cross-sell the full service catalogue" — flag it for removal.
- **Match, don't merely avoid contradicting, the existing "convert to subsidiary" FAQ answer** on `/doing-business-in-india/incorporation`: an LO cannot be legally converted — it must be wound down and a subsidiary separately incorporated — and an LO fits genuinely time-bound research/coordination needs better than it fits as a deliberate staging step toward a subsidiary you already expect to need.
- **Avoid advertising language generally:** no "industry-leading," "trusted partner," "one-stop-shop," "seamless," or similar. The credibility case for AU Corporate on this page is made entirely through the accuracy and specificity of the regulatory content itself (naming forms, sections, authorities, dates correctly) plus one clearly-scoped support paragraph near the end — not through adjectives.
