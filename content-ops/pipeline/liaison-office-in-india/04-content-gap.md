## Minimum Coverage List
(What the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes. Every item here is either explicitly confirmed on multiple analyzed pages, or is a structural fact so basic to the entity type that its absence would read as an incomplete page.)
1. Clear definition of a liaison office and its legal basis (FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB).
2. RBI approval requirement and the role of the AD Category-I Bank as the routing authority to RBI's Foreign Exchange Department.
3. Form FNC application process (Annex B/Annex-1, FED Master Direction 10/2015-16).
4. Eligibility criteria: net worth ≥ USD 50,000 and a 3-year profit track record for the parent entity — stated as a specific number, not a vague reference to "financial soundness" (the gap ahlawatassociates leaves open).
5. Permitted activities (market research, liaison/coordination, representation) and prohibited activities (no income-generating/commercial activity) — stated together, not just one side.
6. Validity/renewal cycle: 3 years generally, 2 years for NBFC and construction-development-sector entities — presented as current, firm law, with a clearly dated footnote on the pending (not notified) 2025 draft that proposes eliminating this cycle.
7. Post-approval MCA/ROC registration via Form FC-1 within 30 days of RBI approval, and allocation of the UIN.
8. Annual Activity Certificate (AAC) reporting obligation — filed with the AD bank and DGIT, New Delhi, by September 30 for the FY ending March 31.
9. Closure process (Form FC-2, Section 380(3) Companies Act 2013) — even a brief, accurate treatment beats what appears to be thin-to-absent coverage across the analyzed set.

## Differentiation List
(What almost nobody covers well, or covers at all, across the five analyzed pages — the real opportunity. Every item below traces to a specific gap Stage 3 observed, not a generic "add more detail" instinct.)

1. **Route-dependent RBI/AD-bank timeline.** No analyzed competitor explains that the wide range of timeline figures circulating (3-4 weeks to 20-24 weeks) exists *because* of route (RBI/automatic route vs. Government route for sensitive sectors or flagged-country applicants) and documentation completeness — each instead states a single flat number. A page that presents this as a clearly-reasoned range, tied explicitly to route, closes a checkable accuracy gap every reviewed competitor has.
2. **PE (Permanent Establishment) risk.** Confirmed absent from all five analyzed pages despite independent, standalone search demand (TaxGuru ranks separately for this exact question). A brief, well-sourced section on when an LO's on-ground activity risks crossing into PE territory is currently owned only by a non-competing publisher — open ground for a service-selling page.
3. **GST registration in limited circumstances.** Same pattern as PE risk: confirmed absent from all five, despite independent search demand. The "form vs. substance" framing (GST not required for a compliant LO, but triggered if actual activity strays into taxable-supply territory) is a natural pairing with the PE-risk section.
4. **Compliance-boundary-first framing of the permitted-activities section.** vjmglobal (the clearest offender found) leads with generic setup benefits (low cost, local hiring) rather than the activity boundary that actually worries a risk-aware CFO. Leading with "here is exactly where permitted activity ends and taxable/PE exposure begins" is a framing differentiator, not a new topic.
5. **The 2025 draft Establishment Regulations, integrated inline rather than siloed.** india-briefing is the only analyzed page that covers the draft at all, and it does so in a separate 2025 news article disconnected from its core evergreen how-to page — a reader landing on the how-to content gets no signal a change is pending. Integrating current law + draft status in one clearly-dated place, on the same page as the how-to content, beats every analyzed competitor's execution on this point.
6. **Nepal-only-LO rule, Government-route sector list, and restricted-country/J&K-NE-Andaman handling.** None of these narrow-but-genuine, citable facts surfaced in any snippet from any of the five analyzed pages. Low effort, real E-E-A-T signal, and a specific, low-competition long-tail capture opportunity.
7. **A single-page structure that carries compliance depth inline.** treelife.in runs the most sophisticated cluster found (main page + separate compliance deep-dive + separate comparison page), but that is also its structural weakness: a visitor who lands only on the main page and doesn't click through gets thinner treatment than a well-built single page would deliver. Consolidating the full setup-to-closure journey (including AAC mechanics and the LO vs BO vs Subsidiary comparison) on one page is achievable and not currently done by anyone in the analyzed set.
8. **LO vs BO vs Subsidiary comparison folded into the main page, not a separate click.** Only treelife and (per Stage 1) setindiabiz appear to run this as a distinct page; neither integrates it with the core how-to content. A brief, well-built comparison section inline (not a full standalone pillar — see Stage 2's Comparison Scope Note) beats both the "missing entirely" pages and the "requires a second click" pages.

## Potential Unique Angles
(Grounded directly in the gaps above — not invented.)
- A clear route-dependent timeline framework (RBI/automatic route vs. Government route), presented as a reasoned range rather than a single number, with the reasoning stated — the single most checkable, currently-universal gap in the field.
- A short, well-sourced PE-risk explainer paired with a GST-in-limited-circumstances callout, framed around the same "form vs. substance" test, positioned to lead the activity-restriction section rather than trail generic setup benefits.
- A worked example illustrating the PE-risk boundary in practice (e.g., contrasting "relaying customer inquiries to the parent" with "negotiating contract terms on the parent's behalf") — grounded in the specific compliance-boundary gap Stage 3 identified in vjmglobal's benefits-led framing.
- A compact reference block covering the Nepal-only-LO rule, the Government-route sector list, and restricted-country/J&K-NE-Andaman handling — narrow but genuinely differentiating since zero analyzed competitors surface any of it.
- One consolidated page carrying the full setup-to-compliance-to-closure journey (including AAC mechanics and a brief LO vs BO vs Subsidiary comparison section) inline, rather than requiring a click-through cluster — directly answers treelife's structural weakness.
- A single clearly-dated section integrating current law (FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB, 3-yr/2-yr validity) with the pending 2025 draft's specific proposed changes (EROI category, elimination of the validity cycle, elimination of net worth/profit thresholds) — closing the gap left by india-briefing's siloed coverage.

## Opportunities for Authoritative Sourcing
(Where citing MCA/RBI/Income Tax/GST primary sources would beat what's currently ranking.)
- Cite RBI's own FAQ page (rbi.org.in) and FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB directly for eligibility figures, permitted/prohibited activity scope, and validity period, rather than repeating secondary paraphrase the way the analyzed pages appear to — particularly valuable once egress access allows direct verification against RBI's own timeline language (flagged as unresolved by Stage 3).
- Name the exact form and master direction — Form FNC, Annex B/Annex-1 of FED Master Direction No. 10/2015-16 — rather than the vague "the application form" framing several competitors use.
- Ground the PE-risk section in Section 9(1)(i) of the Income-tax Act 1961 and DTAA Article 5, and cite the actual case law TaxGuru references (a Supreme Court ruling on LO/PE exposure) rather than a generic "PE risk exists" statement — no analyzed competitor does this at all, so even a brief, correctly cited treatment is a clear step up.
- Cite the specific MCA mechanism — Form FC-1 for registration, Form FC-2 and Section 380(3) of the Companies Act 2013 for closure/cessation — by name and section number, not just "register with MCA."
- For the 2025 draft, cite RBI's own "Connect 2 Regulate" portal and the draft's actual title ("Foreign Exchange Management (Establishment in India of a Branch or Office) Regulations, 2025") with the October 3, 2025 publication date and October 24, 2025 comment deadline, clearly labeled as not yet notified as of the page's publish/update date — matching the precedent already set on the published Branch Office page, and beating india-briefing's siloed, undated-relative-to-the-main-article treatment.
