## Per-Page Analysis

### india-briefing.com — "Branch Office in India: Regulatory Framework, Eligibility & RBI's 2025 Draft Reforms"
**Does well:** This is the only page in the set confirmed (via WebSearch-surfaced summary) to explicitly date its update — **May 13, 2026** — and to correctly frame the draft 2025 regulations as *not yet notified*, stating the 2016 framework "continues to remain in force as of May 2026." That is a materially accurate, current regulatory position, not an error. The page also covers the draft reforms' substantive shift (principle-based regime, expanded AD-bank delegated powers, move from a prescriptive permitted-activity list toward a negative-list model) with more analytical framing than a simple "here's what changed" bullet list — it reads as genuine regulatory commentary rather than a repackaged checklist. It sits inside india-briefing.com's broader comparative doing-business-guide architecture (the same publisher also ranks a "Representative Office vs Branch Office vs Project Office vs LLP vs Subsidiary" comparison page), suggesting real topical breadth and internal linking across the market-entry-structure decision space, not an isolated orphan page.
**Does poorly:** No author byline or named credential surfaced in any search snippet reviewed — trust signaling appears to rest on the Dezan Shira & Associates masthead rather than a visible individual expert. Because WebFetch could not open the page, sub-question coverage (does it name Form FNC specifically? does it walk through AD-bank routing? does it cover AAC and closure in the same depth as the regulatory-change narrative?) could not be verified — the search-surfaced summary skews heavily toward the "what's changing" regulatory-history angle and did not surface strong process/documents/timeline detail, which may mean that operational depth is thinner than its regulatory-currency framing suggests.
**Why it likely ranks:** Freshness (a May 2026 update on a live regulatory-transition story) combined with an established publisher operating the same "market-entry advisory firm running an educational funnel" model AU Corporate is building — likely benefits from domain-level topical authority across the whole India market-entry cluster (comparison pages, country guides, doing-business hub) rather than this single URL's isolated strength. Cannot verify backlink/DA weighting given Semrush's `no_api_units` failure.
**What would beat it:** Match or exceed its regulatory-currency accuracy (correctly labeling the draft as proposed, not current law) while going deeper than it does into the operational mechanics — Form FNC, AD-bank routing specifics, AAC filing mechanics, closure steps — in the same page, and add a named, credentialed author/reviewer that this page appears to lack.

### setindiabiz.com — "Branch Office Registration in India | RBI"
**Does well:** Strong procedural specificity: cites FEMA 22(R)/2016 **Regulation 4** by number (not just the regulation name), names a concrete net-worth threshold (USD 100,000) tied to a profit-track-record requirement across the preceding five financial years, and breaks the process into named document-collation and Form-FNC-drafting turnaround windows rather than a single vague "we handle everything" claim. It also maintains a companion blog post specifically on FEMA 22(R)/2016 as a standalone regulatory explainer and a separate "permitted activities" deep-dive — evidence of intentional sub-topic content architecture (main service page + supporting explainer posts) rather than one page trying to cover everything shallowly.
**Does poorly:** The process description surfaced skews toward internal turnaround times ("we complete document collation in 5–7 days") rather than the regulator-side timeline a founder actually needs to plan around (AD-bank review time, RBI CoC decision time) — this frames the page around the seller's service delivery rather than fully around the reader's planning question. No FAQ content, authorship, or "last updated" signal surfaced in search results, so genuine freshness/trust-signal strength versus setindiabiz's other pages could not be confirmed.
**Why it likely ranks:** Precise, regulation-numbered specificity (citing Reg. 4, the exact net-worth figure, the exact document list) signals genuine topical authority to both users and, plausibly, to Google's relevance matching for this exact commercial-investigation query — this is a case where the content itself, not just domain reputation, appears to be doing real work.
**What would beat it:** Reframe the timeline around the regulator's actual decision windows (AD bank review period, RBI CoC turnaround) rather than the vendor's internal processing time, and pair the same regulation-numbered specificity with a visible author/reviewer credential and explicit currency statement (FEMA 22(R)/2016 confirmed current, draft 2025 regs flagged as pending) that this page does not appear to include.

### vjmglobal.com — "Branch Office Registration In India | Process And Compliances" + RBI-approval blog
**Does well:** Clearly explains the Automatic-vs-Approval-route mechanism in plain decision-rule terms (principal business under 100%-automatic-FDI sectors → Automatic route; otherwise → Approval route) rather than just naming the two routes without explaining what determines which one applies — this actually answers the underlying "which route am I on" question a founder has, not just the vocabulary. It also names a concrete AD-bank processing ceiling (5 working days to approve/reject/forward) and a total RBI timeline estimate (4–6 weeks), and surfaces a genuinely non-obvious edge case in its FAQ layer: that an Indian subsidiary of a foreign company *cannot* itself open a BO under the automatic route, and that Project Offices don't require a RBI-issued UIN the way BOs/LOs do — specific, correct-sounding distinctions that go beyond generic definitional content.
**Does poorly:** Split across a service page and a separate blog post means the FAQ/edge-case depth (the subsidiary-can't-open-a-BO nuance, the PO/UIN distinction) is not necessarily sitting where the transactional page itself scores strongest — a reader converting on the service page may not encounter the more nuanced blog content unless internal linking is strong, which was not verifiable via search snippets alone. No authorship/date/citation-to-primary-source detail surfaced.
**Why it likely ranks:** The Automatic/Approval route explanation and the specific procedural edge cases (subsidiary restriction, PO/UIN exception) are the kind of correct, non-generic detail that differentiates a page from templated competitors — this looks like genuine expertise-driven content depth on the CA-firm-blog end of the spectrum, likely reinforced by the firm's broader India market-entry service-page cluster (liaison office, business setup) rather than by outsized backlink authority.
**What would beat it:** Consolidate the route-determination logic, the AD-bank/RBI timeline, and the specific edge cases (subsidiary restriction, PO/UIN exception, and equivalents) into one page rather than splitting service-page and blog-post content, and add the regulatory citation (FEMA 22(R)/2016, specific regulation numbers) that gives this kind of procedural claim verifiable weight.

### companiesnext.com — "Branch Office Registration in India - Process, Documents"
**Does well:** Runs a genuinely broad topic-cluster architecture around this single entity type — separate ranking URLs for the BO service page, a "process to incorporate" blog post, a dedicated compliance-requirements post, and parallel Liaison Office and Project Office registration pages — giving it real comparative depth across the three entity types rather than treating BO in isolation. Names Form FC-1 and the 30-day ROC filing deadline specifically, and correctly ties the Annual Activity Certificate to a March 31 fiscal-year-end filing routed to both the AD bank and the Directorate General of Income Tax (International Taxation) — a specific, correct compliance-routing detail many competitors likely simplify away.
**Does poorly:** The content surfaced is almost entirely procedural/compliance-listing in character (documents, deadlines, filing routing) with no visible evidence of deeper "why/what does this mean for a founder" framing — no visible discussion of tax treatment, permanent-establishment implications, or the practical decision of BO vs. subsidiary vs. LO beyond what the parallel PO/LO pages imply by existing. No authorship, credential, or update-date signal surfaced.
**Why it likely ranks:** Breadth across the full entity-type cluster (BO + LO + PO as separate, cross-linkable pages) plausibly earns this domain topical relevance across the whole query space, not just this one URL — a page-cluster strength rather than a single-page depth strength.

**What would beat it:** Match its specific compliance-routing accuracy (AAC to AD bank + DGIT International Taxation, FC-1's 30-day window) while adding the decision-support layer it appears to lack — why a founder would choose BO over LO/PO/subsidiary given their specific business model, not just parallel registration pages for each.

### taxguru.in — "Everything about Branch Office/ Project Office/ Liaison Office by Foreign Company in India" (article cluster)
**Does well:** This is the only page in the set with a clearly named, individually credentialed author (a named ICAI member, per the search-surfaced byline), and it sits inside a much larger cluster of independently authored articles on the same topic by different practitioners (a CS-firm partner, an FCA practicing this exact service area, an ACS at a named CA firm) — genuine multi-author, practitioner-credentialed depth rather than one firm's marketing content. Because it's a non-commercial knowledge-community publisher, the content reads as motivated by comprehensiveness and professional reputation rather than lead generation, which likely produces less templated, more genuinely explanatory prose than a service-page competitor optimizing for a CTA.
**Does poorly:** No commercial CTA or service path is a strength for trust but a structural weakness for AU Corporate's purposes — it's optimized to be cited/read, not to convert, so it likely doesn't attempt to guide a reader toward a next decision (engage an advisor, compare options) the way a well-built commercial-investigation page should. Being an aggregator of many separately authored articles rather than one maintained, canonical page also means currency is uneven — some of the article cluster (older establishment-guide titles) is plausibly stale relative to the actively-dated 2025/2026 regulatory-change story, though this could not be individually date-verified per article via WebFetch.
**Why it likely ranks:** Named-author, practitioner-credentialed trust signals combined with topic-community domain authority (taxguru.in ranks repeatedly across many tax/FEMA/company-law queries, not just this one) — likely a domain-level authority effect layered on genuinely well-written individual articles, not article-level SEO tactics.
**What would beat it:** Bring the same named-practitioner credibility signal (a real author/reviewer with stated qualifications) into a page that, unlike taxguru's article, also does the commercial-investigation job properly — giving the reader a clear, non-pushy next step once their question is answered, and keeping the regulatory content demonstrably current in one place rather than spread across an aging article cluster.

## Cross-Page Patterns

**What most strong pages share:**
- Specific regulatory citation with real numbers attached (FEMA 22(R)/2016 by name and, on the stronger pages, by regulation number; USD 100,000 net-worth threshold; five-year profit track record; Form FNC; Form FC-1 and its 30-day window) rather than vague "RBI approval is required" framing. The pages that name specifics read as more authoritative than the ones that don't.
- Cluster architecture: nearly every strong domain (india-briefing.com, vjmglobal.com, companiesnext.com, taxguru.in) covers BO alongside LO and PO as parallel or comparative content, rather than treating Branch Office as a fully isolated topic — this appears to reinforce topical relevance for the whole entity-type decision space.
- None of the five pages checked in this stage were found treating the draft 2025 Establishment Regulations as already-notified current law — this is worth noting explicitly as a **non-finding**: no confirmed factual error to differentiate against was located. Any claim to the contrary should not be asserted without direct page verification, which this environment could not perform.

**What's uniformly weak across all of them (based on available evidence):**
- No page in this set surfaced visible authorship/credential/last-updated metadata *on the transactional service pages themselves* — only taxguru.in (a non-commercial publisher) showed a clear named, credentialed author. The commercial competitors' trust signaling appears to rest on firm branding, not individual named expertise or visible currency dating.
- No page surfaced clear treatment of the practical "why choose BO over LO, PO, or a subsidiary" decision framework in the same breath as the registration process — the cluster pattern (separate BO/LO/PO pages) answers "how do I register a BO" well but doesn't obviously help a founder decide *whether* BO is the right structure for their situation, which is arguably the higher-value question for someone still in commercial-investigation mode.
- No page surfaced explicit, reader-facing acknowledgment of the BO-vs-LO validity/renewal inconsistency Stage 1 flagged (whether BOs specifically require 3-year extension the way LOs clearly do) — this remains genuinely unverified against a primary RBI source in this pipeline (WebFetch to rbi.org.in was also blocked in this stage) and should not be asserted as resolved by any downstream stage without independent primary-source confirmation.
- Tax-treatment depth (effective rate, PE characterization, transfer pricing, DTAA interaction) was largely absent from what search snippets surfaced for the registration-focused service pages — it appears siloed into separate tax-specific content (PwC's reference tool, generic foreign-company-taxation blog posts) rather than integrated into the main BO-registration page a founder would actually land on.
- Restricted-country/government-consultation nuance (Pakistan, China, Bangladesh, border-state and sensitive-sector scrutiny) was named in Stage 1's research as a recurring PAA-style question but did not surface as a dedicated, well-developed section on any of the five pages checked here — it appears to be a named-but-underdeveloped topic across the set.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely better AU Corporate page needs to combine three things no single competitor page in this set was confirmed to have together: (1) regulation-numbered procedural specificity (FEMA 22(R)/2016 cited with actual regulation/section numbers, exact net-worth and track-record thresholds, Form FNC and Form FC-1 with their actual filing windows) matched against a clearly labeled, explicitly-dated statement of current-vs-proposed regulatory status (2016 rules operative; 2025 draft named and described but never presented as current law) — this is the single most differentiating trust signal available given the live regulatory transition; (2) a visible, named author/reviewer credential on the commercial page itself, which none of the CA-firm/platform competitors in this set were confirmed to show; and (3) content that actually helps a founder *decide*, not just *register* — a real treatment of why/when a Branch Office beats a Liaison Office, Project Office, or subsidiary for a given business model, integrated tax-treatment context (not siloed to a separate tax page), a properly developed restricted-country/sensitive-sector subsection, and an honest, explicitly-flagged treatment of the BO-vs-LO validity/renewal question rather than silently picking one version of an unresolved fact. None of this requires being longer than the competition — it requires closing specific, identifiable gaps the strongest current pages leave open.

**Directive for Stage 9 (fact-authority-checker):** this is a MUST-VERIFY item before publish, not a should-improve.
