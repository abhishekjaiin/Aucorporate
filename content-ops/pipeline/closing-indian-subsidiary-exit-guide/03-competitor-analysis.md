## Per-Page Analysis

### treelife.in — "Winding up a Wholly Owned Subsidiary in India" (and the companion "How to close an Indian subsidiary" piece)
**Does well:** Explicitly scopes itself to the foreign-parent-owned WOS scenario rather than a generic company-closure explainer. States concrete strike-off eligibility conditions (no operations for two years, nil/dormant bank balance, no unsettled bank loans — and specifically flags that an unsettled loan will draw a bank objection during the ROC gazette-notice window and sink the application). Gives differentiated timelines by route (strike-off ~3-5 months; voluntary liquidation for a solvent WOS ~6-12 months) and names the specific factors that blow either timeline out to 18-24 months (FEMA compliance corrections, tax disputes, labour matters) rather than quoting one flat number. Covers the closure-side FEMA reporting chain in real terms: FC-GPR reporting reversal on closure, dividend/surplus remittance via AD bank under the automatic route gated on Form 15CA/15CB.
**Does poorly:** Based on available snippets, the decision framing reads more like "here are the eligibility gates for each route" than a genuine decision framework that walks a reader through the actual judgment calls a real CFO faces (e.g., what to do when the subsidiary is *nearly* but not fully debt-free, or when the parent wants speed over formality despite some open contracts). No snippet evidence found of the page citing RBI Master Direction 13/2015-16 by name or walking its specific mechanics (the liquidator's/auditor's certificate content, what the AD bank checks before releasing funds, the distinction between a capital-account remittance and a current-account dividend remittance) — the FEMA content found is closure-reporting-focused (FC-GPR reversal, 15CA/15CB) rather than a deep treatment of the remittance-of-assets regulatory mechanism itself. Critically, Treelife's own site has a separate, unconnected article on Form 5471/Section 482 compliance scoping for US-parent SaaS companies — but nothing in the winding-up guide itself references it, meaning a US-parent reader has to find and cross-reference a second, unrelated Treelife article to get any US-tax-side context, and even that article explicitly says Form 5471/GILTI work "sits entirely with the US CPA and is outside any India retainer" — i.e. Treelife deliberately scopes the US-side tax question out rather than addressing it.
**Why it likely ranks:** Treelife has built a genuine multi-URL content cluster (4-5 distinct pages) specifically on this topic with real specificity (named sections, named forms, differentiated timelines by scenario) rather than one generic explainer — this is topical depth and apparent regulatory currency, which for an evolving compliance topic is a stronger ranking signal than a single comprehensive page would be. No backlink/domain-authority data was obtainable (Semrush blocked) to confirm whether this is content-driven or authority-driven; the cluster-depth pattern itself is consistent with a genuine content-investment strategy rather than pure authority carry-over.
**What would beat it:** A page that resolves the strike-off-vs-liquidation choice with an actual decision framework (not just eligibility gates — the judgment calls at the margins), that names and actually explains RBI Master Direction 13/2015-16's remittance mechanics in the body (not just the downstream reporting forms), and that integrates the US-side tax consequence (Form 5471 final-year box, deconsolidation, Section 482 close-out considerations) into the *same* page a US CFO is reading — rather than requiring them to separately discover and cross-reference an unconnected article, or be told outright that this is "out of scope."

### ODI Part IV — verification finding (applies across the topic, not one page)
This stage's targeted verification **confirms Stage 1's suspicion that "ODI Part IV" is a mis-citation/conflation when applied to this topic.** ODI (Overseas Direct Investment) Part IV is the disinvestment-reporting form filed under RBI's Master Directions on Overseas Direct Investment when an **Indian resident entity** winds up **its own investment in a foreign subsidiary** (the outbound direction) — this is confirmed directly by indialawoffices.com's own "Process of Closing an Overseas Subsidiary by Indian Company" article, which is unambiguously about that outbound scenario and is where the ODI Part IV / UIN / AD Category-I bank language actually lives. It has no application to the inbound scenario this page targets (a US/foreign parent closing its **Indian** subsidiary, funded via FDI/equity, not ODI). **Recommend Stage 4/6 explicitly exclude ODI Part IV from the content brief** — and optionally use the AU page itself as an opportunity to correct this circulating confusion (a short, precise note distinguishing "closing your Indian subsidiary" FDI/FEMA inbound rules from "ODI Part IV" outbound-investment rules would be a genuine, differentiated trust signal, since at least one competitor's own site structure appears to invite exactly this confusion).

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely better AU Corporate page needs to do four specific things none of the pages assessed this stage do together on one page: (1) resolve the strike-off-vs-voluntary-liquidation choice as an actual decision framework tied to the reader's real situation (dormancy status, outstanding obligations, timeline pressure, risk tolerance for post-closure liability exposure) — not just restate each route's eligibility gate in isolation; (2) explain RBI Master Direction 13/2015-16's remittance-of-assets mechanics with real procedural depth — what the liquidator's/auditor's certificate must show, what the AD bank actually checks before releasing funds, and how this differs for a strike-off's simpler surplus-distribution path versus a formal liquidation's court/IBBI-order-backed repatriation — since this is thin or absent across every competitor page checked; (3) integrate the US-parent tax consequence (Form 5471 final-year reporting and deconsolidation, Section 482/transfer-pricing close-out considerations) directly into the closure narrative itself, as the natural "what this means for you" continuation of the India-side process — not as a separate disclaimed-away article, which is the one place a competitor has even gestured at this and explicitly declined to own it; (4) stay precise about direction and scope in a way indialawoffices.com's own site structure fails to be — explicitly distinguish this page's inbound (foreign parent closing an Indian subsidiary, FDI/FEMA-inbound rules) framing from the outbound ODI/Part-IV scenario, both to avoid the mis-citation Stage 1 flagged and as a genuine trust-building clarification competitors don't make. None of this requires more length for its own sake — it requires closing specific, verified gaps that recur across every strong page checked this stage, using AU's own already-live figures (Section 248, IBBI voluntary liquidation regs, the 2-6 month branch-closure timeline, Form 15CA/15CB) as the foundation rather than re-deriving them.
