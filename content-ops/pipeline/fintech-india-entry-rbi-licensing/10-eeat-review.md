- **The NOF table's category-and-phase precision**, including the corrected NBFC-MFI/NBFC-Factor row with its distinct ₹5cr→₹7cr→₹10cr path (North-East carve-outs included) — this is exactly the kind of detail Stage 3/4 confirmed zero competitors get right, and it now reads as confirmed-correct per Stage 9's re-verification, not merely asserted.
- **The five-portal map (PRAVAAH/DAKSH/COSMOS/CIMS/FIRMS)** with a genuinely distinct function for each and a one-line "practical mapping" summary — this is the kind of detail that only shows up when someone has actually tracked which filing goes where, not summarized a single source.

- **Deliberate restraint**: declining to state a single-source, since-verified-stale escrow deadline (Writer's Notes item 7), declining to force a precise PA timeline (FAQ), and explicitly not building GST/Valuation/Payroll/Risk-Advisory sections per Stage 5's positioning guidance — all read as professional judgment about what belongs, not gap-filling.

## Where It Falls Short

**1. Three sentences read as leaked internal pipeline notes, not reader-facing hedges.** These need to be rewritten before publish:
- NOF section (para after the table): "...it's worth confirming with your advisor as part of your capital-timing plan; **flagged for fact-check verification below rather than stated here as settled.**"
- PA net worth section: "...it's worth confirming this specific circular reference and the ₹15cr/₹25cr figures themselves are still current under that consolidated Master Direction rather than an artifact of an earlier framework — **flagged below for fact-check verification.**"
- Digital Lending Directions section: "Both figures are worth confirming against the current Directions text before treating them as fixed — **flagged below.**"

On the live page, "below" refers to nothing — the Writer's Notes appendix is a pipeline artifact, not published page content. A real visitor hits a dangling reference that reads like unfinished draft scaffolding, which is a worse trust signal than the thing it's trying to avoid (false confidence). The fix is mechanical and fast: restate each in the same first-person, reader-facing hedge voice the page already uses well elsewhere (e.g., the FAQ's "we'd rather flag that than invent false precision," or the NOF section's own "note on precision here rather than false confidence" two paragraphs earlier). This is not a factual problem — it's a register problem, and it's the single most important fix before this page goes live, because it's the kind of thing that makes an otherwise-authoritative page suddenly look like an AI draft someone forgot to finish editing.

**2. Attribution is generic where the page's own stakes argue for something more specific.** "Prepared by AU Corporate's FEMA & RBI compliance and cross-border tax practice" is honest but anonymous. Given the fact-check's explicit conclusion that a qualified professional must sign off before publish, the natural and low-cost improvement is to make that sign-off visible: name the reviewing professional and their credential (e.g., "Reviewed by [Name], CA — FEMA & RBI Compliance" or similar, matching whatever attribution pattern AU uses elsewhere, if any exists) rather than leaving the practice-level attribution as the only credit. This does double duty: it's a stronger EEAT signal for a YMYL-adjacent regulatory page, and it operationalizes the standing sign-off requirement rather than leaving it as an internal-only pipeline artifact the reader never sees evidence of.

**3. Minor: DPDPA is not named despite the data-localization rule being DPDPA-adjacent** (flagged by Stage 8, not resolved by Stage 9 either way). Not a trust problem as written — the rule is stated correctly as an RBI Digital Lending Directions requirement — but naming DPDPA 2023 explicitly where relevant would read as slightly more precise to a sophisticated reader (e.g., in-house counsel) without any real cost. Optional, not blocking.

## On the "Last Updated" Date vs. the Already-Passed PA Transition Deadlines

This does not read as a false or misleading currency claim, but it is a genuine tightening opportunity, and worth being precise about why. The Post-License section describes escrow arrangements, KYC certification, and IS-audit expectations in generic, present-tense, steady-state terms ("customer funds generally need to sit in an RBI-compliant escrow arrangement... not a general corporate account") without asserting that a transition is currently in progress or already closed. Because the language is deliberately generic rather than dated, nothing on the page is actually contradicted by the fact (confirmed independently by Stage 9) that the 31 December 2025 escrow/PA-P conformance deadline, the 28 February 2026 wind-up deadline, and the 15 September 2026 merchant-due-diligence deadline have all already passed as of the page's own "Last updated: 30 September 2026" date.

That said, the "Last updated" framing does create an implicit reader expectation — especially for a compliance-minded reader (in-house counsel, a CFO who's done some of their own research) — that the page reflects the state of the world as of that date. A reader who already knows about the PA transition deadlines and notices the page doesn't confirm one way or the other whether that window has closed may read the silence as a gap rather than a deliberate hedge, even though the fact-checker is right that nothing false is stated. This is exactly the "completeness opportunity" Stage 9 flagged, not a fabricated-currency problem: the fix Stage 9 already proposed — once a professional confirms current status, stating plainly that the PA transition window has closed and escrow/authorization compliance is now a steady-state requirement rather than an open migration — would convert a defensible-but-slightly-soft hedge into a genuinely confident, current statement, which is a stronger trust signal than either silence or a stale date would be. Recommended as a same-batch fix alongside the professional sign-off, not as a standalone blocker.
