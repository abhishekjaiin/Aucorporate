## New Authoritative Information Found
Verified via primary sources and independently corroborated legal-industry commentary:
- **Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, as amended (2015, 2019, 2021)** remains the operative statute; Sections 9/17 (interim relief), 29A (award timelines) and 34/36 (challenge/enforcement) are the same provisions grounded in the `/arbitration-services` refresh and are cross-referenced rather than re-explained in full here to avoid duplication.
- **Section 74, Indian Contract Act, 1872**: a tribunal or court is not bound to award the full stipulated liquidated damages figure — it may award the stipulated amount or a lesser "reasonable compensation," whichever is lower, particularly where the figure is not shown to be a genuine pre-estimate of loss. This is one of the most frequently arbitrated issues in Indian construction contracts and was entirely absent from the old page.
- **CPWD / NHAI / MES standard-form contracts** and **FIDIC-based contracts** both impose a multi-tier pre-arbitration process (engineer referral/conciliation/DRB, or a Dispute Adjudication Board under FIDIC) before arbitration can be invoked — verified via Global Arbitration Review's India construction-arbitration guide and independent commentary.
- **Associate Builders v. Delhi Development Authority, (2015) 3 SCC 49** — verified as a real Supreme Court case (Indian Kanoon, case notes from multiple independent sources), arising from a construction contract for housing in Trilok Puri, Delhi, where completion stretched from 9 to 34 months largely due to DDA-caused delay. The Court's articulation of the narrow Section 34 interference standard (patent illegality, perversity, public policy) is directly relevant to construction claimants and is now cited in place of the unverifiable case.
- **Hindustan Construction Co. Ltd. v. Union of India, (2020) 17 SCC 324** — verified: the Supreme Court struck down Section 87 (which had attempted to revive automatic stays for certain pre-2015 arbitrations) as arbitrary and contrary to the object of the Act.
- **Delhi Metro Rail Corporation Ltd. v. Delhi Airport Metro Express Pvt. Ltd.** — verified via multiple independent sources (SCC Online, Supreme Court Observer, Linklaters, Law.asia): the Supreme Court exercised its curative jurisdiction on 10 April 2024 to set aside a roughly ₹8,000 crore arbitral award concerning termination of the Delhi Airport Metro concession agreement, after the award had already survived a Section 34 challenge, a Section 37 appeal and a review petition. Used as a grounded, verifiable cautionary illustration of enforcement risk in the infrastructure/construction sector, replacing the removed unverifiable case.
- **"United India Insurance Company vs. M/s Kishan Singh & Others" (April 2026, HP High Court)** — could NOT be verified against Indian Kanoon, LiveLaw or general search; targeted searches for the named parties returned unrelated United India Insurance matters. Removed from the page entirely rather than repeated as fact.
