## Headline Finding

Two of Stage 4's claims were independently re-verified by direct read of the live `.tsx` files (not re-trusting Stage 4's summary), and a third, broader claim needed correcting.

**Confirmed exactly as Stage 4 reported:**
1. `/home/user/Aucorporate/app/india-entry-for-us-companies/fema-compliance-us-company-india-subsidiary/page.tsx` is genuinely and explicitly scoped to *after* incorporation. Its own intro states verbatim: "Once your Indian subsidiary is incorporated and receives foreign direct investment (FDI) from the US parent, FEMA... compliance becomes an ongoing obligation." The page covers FC-GPR, FC-TRS, Annual FLA, ECB registration, and RBI compounding mechanics — none of it addresses pre-incorporation exposure, and PE/permanent establishment is not mentioned anywhere in the file.
2. `/home/user/Aucorporate/app/india-entry-for-us-companies/page.tsx` (the US pillar) has zero PE content — no mention of "permanent establishment," "PE risk," "dependent agent," "fixed place," or "DTAA Article 5" anywhere in the file, across its intro, five narrative H2 sections (structure choice, incorporation, FEMA, transfer pricing, cost/timeline), five-item subPages grid, or FAQ block. Stage 4's finding that PE risk is absent from this specific cluster holds up.

**Correction to Stage 4's broader framing — the site is not PE-content-blank:**
Stage 4 diffed competitors against each other, not against AU Corporate's full site, and its "Structural / Linking Notes" section only checked the two files above. A wider grep across `app/` for "permanent establishment," "PE risk," "dependent agent," and "Employer of Record" surfaced real, already-published PE-adjacent content elsewhere on the site that Stage 6 needs to know about before drafting:

- **`/home/user/Aucorporate/app/doing-business-in-india/incorporation/page.tsx`** (general, nationality-agnostic "should you incorporate now" page) has a dedicated section, `id="pe-risk"`, headed "The Real Cost of Waiting Too Long: Business Connection Risk." It states that under **Section 9 of the Income-tax Act, 2025** — described explicitly as "the successor to the identically structured business-connection provision in the 1961 Act" — a dependent agent or a codified Section 9(9) "Significant Economic Presence" can trigger Indian tax exposure with no entity registered at all, and that DTAA protection narrows this but "has to be actively claimed and supported, not assumed."
- **This directly and usefully resolves Stage 4's Open Item 9.** AU Corporate's own live site has already taken the position that the business-connection/PE provision was carried over unchanged (still Section 9), unlike transfer pricing, which the same Income-tax Act, 2025 did renumber to Sections 161–173. This is not a fresh Stage 9 discovery — it's an existing published claim on `theaucorp.com` that the new PE page must be **consistent with**, not a separate open research question to re-litigate from scratch. Stage 9 should still confirm it against the primary Act text before this page ships (per the pipeline's normal fact-check discipline), but Stage 6 can draft on this basis with reasonable confidence rather than treating it as unresolved.
- **`/home/user/Aucorporate/app/doing-business-in-india/entry-process/page.tsx`** already names "a permanent establishment risk from an EOR or liaison arrangement that drifts into commercial activity" as a named Tax Exposure risk, and treats EOR itself neutrally as one legitimate route to market ("works well for a handful of people; it stops working once...") — not something AU is selling against.
- **`/home/user/Aucorporate/app/india-entry-for-australian-companies/page.tsx`** carries a full "Permanent Establishment Risk" info card and a dedicated FAQ on Service PE, and — importantly — already states the **India-US DTAA's Service PE threshold as 90 days**, used explicitly as the comparison point against the India-Australia treaty's more generous 183-day threshold. **The new page must use 90 days for the US treaty, matching what's already live**, not import an unverified competitor figure that happens to diverge.
- **`/home/user/Aucorporate/app/india-entry-for-german-companies/page.tsx`** carries a lighter, single-sentence Service PE mention (Article 5, no day threshold stated).
- **`/home/user/Aucorporate/app/services/taxation-regulatory/page.tsx`** already states the domestic concessional corporate tax rates a properly incorporated Indian subsidiary can elect into (22% concessional / 25% / 30% depending on turnover, 15% for qualifying new manufacturing) — see Open Item 1 handling below for why this matters.

Net effect: this isn't virgin territory, and it isn't "AU has nothing to say yet" — it's an **underdeveloped topic scattered in fragments** (one paragraph on a general incorporation-timing page, one FAQ on the Australian regional page, one line on the German page, nothing at all on the US cluster) that has never been given a dedicated, case-law-grounded, US-treaty-specific treatment. That is arguably a *stronger* right-to-win story than "starting from zero," because it means AU Corporate already has committed numbers and positions in production (90-day US threshold, Section 9 non-renumbering, EOR-as-legitimate-route framing) that this page must build on and stay consistent with — and a real internal-linking opportunity Stage 6 should action, not just avoid contradicting.

## Explicit Guidance for the Writer

**Tone, generally**: match the register already live on the FEMA and transfer-pricing sibling pages — specific mechanics, named forms/sections/thresholds, "here's why this trips up a US parent specifically," not a generic explainer voice. Avoid the wisemonk.io/kaam.work pattern of a reassuring rule-of-thumb followed by a soft service pitch — the whole point of this page's differentiation is going one level past that.

**On "does an EOR eliminate PE risk" — this is the section most at risk of reading wrong, handle it deliberately:**
- AU Corporate does not sell EOR services and is not competing with EOR platforms for that budget line — so this section must not read as "here's why EOR is risky, use us instead." That would be arguing against a straw man and would immediately read as commercially motivated to a reader who can tell AU doesn't offer the alternative it's implicitly selling.
- Follow the tone already established on `entry-process/page.tsx`: EOR is one legitimate, lawful route to market with real limits, not a villain. Frame the actual legal point precisely: PE exposure is a function of *conduct and control* (who negotiates and concludes contracts, who directs day-to-day work, whether activity is habitual and not merely preparatory/auxiliary) — not of which employment vehicle a person sits under. An EOR reduces certain exposure (it removes the "is this person even legally engaged with India" question) but does not, by itself, wall off dependent-agent or fixed-place risk if the underlying conduct on the ground still looks like the US company operating in India.
- Go to the operational level Stage 3/4 identified as the real gap: name the specific conduct beyond formal contract-signing that creates exposure (habitual involvement in negotiating deal terms, de facto operational control over the EOR-employed staff's day-to-day work, local decision-making authority) and the kind of documentation/operating practice that supports a defensible "no PE" position under audit. This is rigorous, not promotional, because it's equally true and equally useful whether the reader uses an EOR, a contractor, or has already incorporated.
- Never write a sentence structured as "unlike EOR providers, AU Corporate..." — there's no need to position against a category AU doesn't compete in. Let the depth of the analysis itself be the differentiator.
- Any CTA at the end of this section (or the page generally) should be entity-agnostic: an offer to review PE exposure works the same whether the reader is using contractors, an EOR, or already has a subsidiary — not an offer framed as "switch from your EOR to incorporating with us."

**Where NOT to force a service mention**: the case-law/typology/threshold sections (the bulk of the page's actual content) should read as pure legal/tax analysis with no service-line language at all. Service-line connections belong only at the natural transition points already identified above (liaison-office → FEMA page; PE-finding consequence → transfer pricing/documentation; "what would a PE exposure review actually involve" → compliance/risk advisory) — not sprinkled through the explanatory content. If a section doesn't have one of those natural transitions, the honest answer is no service belongs there, and the writer should leave it as pure analysis rather than manufacturing a connection.

**Numeric/factual consistency checks the writer must honor** (not open questions — already-published positions on this live site):
- India-US DTAA Service PE threshold: **90 days** in any 12-month period (already stated on `/india-entry-for-australian-companies` as the comparison point against Australia's 183 days) — do not use a different number without a documented reason.
- The Income-tax Act, 2025's business-connection provision is **Section 9**, not renumbered (unlike transfer pricing's move to Sections 161–173) — already asserted on `/doing-business-in-india/incorporation`.
- Domestic concessional corporate tax rates for a properly incorporated subsidiary (22% concessional / 25% / 30% / 15% for qualifying new manufacturing) are already published on `/services/taxation-regulatory` — use these as the contrast point for the PE consequence framing per Open Item 1 above, rather than citing an unverified PE-specific percentage.
