## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The dual CA/US-CPA framing is used precisely, not decoratively.** The intro's Form 5471/Schedule M and Form 8858 connection is a real, specific, non-generic claim that no India-only competitor in the reviewed SERP set can credibly make — and it's stated once, in the right place, not repeated as a slogan.
- **The associated-enterprise Service PE correction is the single strongest EEAT signal on the page.** The final copy doesn't just state the corrected "no minimum-day floor" rule — it explains *why* the related-party case is actually the higher-risk one ("services rendered to a related party are treated as inherently more likely to reflect sustained operational integration... the same reasoning that makes tax authorities look harder at related-party dealings generally, transfer pricing included"). That's a professional explaining the logic behind a rule, not a content page reciting a threshold.
- **The EOR section is the clearest evidence of real practitioner judgment on the page.** It resists the two easy failure modes (a reassuring "EOR solves this" rule of thumb, or an implicit sales pitch against EOR platforms AU doesn't compete with) and instead names the actual conduct-based factors an examiner would look at and the actual documentation that supports a position. This is exactly the kind of content a generic AI-with-a-logo page does not produce — it requires knowing what an audit actually interrogates.
- **The treaty-caveat discipline (Clifford Chance, then extended to Formula One) is a genuine trust signal.** Correctly flagging that two of the five load-bearing cases were decided under different treaties, while still explaining why they remain persuasively relevant, is more honest than most competitor pages Stage 3 reviewed — none of which disclosed this at all.
- **The consequence section's handling of the disputed tax-rate figure is a model of how to write around an unverified number without the page feeling thin.** It states the real, verifiable mechanism (foreign-company rate vs. domestic concessional rate, sourced to AU's own published rate page) and is honest that the exact effective rate isn't stated pending verification, without ever reading as evasive.
