- **Case identity confirmed accurate on the old page, with one correction**: three Mauritius entities were involved — Tiger Global International **II, III, and IV** Holdings — not just "II" as the old page implied by naming only one entity throughout. Full case: *Authority for Advance Rulings (Income Tax) & Ors. v. Tiger Global International II Holdings, III Holdings and IV Holdings*, reported as **2026 INSC 60**, Civil Appeal No. 262 of 2026 (arising out of SLP(C) No. 2640 of 2025) with connected Civil Appeal Nos. 263 and 264 of 2026 — this specific appeal number was already correct on the old page and is now independently confirmed (matches caseciter.com's case summary).
- **Judgment date**: 15 January 2026 — confirmed (old page said "2026" without a date; now dated precisely).
- **Bench**: Justices R. Mahadevan and J.B. Pardiwala — new addition, not previously on the page.
- **AAR order date**: 26 March 2020 (old page said only "(2020)").
- **Delhi HC order date**: 28 August 2024, a common order across all three entities (old page said only "(2024)").
- **Core legal mechanism** (missing from old page entirely): the Court's holding did not simply invoke "substance over form" in the abstract — it held the transaction fell under **Article 13(4)** (indirect transfers, no grandfathering) rather than Article 13(3A) (direct transfers of Indian shares, grandfathered), because Tiger Global sold shares in a *Singapore* company, not an Indian one. Combined with the GAAR finding, this left no treaty shelter available.
- **GAAR statutory mechanics** (missing from old page): Section 96 (impermissible avoidance arrangement definition), Section 96(2) (burden-of-proof shift to the taxpayer once Revenue makes a prima facie case), Section 90(2A) (Chapter X-A/GAAR applies notwithstanding Section 90(2), i.e. overrides treaty election). GAAR has applied since AY 2018-19 (tax benefits arising on/after 1 April 2017) — confirmed via ICAI GAAR material, Taxmann, and azb & partners analysis.
- **Beneficial ownership nuance**: Article 13 capital-gains clauses in the Mauritius/Singapore/Netherlands DTAAs do not contain explicit beneficial-ownership wording; courts (per Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas/Lexology commentary and NatLawReview) have declined to read such a requirement into Article 13. This is a materially important, previously-absent distinction from what "beneficial ownership" bullets on competitor pages often imply.
- **2016 Protocol LOB clause (Article 27A, India-Mauritius)**: shell/conduit company test with an expenditure threshold of Mauritian Rs 15,00,000 (~Indian Rs 27,00,000) in the preceding 12 months, applicable to the Article 13(3B) transitional-period benefit — confirmed via TaxGuru and BCA Journal analysis of the 2016 protocol.
- **India-Singapore DTAA has a parallel 2016-protocol Limitation of Relief clause (Article 24)** with its own transition period (1 April 2017–31 March 2019, taxed at 50% of domestic rate) — confirmed via BCA Journal/VJM Global commentary. Netherlands-specific LOB text could not be independently verified within this session and was deliberately **not** given specific treaty-article claims in the new content — only the general (well-established) GAAR-override point via Section 90(2A), which is treaty-agnostic.
- **2024 India-Mauritius Protocol — Principal Purpose Test (PPT)**: signed 7 March 2024, incorporating BEPS minimum-standard anti-abuse provisions; **CBDT Circular No. 01/2025** (21 January 2025) clarifies prospective application from the protocol's entry into force and confirms grandfathered transactions remain outside its scope — confirmed via EY India alerts and Apex Group insights. This is a genuinely new regulatory development not reflected anywhere on the old page.
