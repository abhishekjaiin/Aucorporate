## Per-Page Analysis

### vjmglobal.com/blog/establishment-subsidiary-tantamount-having-permanent-establishment-india
**Does well:** Answers a narrow, sharply-scoped legal question — whether incorporating an Indian subsidiary automatically creates PE for the US/foreign parent — rather than a generic "what is PE" sweep. Grounds the answer in the actual controlling standard from the E-Funds Supreme Court decision (mere access to premises is insufficient; the foreign enterprise needs effective, sustained operational control) rather than just name-dropping the case. Includes a concrete, practitioner-level numeric illustration of how easily the 30-day associated-enterprise threshold is breached (a three-person team spending two weeks in Bangalore already equals 42 person-days) — a specific worked example, not an abstract threshold statement.

**Does poorly:** Snippet evidence suggests this is a single-issue legal note focused on the subsidiary question specifically, not a comprehensive page that also walks through the full four-way typology (fixed place / service / dependent agent / construction) the way the wisemonk.io and treelife.in pages do — so it likely under-serves the broader "PE risk India" head-term reader who hasn't yet narrowed to the subsidiary-specific question. No EOR-specific content surfaced (unsurprising — VJM Global doesn't sell EOR services, so this isn't their differentiation angle, but it does mean a reader landing here for the EOR question gets nothing). Structure (heading hierarchy, presence/absence of FAQ, author byline, update date) is unverified — WebFetch blocked.

**Why it likely ranks:** Topical precision — it answers the exact sub-question a genuinely worried CFO/counsel types after already knowing they're setting up (or have set up) a subsidiary, matched to a specific, citable Supreme Court precedent — combined with the firm apparently running a broader PE-risk content cluster (a Singapore-buyer variant of the same topic per Stage 1), which is a topical-authority signal rather than a confirmed backlink-driven one (Semrush unavailable).

**What would beat it:** Pair this same subsidiary/E-Funds legal answer with the full four-way typology in one page, so it satisfies both the narrow "does incorporating a subsidiary create PE" searcher and the broader "what is PE risk in India" searcher — and extend the analysis backward to the pre-incorporation stage (EOR/contractor exposure before any entity exists), which this page doesn't touch.

### wisemonk.io/blogs/permanent-establishment-risk-in-india
**Does well:** The cleanest, most scannable exposition of the full four-way PE typology found across all pages reviewed, with a specific numeric threshold attached to each type (roughly six months of continuous use for fixed place; 90 days in 12 months for service PE, tightening to about 30 days for associated-enterprise services; six to twelve months for construction; dependent agent framed as activity-based rather than time-based). Pairs each PE type with a concrete, tactical mitigation move (e.g., using short-term/non-exclusive workspace arrangements instead of a standing leased office; keeping contract-signing authority with the US entity rather than local staff).

**Does poorly:** Its answer to "does an EOR eliminate PE risk" is thin and reassuring rather than rigorous — it reduces the whole question to "keep contract-signing authority in the US and you're largely fine," without engaging the harder question of what conduct short of formal contract-signing (day-to-day negotiation behavior, habitual involvement in deal terms, de facto operational control) tribunals have actually treated as creating exposure. No case-law citation surfaced anywhere in the available snippets — the thresholds are presented as facts rather than grounded in the Morgan Stanley/Formula One/E-Funds line of precedent the rest of the SERP leans on, which is a real gap given how case-law-driven this topic is. As an EOR platform, its commercial incentive is to reassure a prospect rather than fully stress-test the risk boundary.

**Why it likely ranks:** Likely the most featured-snippet/AI-overview-friendly page in this set — it answers "what are the PE types and thresholds" completely and at a glance — reinforced by wisemonk.io running at least two pages on this exact topic (a topical-depth/internal-linking signal, not a confirmed authority signal since Semrush is unavailable).

**What would beat it:** Keep the same clean typology-plus-thresholds structure but ground each threshold in the actual case law that established it, and replace the reassuring EOR answer with a genuinely rigorous one that names the specific conduct that creates residual exposure even inside an EOR structure.

## Cross-Page Patterns

**What most strong pages share:**
- Near-universal convergence on the same four-way PE typology (fixed place / service / dependent agent / construction) as the organizing framework — even pages that don't spell out all four explicitly (vjmglobal.com, ksandk.com) clearly assume reader familiarity with it as the baseline structure.
- Explicit "US company" or "foreign company" audience framing is treated as a first-class qualifier across nearly every page, not folded into generic "PE in India" language — confirms Stage 2's framing decision.
- The pages that feel most actionable are the ones with numbered, specific thresholds (90 days, 30 days for associated enterprises, 6 months) rather than qualitative descriptions — this is the clearest differentiator between "abstract legal explainer" and "content a reader can actually act on."
- Firms with an adjacent service line (EOR platforms, CA/law firms) consistently close on a soft advisory or service-engagement pitch rather than a hard transactional CTA — consistent with Stage 1's finding that this is a guide/education-first SERP, not a bottom-funnel one.
- No single page reviewed combines all of: full case-law grounding (specific holdings tied to specific PE types), the full numeric typology, a rigorous (non-reassuring) EOR answer, and a sourced consequence stat. Each page is strong on one or two of these dimensions and visibly weak or silent on the rest.

**What's uniformly weak across all of them:**
- No page reviewed cites both the Clifford Chance ruling (Delhi HC, Dec 2025 — rejecting a "virtual" Service PE and reaffirming a physical-presence requirement) and the Booking.com tribunal outcome (Feb 2026) together with the older Morgan Stanley / Formula One / E-Funds trio in one place. treelife.in comes closest on case-law currency but its EOR coverage looks thin-to-absent; kaam.work has the Booking.com stakes figure but no older precedent at all.
- The EOR question is answered shallowly everywhere it's actually addressed. Every EOR-platform page that raises "does an EOR eliminate PE risk" answers it in a way that serves the platform's own commercial interest (reassuring, "keep contract authority and you're largely fine") rather than walking a CFO through the actual residual-risk analysis: what specific conduct beyond formal contract-signing has actually been treated by Indian tax authorities/tribunals as creating dependent-agent or fixed-place exposure for EOR-employed staff, and what documentation or operating practice an EOR arrangement should maintain to support a "no PE" position under audit. This is precisely the gap Stage 2 flagged as AU Corporate's best differentiation opportunity, and it holds up under this closer pass.
- No page reviewed resolves the effective PE tax-rate conflict flagged in Stage 1/2 (roughly 40%/43.68% vs. 35%/38.22% across secondary sources) — no snippet surfaced a primary rate-schedule citation for either figure.
- No page reviewed appears to address whether the new Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbers or restructures the PE/"business connection" provisions the way it restructured transfer pricing into Sections 161-173 — this remains an open gap across the whole SERP, not just AU Corporate's page, and is worth flagging to Stage 5/6 as a potential freshness/authority differentiator if it can be resolved against a primary source.
- No page reviewed structures the pre-incorporation vs. post-incorporation decision as a single coherent path. Individual pieces exist scattered across different competitors (kaam.work's rule-of-thumb bundle, wisemonk.io's mitigation-by-PE-type tips, ksandk.com's employee-vs-consultant test, ahlawatassociates.com's observation that a subsidiary doesn't automatically contain the parent's own PE risk) but no single page walks a founder/CFO through "you have no entity yet → here's your exposure using contractors/EOR → here's what changes once you incorporate → here's what still doesn't change." This is also the exact gap Stage 1 identified in AU Corporate's own existing FEMA/subsidiary content, which is explicitly scoped to *after* incorporation.
- No author byline, professional credential, or explicit "last updated" date was confirmed on any page reviewed. This cannot be fully verified given WebFetch is blocked, but no snippet across any of the searches run in this stage surfaced a named individual author, stated credential, or explicit update date on any competitor page — trust-signal depth here is either genuinely thin across the SERP or simply not indexed/surfaced in search snippets; either way it's an area AU Corporate can differentiate on if it can genuinely back it with real authorship and dating.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear
A genuinely more useful AU Corporate page needs to combine, in one place, what no single competitor page reviewed does: (1) the full four-way PE typology with specific numeric thresholds, matching wisemonk.io's clarity; (2) real case-law grounding that ties each specific holding to the specific PE type it defines, extended through the Clifford Chance ruling (Dec 2025, physical-presence requirement for Service PE) and the Booking.com tribunal outcome (Feb 2026) — currency neither treelife.in nor kaam.work fully achieves; (3) an honest, operationally specific answer to "does an EOR eliminate PE risk" that goes beyond "keep contract authority in the US" to name the actual conduct that creates residual dependent-agent or fixed-place exposure inside an EOR arrangement, and what documentation/operating practice supports a "no PE" position — this is the single clearest gap across every page reviewed and the exact opportunity Stage 2 flagged; (4) an explicit pre-incorporation-to-post-incorporation decision path (no entity yet, using contractors or an EOR → what changes once incorporated → what doesn't change) that no competitor page structures as one coherent journey, and that closes the specific gap in AU Corporate's own existing (post-incorporation-only) FEMA content; (5) a consequence stat — tax rate or aggregate assessment figure — that is actually verified against a primary source rather than repeating one of the two conflicting secondary figures currently circulating, presented with the same "why should a CFO care" force as kaam.work's Booking.com number or beaconfiling.com's aggregate assessment stat, but defensible. None of this requires the page to be longer than competitors' — it requires closing specific, named gaps that persist across every strong page in this set.
