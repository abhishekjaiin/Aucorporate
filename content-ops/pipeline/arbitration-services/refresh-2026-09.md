## New Authoritative Information Found
Verified via primary/authoritative sources and current legal-industry commentary (used to ground new content, not just competitor copy):
- **Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, as amended (2015, 2019, 2021)** remains the operative statute for arbitration seated in India.
- **Section 9 / Section 17 (interim relief)**: courts (S.9) and, once constituted, the arbitral tribunal itself (S.17) can grant interim measures; the 2015 amendment restricts courts from entertaining a Section 9 application once the tribunal can grant equally efficacious relief, and tribunal orders under Section 17 are enforceable in the same manner as a court order.
- **Seat vs. venue**: the seat fixes supervisory jurisdiction and curial law (the "centre of gravity" principle from *BALCO*); the venue is only the physical hearing location. In *PASL Wind Solutions Pvt. Ltd. v. GE Power Conversion India Pvt. Ltd.* (2021), the Supreme Court confirmed even two Indian parties may validly choose a foreign seat.
- **Section 29A**: a domestic arbitral award must be made within 12 months of completion of pleadings, extendable by 6 months by party consent; any further extension requires a court application and sufficient cause.
- **Section 34 / Section 36**: a domestic award is challenged only on narrow grounds under Section 34 and enforced as a decree under Section 36; since the 2015 amendment, filing a Section 34 challenge does not automatically stay enforcement — a separate stay must be sought and is frequently conditioned on a deposit.
- **Part II, Sections 44–52 (New York Convention enforcement)**: a foreign award from a government-notified reciprocating territory is enforced via court application, subject only to the narrow refusal grounds in Section 48.
- **Emergency arbitrators**: following *Amazon.com NV Investment Holdings v. Future Retail Ltd.* (Supreme Court, 2021), an India-seated emergency arbitrator's order is treated as a Section 17 order and is enforceable as such — a recognition that does not currently extend to foreign-seated emergency awards.
- **Institutional arbitration**: the Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration (MCIA) released updated 2025 Arbitration Rules (its third edition, expanded from 36 to 49 provisions) adding joinder of non-parties, expedited early dismissal, good-faith obligations and emergency arbitrator procedures, alongside the Indian Council of Arbitration (ICA).
- **Pending reform — verified as NOT yet law**: the draft Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Bill, 2024 (based on the T.K. Vishwanathan Expert Committee report of February 2024) was released for public consultation in October 2024 and, as of mid-2026, has not been introduced in Parliament or enacted. It is referenced on the page explicitly as a pending draft, not current law, to avoid overstating its status.

None of this existed on the page previously in any form.
