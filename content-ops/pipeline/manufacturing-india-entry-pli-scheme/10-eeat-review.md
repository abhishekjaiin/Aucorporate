## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The Form 5472 correction is the single strongest EEAT signal on the page.** It doesn't just avoid an error — it proactively corrects a plausible misconception (that Form 5471 and 5472 are a matched pair for this fact pattern), states the actual rule, names the narrow fact-specific exception, and explains *why* it's worth stating plainly rather than hedging generically. This is what "experience" looks like on the page: knowing what a reader might reasonably (and wrongly) assume, and addressing it directly. Fact-check independently confirmed this against IRS primary-source text.
- **The PLI/ECMS transition framing** treats a genuinely moving regulatory target with real precision — naming ECMS, stating plainly that original sector windows are closing on a rolling basis, and explicitly instructing the reader not to build an investment case around original PLI terms as settled. Fact-check found the ECMS outlay figure had already moved once (₹22,919cr → ₹40,000cr) within ten months, which validates this caution as substantive risk management, not reflexive hedging.
- **The TNMM correction and the tax-stack synthesis** (PLI as disbursed subsidy vs. the 15% rate as a separate corporate-tax election, explicitly named as two stackable levers "evaluated and elected into separately") are the kind of distinction a generic AI overview of Indian manufacturing tax would blur together. Connecting them is Stage 5's identified right-to-win angle, and the execution delivers on it.
- **Restraint on cannibalization** — the page consistently does 2–4 sentences of genuinely new manufacturing-specific delta before linking to the deeper sibling page (transfer pricing, FEMA), rather than re-deriving content that already exists elsewhere on the site. This reads as a coordinated professional practice's content architecture, not a standalone page written in isolation — a meaningful, if subtle, trust signal in itself.
- **The no-CTA rule inside the Factories Act section** is a small but real signal: a page willing to give away pure procedural expertise without a sales pitch attached reads as more credible, not less commercially effective.

## Where It Falls Short

1. **The 115BAB eligibility-window hedge is well-written where it appears, but under-surfaced relative to its own stated importance.** Reading the body-copy paragraph in the Tax Stack section in isolation, the hedge is genuine and calibrated — it names the real risk (a commencement-of-manufacturing sunset condition, not the incorporation date), avoids inventing a section number or date it can't confirm, and mirrors the page's own PLI/ECMS caveat style. Fact-check independently confirmed this reads as "accurate and appropriately scoped." The problem is placement, not content:
   - The **Quick Facts box at the top of the page** — exactly where a skimming CFO reader is most likely to look first — states "**15% concessional rate** — the narrower corporate tax rate available to qualifying new manufacturing companies, stackable with PLI as a separate incentive," with **no hedge marker at all**, while the two other genuinely uncertain Quick Facts items ("14 sectors," "10+/20+ workers") both carry an inline asterisked caveat. A reader who only reads the Quick Facts box — a real reading pattern this format is designed for — would come away believing the 15% rate is straightforwardly available, which is precisely the outcome fact-check flagged as the "genuine live eligibility-window question" risk.
   - The **FAQ gives the PLI/ECMS transition its own dedicated question (FAQ #5)** but has **no equivalent FAQ for the 115BAB eligibility window**, even though fact-check ranked this risk *above* the Form 5472 point (which does get its own FAQ, #6) as "the single highest remaining publish-risk item on the page." A reader scanning the FAQ for exactly this kind of "is this actually available to me" question won't find it addressed there.
   - Net effect: the hedge exists and is accurate, but its prominence is inversely related to its importance, in a way that's inconsistent with how the page treats the PLI/ECMS transition risk, which is a structurally comparable open question given equal or greater visibility (Quick Facts hedge + dedicated H3 + dedicated FAQ). This reads as the hedge having been added correctively at the end of the drafting process (which it was, per the Stage 9 loop-back log) rather than designed in from the start — worth fixing before this page is treated as fully calibrated, not just factually corrected.
2. **Zero external primary-source citations anywhere on the page.** Every regulatory claim is either hedged with "confirm against the relevant ministry's guidelines" or stated as settled without a link a skeptical reader (or Google) could independently check. This is a missed trust opportunity, not an accuracy problem — fact-check found real, citable primary sources for several of this page's core claims (PIB's 14-sector press release, MeitY's PLI FAQ PDF, DPIIT's March 2026 Press Note 3 amendment) that could be linked or named without over-committing to volatile figures.
3. **No named individual expert attribution**, only a practice-level byline. Given the page explicitly asks the reader to "confirm directly with AU Corporate" on two separate live judgment calls (PLI window, 115BAB window), and given the fact-check gate itself requires a specific qualified professional's sign-off before publish, the page is currently missing the natural, load-bearing opportunity to name that person.
4. **China+1 rationale is argued without a single concrete, sourced data point or example** — a defensible choice given the session's sourcing constraints, but it's the one place the page's caution measurably costs it persuasive/practical weight rather than just protecting accuracy, since the strategic case (not a numeric incentive figure) is the page's actual top-of-funnel hook.

The page's underlying expertise, restraint, and factual discipline are genuinely strong (see above), and the standing fact-check publish-gate on the 115BAB window is being handled consistently with how this pipeline has treated comparable judgment-dependent items elsewhere — that gate is not, by itself, what's holding this page back from an EEAT pass. What is: the 115BAB hedge, while accurate, is not yet surfaced with the prominence its own stated risk level (per fact-check, the single highest remaining publish-risk item on the page) requires. None of these require new research or reopen any already-resolved fact-check item — they are presentation/structure fixes to content and sourcing that already exists or is already pending.
