## Recurring Entities & Regulations
- **DTAA Article 5** (US-India tax treaty) — the central legal anchor; named explicitly on nearly every ranking page.
- **Fixed Place PE, Service PE (90 days/12 months, ~30 days for associated enterprises), Dependent Agent PE (DAPE), Construction PE** — the four-way PE typology repeated near-verbatim across almost every competitor page. This looks like the de facto standard framework the whole SERP has converged on.
- **Income Tax Act, 1961** — Section 9(1)(i) ("business connection") cited alongside treaty PE as the domestic-law parallel/overlap concept.
- **RBI** — in the liaison-office-specific PE discussion (RBI approval does not itself prevent a PE finding).
- **Case law cited repeatedly**: Morgan Stanley & Co. (SC, 2007) — Service PE / stewardship activities; Formula One World Championship Ltd. v. CIT (SC, 2017) — "stability, productivity, dependence" fixed-place test; ADIT v. E-Funds IT Solution Inc. (SC) — "virtual projection" concept, subsidiary-as-PE standard under India-US DTAA specifically (this is the direct precedent behind the VJM Global page named in the brief); CIT v. Clifford Chance Pte Ltd. (Delhi HC, reported Dec 2025) — physical presence required for Service PE under India-Singapore treaty (a very recent, actively-cited ruling); an ITAT ruling on Booking.com (reported Feb 2026) that set aside a large PE-based tax demand.
- **Section 271, Income Tax Act** — penalty provision cited for PE-related non-compliance (100-300% of unpaid tax).
- **PAN, TAN, ITR-6, transfer pricing documentation** — named as the compliance cascade that follows a PE finding.
- **40% + surcharge + cess (~43.68% effective)** — the foreign-company/PE tax rate, repeated across multiple sources as the headline consequence figure. Not independently verified against a primary Income Tax Act rate schedule in this pass — flagged for Stage 2/5 to confirm against a primary source before AU Corporate cites it.
- **OECD Commentary** — referenced alongside Indian case law for the "virtual projection" / dependent-agent standard.
- **Income-tax Act, 2025** (transfer pricing sections 161-173, effective April 2026) — this is the same Act AU Corporate's existing transfer-pricing page already covers; it did not surface directly in PE-specific searches in this pass but is worth Stage 2 double-checking whether the new Act also restructures the PE/business-connection provisions, since it replaces the 1961 Act.

## Search Intent (page-level read)
- **Predominantly informational-with-embedded-commercial-intent.** The overwhelming majority of ranking pages are educational/explainer content (definitions, thresholds, case law, mitigation checklists) published by firms that sell an adjacent or directly comparable service — i.e., classic "answer the question, then pitch the service" content marketing. Evidence: EOR/PEO platforms (wisemonk.io, kaam.work, remunance.com, omnivoo.com, asanify.com) frame nearly every mitigation strategy around "use our EOR," and CA/law firm blogs (VJM Global, treelife.in, ahlawatassociates.com, ksandk.com) end on PE-exposure-review/advisory service framing per the search-result summaries.
- **Some pages are purely informational with no visible sell**: the case-law databases (itatonline.org, casemine.com, recordoflaw.in), Wikipedia, and the IRS treaty PDF itself carry no commercial framing.
- **No purely transactional pages observed** (e.g., no "buy/book a PE assessment" landing page ranked in the organic results reviewed) — this looks like a guide/education-first SERP, not a bottom-funnel one, which is consistent with this being a pre-purchase, risk-awareness stage query rather than a "hire us" query.
- **Navigational intent: not applicable** — no evidence any of these queries are branded/navigational.

- Multiple pages cite a Delhi High Court ruling reported **December 2025** (CIT v. - One KPMG PDF alert used in results is dated **2016** — a decade-old primary-firm publication still surfacing in search, which suggests the SERP has some genuinely stale content mixed in alongside 2025/2026-dated pages; this is a gap AU Corporate could exploit with clearly-dated, current content.

- No specific Indian FY/AY (e.g., "AY 2026-27") was seen named directly on a PE-risk page in this pass, unlike AU Corporate's own transfer-pricing content which is tied to specific assessment years — this is a structural difference Stage 2/5 should note: PE-risk content anchors to case law and treaty dates, not to an annual filing calendar the way FEMA/transfer-pricing content does.
