The IRS Treasury Technical Explanation (irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/inditech.pdf) and the Indian Embassy USA's own "Tax rates as per IT Act vis-a-vis Indo-US DTAA" comparison PDF (indianembassyusa.gov.in) were both reached this way and **independently corroborate identical figures**, which is the strongest confidence this pipeline has had on the rate question so far:
  - **Dividends (Article 10):** 15% if the beneficial owner is a company holding ≥10% of the payer's voting stock; 25% in all other cases. (Confirms this is a genuine two-tier structure, not a single flat treaty rate — several competitor summaries flatten this.)
  - **Interest (Article 11):** 15% general rate; 10% where the beneficial owner is a bank or similar financial institution; 0% on certain government-approved/government-guaranteed loans.
  - **Royalties (Article 12):** 15% for copyrights (literary, artistic, scientific, film/TV/radio); 10% for patents, trademarks, designs, models, plans, secret formulas/processes, or know-how.
  - **Fees for Included Services / FIS (Article 12, the India-US treaty's specific "make available" standard, narrower than the generic FTS term used in most other Indian DTAAs):** 15% standard rate; 10% where the fee is ancillary/subsidiary to a royalty payment.
  - **Domestic (non-treaty) baseline:** 20% under the Income-tax Act, plus surcharge and cess, producing an effective rate commonly cited as ~20.8%–23.9% depending on payer/payee category — this is the "before treaty relief" number several competitors cite, and it is consistent with what the government sources imply (Section 90(2) lets the taxpayer elect whichever of the domestic or treaty rate is lower).
  - One specific, useful nuance surfaced only by KRPR Associates (not by any other page checked): management/advisory/service fees that don't meet the FIS "make available" standard fall under the treaty's Business Profits article and are **not taxable in India at all absent a US parent's permanent establishment there** — a materially different (and more favorable) outcome than the FIS withholding-rate framing most competitors default to for any cross-border service payment. Worth independently verifying against the treaty's Article 7/PE text before use, but it is a real differentiator to check.

## Per-Page Analysis

### india-briefing.com (Dezan Shira & Associates)
**Does well:** Covers the full route list (dividend, buyback, capital reduction, winding-up surplus, FTS/consultancy fees, royalty) at a level that functions as an orientation map for someone who doesn't yet know repatriation has more than one channel. As a "Doing Business Guide" reference property with a large existing India-content library, it carries real topical authority signals (the property covers India market entry broadly, so this page sits inside a much larger trust context than a single standalone blog post).

**Does poorly:** The buyback figure surfaced in search excerpts is the **old rate framing (a flat buyback tax, proceeds effectively treated as a distribution)** with no visible reference to the Finance Act 2026 reform that moved non-promoter non-resident buyback proceeds to capital-gains treatment from 1 April 2026 — this reads as a live freshness gap, not just a stale title-tag (per Stage 1's suspicion about Perfect Accounting, but here found on India Briefing instead). No FIS/Article 12 rate specificity surfaced — royalty coverage reads generic rather than treaty-article-sourced. No mention of Form 145/146 or Form 41 renumbering in the excerpts found for this specific page (India Briefing does have a *separate* article dedicated to the Form 41/TRC transition, which is a hub-and-spoke content strategy — splitting the topic across pages rather than answering it in one place).

**Why it likely ranks:** Domain-level trust and topical breadth from being part of a long-running, broad "Doing Business in India" reference library, plus the fact that it answers the top-level "what are my options" question cleanly and early — likely wins on relevance-to-a-broad-query and accumulated authority rather than because this specific page is the most current or most technically deep.

**What would beat it:** Match its route breadth but with the buyback section actually current to Finance Act 2026, and add the specific treaty-article sourcing (Article 10/11/12 rate tiers with the shareholding/institution conditions attached) that this page's excerpts don't show.

## Verdict: The Bar to Clear

A genuinely more useful AU Corporate page needs to do three specific things no single competitor page checked does all at once, rather than simply being longer or covering more routes:

1. **Source the rate table to the actual treaty text and a government reference**, not firm commentary — state the Article 10/11/12 tiers with their exact conditions (≥10% voting stock for the 15% dividend tier; bank/financial-institution status for the 10% interest tier; copyright vs. patent/trademark/know-how for the royalty split; ancillary-to-royalty for the FIS 10% tier) and note these were checked against the IRS Treasury Technical Explanation and the Indian government's own IT-Act-vs-DTAA comparison — a level of sourcing transparency zero pages checked provide.
2. **Put the Finance Act 2026 buyback reform, the Form 15CA/15CB→145/146 remittance-certification chain, and the Form 10F→41/Form 8802-6166 TRC process on the same page as the rate table and route comparison** — not split across satellite posts (India Briefing's pattern) or omitted (KRPR's gap) or present-but-disconnected-from-the-rates (Beacon Filing's pattern). A reader deciding how to move money out this quarter needs the rate, the current regulatory status, and the exact paperwork together in one place.
3. **Close the loop on the US side of the transaction** — bring in Form 1118/1116 foreign tax credit mechanics (not Form 67, which is the wrong, India-side individual mechanism) so the page actually answers "what does this cost the US parent, net" rather than stopping at the Indian withholding figure. This is the single most consistently missing piece across every page checked and is squarely within AU's stated Cluster-A, US-parent-buyer framing from Stage 2 — no direct competitor checked treats it as core content.

None of this requires greater word count for its own sake — CalcGuru's buyback-timeline section and KRPR's treaty-specific PE/Business-Profits distinction are both compact and effective specifically because they're precise, not because they're long. The bar is precision and completeness on the specific sub-questions a US CFO actually has, sourced transparently, brought together on one page rather than scattered across a competitor's content cluster.
