A generic content mill would either ignore the draft entirely or treat it as settled.

Getting this specific distinction right — and explaining it with a concrete example (a Chinese applicant proposing an office in Mumbai isn't automatically restricted) — is real differentiation, not filler.

## Where It Falls Short

1. **The Form FNC annex reference is stated with more confidence than the record supports.** The body text reads: "filed with the designated AD Category-I bank under Annex B/Annex-1 of FED Master Direction No. 10/2015-16" — stated as flat fact, with no hedge visible to the reader. But `09-fact-check.md` explicitly found a conflicting secondary source labelling this "Annex C — Form FNC," and both the original fact-check and the recheck carry this forward as unresolved, flagged for human sign-off. Writer's Note #5 hedges this correctly *internally*, but that hedge does not survive into the published copy — a reader (or Google) sees an unqualified, specific claim. If this is wrong at publish, it's exactly the kind of small, checkable, easily-embarrassing error that undermines trust in everything else on the page, because it's the sort of detail a real practitioner would get right. **This should either be confirmed against the current, in-force Master Direction text before publish, or the sentence should be loosened slightly (e.g., "filed... under the applicable annex of FED Master Direction No. 10/2015-16") until it is.**
2. **The restricted-sector list (Defence, Telecom, Private Security, Information & Broadcasting) is presented as settled fact without inline qualification**, despite being sourced only from convergent secondary reporting, not a directly-read primary text, and despite the fact-check explicitly calling this out as carrying real professional-liability exposure if wrong — it's also flagged as the page's core differentiation angle, which raises the stakes of getting it wrong rather than lowering them. As with the FNC annex, the hedge exists in Writer's Note #11 but not in the copy a reader sees.
3. **The gap between "what's hedged in the Writer's Notes" and "what's hedged in the published body" is a structural risk, not just a two-item problem.** Writer's Notes are (correctly) an internal editorial artifact and won't ship with the page. That means the *only* place these two uncertainties are currently visible is a document the reader will never see. If this page is published before the flagged human sign-off happens, a reader gets false certainty on both points with no way to know AU Corporate itself hasn't independently confirmed them against primary source text yet. The pipeline's design (flag now, confirm before publish) is sound practice — but this review should not treat "flagged internally" as equivalent to "appropriately hedged," which is the specific instruction this stage was given.
4. **Some paragraphs are dense (80–150 words)**, per the SEO edit's own note. This isn't an EEAT defect on its own — reference-guide density is appropriate for this content type and audience — but combined with the volume of citations and cross-references, a reader skimming for a single answer (e.g., "do I need GST registration") has to work a little to extract it from the surrounding qualification. Not a blocker; worth a scannability pass if there's ever a lighter editing round, but explicitly out of scope for prior stages and not a reason to hold publication.

## Author/Expert Attribution Recommendation

This site has no author/reviewer bio system, and none should be invented for this page — a fabricated named "FEMA specialist, 12 years' experience" byline would itself be an EEAT violation (a false credential), and would actually undermine trust more than the current organisational-level framing does. Given that constraint, the achievable attribution signals are:

- **Keep attribution at the organisational/service-line level**, which the page already does reasonably well: the "How AU Corporate Supports" section names the actual coordination role (Form FNC through the AD bank, Form FC-1/ROC registration, AAC dual-routing, renewal tracking, closure) rather than an anonymous "we help with compliance" line. This is the correct substitute for individual attribution in the absence of a bio system — specificity of function stands in for a named credential.
- **The regulatory-currency callouts function as a soft trust signal in place of a "reviewed by" date-stamp.** Explicitly flagging the October 2025 draft regulations as not-yet-notified, and instructing the reader to "re-check the notification status before you file," reads as something only an entity actively monitoring RBI activity would write — it does real, if implicit, attribution work.
- **If AU Corporate wants a stronger attribution signal without inventing a bio, the lowest-risk option is a site-wide "last reviewed" date plus a link to a genuine, non-fabricated firm credentials/registration page** (e.g., ICAI/company-secretary registration, AD-bank-facing regulatory registrations actually held by the firm) — but that is a site-architecture decision outside this page's scope, not something to retrofit into this draft.
- **Do not add a named individual, a stated years-of-experience figure, or a client-count claim to this page to manufacture an attribution signal.** None of that currently exists in the draft, and it should stay that way; the fact-check and this review both confirm no fabricated credentials are present, and that absence should be preserved rather than "fixed."
