## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- **The "Regulatory watch" callout on the October 2025 draft regulations.** Naming a not-yet-notified draft, stating plainly it isn't law, and telling the reader to re-check notification status before filing is exactly the kind of regulatory-currency signal that separates a page written by people who track RBI activity from one written off a single scrape of "current rules." Per the positioning brief, this is a move only one competitor in the analyzed set was found doing at all — it's a real differentiator, not a courtesy hedge.
- **The BO-vs-LO validity/renewal section is the standout trust signal on the page.** Rather than repeat the commonly-cited (and, per Stage 9, apparently wrong-for-BOs) 3-year renewal figure that "a number of secondary sources appear to apply... which does not hold up on closer reading," the page explains *why* that figure doesn't transfer from Liaison Offices to Branch Offices, states what it could and couldn't confirm, and gives the reader an operationally correct answer (continuity tied to approval + AAC compliance) instead of a number it couldn't stand behind. This is precisely the "honest and current, not authoritative-sounding-but-wrong" posture the task asked this review to check for.
- **Correct disambiguation of the two "Automatic vs Approval" frameworks** (FEMA 22(R) Branch Office route vs. FEMA 20(R) equity/FDI route) is stated explicitly and links to the firm's own FDI channels page rather than leaving the reader to infer it. This is a genuine accuracy differentiator flagged by both Stage 9 (verified) and the positioning brief (identified competitor confusion point).
- **Internal linking depth is a real EEAT asset here, not decoration.** The page links to three country-specific subsidiary-vs-branch comparison pages, the company-formation pillar, the FDI-channels page, the regulatory-compliance page, and the wholly-owned-subsidiary blog post — all pages the positioning brief confirms already carry live, consistent figures on this exact topic (tax rate, closure sequence, eligibility thresholds). A single orphan page asserting these facts in isolation would be far weaker evidence of expertise than a page that visibly sits inside a coherent, cross-referenced body of the firm's own published work on the same regulations.
- **Multi-authority coordination is described mechanically, not claimed as a slogan** — RBI → AD Category-I bank → ROC/MCA → DGIT (International Taxation) is laid out as an actual sequence with what each authority does, which lets the reader infer AU Corporate's coordination value rather than being told to believe it.

## Where It Falls Short

- **The fact-check verdict is not fully clean**, and this page's own Writer's Notes and Stage 9 both say so directly. Two items — BO validity/renewal, and the draft regulations' notification status — are flagged for **human professional sign-off before publish**, not just AI-pipeline verification. That gate has not yet been exercised by a human reviewer. The content treatment is correct as drafted (see above), but "the content is honest about what it doesn't know" is not the same thing as "a qualified human has confirmed the primary-source position," which is what Stage 9 explicitly asked for.
- **AAC due date is a missed, low-risk strengthening opportunity, not a flaw** — Stage 9 found a consistently-cited 30 September (or 6-months-from-non-March-balance-sheet-date) due date across independent sources but recommended confirming it against the current Master Direction before publishing a hard date. As currently drafted, the AAC section says only "filed annually, following the close of the financial year" — accurate but less useful than it could be once that date is confirmed.
- **Form FC-2 (closure-stage ROC filing) is unnamed.** Stage 9 flagged that the ROC closure step in section "Closing or Winding Up a Branch Office" doesn't name the specific form (Form FC-2, distinct from the establishment-stage FC-1). Not an error — the sequence is correct — but naming the form would match the specificity the rest of the page achieves elsewhere (e.g., FC-1, Form FNC, Form 15CA/15CB are all named).
- **Internal link paths have not been technically verified.** Writer's Note #13 explicitly punts this to Stage 10 or a dedicated link-QA pass. A trust-signal page that links extensively to the firm's own expertise elsewhere on the site needs those links to actually resolve — a broken internal link on a page built partly on "look how consistent our own site is" undermines exactly the signal it's trying to send. This is a technical QA item, not a content/EEAT rewrite, but it should not be treated as closed.
- **Two rate/threshold facts are dated to a specific Finance Act / assessment year** (35%/36–38% tax rate under Finance Act 2024; USD 100,000/5-year eligibility thresholds, which Stage 9 notes the 2025 draft regulations propose removing entirely). These are correctly caveated in Stage 9's "Requires Human Professional Verification" table as time-sensitive, but the published page itself has no visible "current as of" date or last-reviewed marker for a reader to judge currency at a glance — see attribution recommendation below.

## Author/Expert Attribution Recommendation

The site has no author/reviewer bio system, and none should be invented for this page — a named credential with no basis would itself be a fabricated-trust-signal problem, which is exactly what this review is checking against. Recommending a specific named "reviewed by [name], [credential]" byline is out of scope and not advisable here.

What is achievable, and what this page already does well, is source-citation quality and regulatory-currency framing used *as* the trust signal in place of a named author — precise statute/section/form citations, the explicit "not yet notified" framing on the draft regulations, and the refusal to assert an unconfirmed renewal figure. These substitute for individual credentialing reasonably well on a regulatory reference page, because the credibility claim being made is "this is accurately and currently stated," not "trust this specific person's opinion."

Two low-cost, non-fabricated additions would strengthen this further without inventing anyone:
1. **A visible "Last reviewed / current as of [date]" marker** at the top of the page (tied to actual publish/review date, not a person) — this is a legitimate, truthful currency signal that costs nothing to maintain honestly and directly supports the page's own internal framing ("this page is written against [FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB], the framework currently in force").
2. **A firm-level (not individual) editorial-process statement**, if AU Corporate wants one site-wide — e.g., a short standard line describing how regulatory content is checked/updated — is a defensible, generic trust signal that doesn't require a named person. This is a site-wide backlog recommendation, not something to bolt onto this page alone.

Given the absence of a bio system, this page's real "expert attribution" is the demonstrated specificity and honesty of the content itself, reinforced by its consistency with the firm's three already-published country-specific comparison pages (per the positioning brief's "right-to-win" angle #1) — that internal consistency is a more credible, harder-to-fake signal than a single invented byline would be.

## Verdict

**Needs revision — conditional, process-gated, not a content rewrite.**

The page copy, as drafted, meets the EEAT bar: claims are backed, hedging is scoped honestly to the two genuinely unresolved regulatory points rather than spread across the page, real-world practitioner context is present, internal linking to the firm's own demonstrated expertise is substantive, and no fabricated credentials, statistics, or testimonials exist anywhere in the draft. If the only question were "does this read as produced by an experienced advisory firm," the answer is yes.

However, per the standing guardrail not to pass a page whose fact-check verdict was not clean: Stage 9's overall verdict was explicitly **"Needs human professional sign-off before publish,"** not a clean pass, on two items — (1) Branch Office validity/renewal framing, confirmed only via convergent secondary sourcing and never checked against the primary FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB or RBI Master Direction text directly (WebFetch blocked all session), and (2) the October 2025 draft regulations' notification status, which is time-sensitive by nature and must be re-confirmed at the actual moment of publish. This page cannot be marked fully cleared for publish until:

1. A human reviewer with direct (non-proxy-restricted) access to RBI's Master Direction / FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB text confirms the no-fixed-renewal-cycle framing for Branch Offices specifically, or supplies a verified figure if one exists.
2. Notification status of the draft 2025 Establishment Regulations is re-checked at the moment of publish (not at fact-check date), since if it has been notified, the "Regulatory watch" callout, the eligibility thresholds section, and the UIN-sequencing description would all need updating.
3. (Low priority, non-blocking) Internal link paths get a technical QA pass, given how many links this page carries and how load-bearing they are to its own EEAT case.

None of these require rewriting the page's voice, claims, or structure — they require closing the two specific sign-off items Stage 9 already identified, exactly as Stage 9 itself recommended, before this page goes live.
