## Claims That Could Not Be Verified — MUST FIX

| Claim | Issue |
|---|---|
| **"Section 115BAA"** cited as the current provision for the 22% concessional corporate tax rate | **This is now a superseded citation, not merely unsourced — the same category of error as Page A's REG-09/REG-10 mistake.** Section 115BAA was a provision of the Income-tax Act, **1961**. That Act has been replaced by the Income-tax Act, **2025**, effective 1 April 2026 and governing the current tax year. Multiple independent sources confirm Section 115BAA's concessional-rate provision was recodified as **Section 200 of the Income-tax Act, 2025** ("Section 200... equivalent to Section 115BAA of the Income-tax Act, 1961... substantially carries forward the policy and structure of Section 115BAA"). The draft cites "Section 115BAA" twice (main body and FAQ) as if it were the live, current-law citation, with no acknowledgment that the numbering changed. This is exactly the kind of specific, checkable, wrong citation the brief asks to be caught before publish — recommend either (a) dropping the section number entirely, matching how the live `/services/taxation-regulatory` page handles this (it states "the concessional 22% corporate tax rate" with **no section citation at all**, which is the safer, already-precedented approach on this site), or (b) if a citation is wanted, updating it to "Section 200 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (the recodified successor to Section 115BAA of the 1961 Act)." Leaving it as bare "Section 115BAA" is inconsistent with how rigorously this same page (and Page A) treats the 1961→2025 Act transition elsewhere — the page correctly updates the SEP citation to Section 9(9) of the new Act, but does not do the equivalent update for the tax-rate citation. Sources: [EZTax Section 200](https://eztax.in/income-tax-act-2025/section-200); [TaxTMI Section 200 vs 115BAA comparison](https://taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/tmi_faq_details.asp?ID=1665); [EZTax "Section 200 (Section 115BAA)" explainer](https://eztax.in/section-115baa-tax-rate-explained). |
| The GSTR-5A → GSTR-1/GSTR-3B transition is described as something that "transitions," "folds into," or is "replaced by" the new entity's filing "as part of the incorporation process" — appearing in the body text, the "What actually happens to the existing OIDAR registration" paragraph, the Post-Incorporation section, and the FAQ answer | **This framing is not supported by, and appears to conflict with, basic GST registration and corporate law principles**, and no source found describes an automatic "transition"/"merge" mechanism. A foreign parent's OIDAR registration (granted under CGST Rule 14 to "a person supplying OIDAR services from a place outside India") and a newly incorporated Indian Wholly Owned Subsidiary's GST registration are registrations of **two separate legal persons** — the Companies Act treats a WOS as a distinct legal entity from its foreign parent, with its own PAN and therefore its own, independently obtained GSTIN (via the ordinary Form GST REG-01 process once liable/opted-in). There is no CGST Rule or procedure found that automatically converts, folds, or merges an existing NRTP/OIDAR registration into a newly incorporated subsidiary's registration. What the sourced material does confirm is that a foreign OIDAR provider's registration can be **surrendered through the standard cancellation process — but only after all pending GSTR-5A returns are filed and outstanding IGST is cleared** — as an entirely separate step from the new entity's own fresh registration. The draft's repeated "transitions/folds into/replaced by, as part of the incorporation process" language risks misleading a reader into assuming this happens automatically or as a single continuous filing thread, when in practice: (1) the foreign parent needs to independently assess whether it still has any OIDAR-taxable supplies once Indian billing moves to the new entity, and (2) if not, it must go through final GSTR-5A filing and a formal cancellation application for its own registration, while (3) the new Indian entity separately registers under the ordinary domestic process. **Recommend reworking this section's wording** to state plainly that incorporation typically means new Indian billing runs through the entity's own, separately obtained GST registration, and that the foreign parent's original OIDAR registration needs to be actively wound down (final GSTR-5A, clearance of dues, formal cancellation) rather than assumed to auto-convert — this is the one place on the page where "conceptually accurate, procedurally vague" tips over into conceptually inaccurate. Sources checked: [TaxTMI OIDAR registration](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=11475); [Casahuja Foreign OIDAR Supplier guide](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/foreign-oidar-supplier-in-india-what.html) — no source confirms an automatic transition mechanism. |

## Overall Fact-Check Verdict

**Needs writer revision, then needs human professional sign-off before publish.**

Two items are must-fix, not merely must-flag, before this page is publish-ready:

1. **"Section 115BAA" must be corrected** — either remove the section-number citation entirely (matching the live `/services/taxation-regulatory` page's safer approach) or update it to **Section 200 of the Income-tax Act, 2025**, explicitly noting it succeeded Section 115BAA of the 1961 Act. This appears in both the main body ("Tax Treatment Once You Incorporate" section) and the FAQ answer — both instances need the same fix.
2. **The GSTR-5A → GSTR-1/GSTR-3B "transition" language must be reworded** to avoid implying an automatic merge between the foreign parent's OIDAR registration and the new Indian subsidiary's separately obtained GST registration — these are two distinct legal persons' registrations, not one registration continuing under a new name. This appears in the "GST After You Incorporate" section, the "Post-Incorporation" section, and the FAQ answer on GST — all three need the correction applied consistently.

Once those two fixes are made, the remaining open items — the 22%/25.17% effective-rate framing (currently well-handled but Finance-Act-dependent), the SPICe+ operational timelines, the FC-GPR/FLA/FIRMS currency at time of publish, the sector-classification edge-case caveat, and the freshly-commenced Income-tax Act 2025 §9(9) text — should go to a qualified AU Corporate tax/FEMA/GST professional for sign-off before this page goes live, consistent with the standard already set for Page A.
