## Minimum Coverage List
(What the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes. Missing any of these will make the page look thinner than the median competitor.)
1. The full four-way PE typology — Fixed Place, Service, Dependent Agent (DAPE), Construction — each with a **specific numeric threshold**, not a qualitative description (matching wisemonk.io's bar, the clear top performer on this dimension).
2. DTAA Article 5 named and cited as the primary legal anchor, ideally linking directly to the actual treaty text (irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/india.pdf) rather than only describing it secondhand.
3. Section 9(1)(i) Income Tax Act "business connection" named as the domestic-law parallel/overlap test, with at least a brief statement of which one governs when (DTAA generally more favorable to the taxpayer — see beaconfiling.com's sequencing framing under Differentiation).
4. Explicit "US company" / "foreign company" audience framing throughout, not generic "PE in India" language (confirmed real SERP-title evidence, not an invented framing).
5. At least one grounded case-law citation per major PE type (not just name-dropped — the holding stated in a sentence, as vjmglobal.com does for E-Funds).
6. Some form of practical mitigation guidance tied to each PE type (workspace arrangements, contract-signing authority, staffing structure) — the wisemonk.io pattern.
7. A direct answer to "does hiring in India create PE" and "does using an EOR eliminate PE risk" — these are now load-bearing questions on this SERP; a page silent on either will look incomplete next to at least 2-3 of the 6 competitors reviewed.
8. A brief, natural (not headline) mention that a liaison office is a distinct PE sub-case subject to RBI — not a full section, per Stage 2's keyword-intent call, but its complete absence would be a visible gap against ahlawatassociates.com's pattern.

## Differentiation List
(What almost nobody covers well, or covers at all — the real opportunity. Every item below traces to a specific observed gap from the table above or Stage 3's per-page findings — none are generic "add more detail" suggestions.)

1. **Full case-law currency through Clifford Chance (Delhi HC, Dec 2025) and Booking.com (ITAT, Feb 2026), tied to specific PE types, alongside the older Morgan Stanley/Formula One/E-Funds trio, in one place.** Observed gap: 0/6 analyzed pages do this; treelife.in comes closest on currency but its EOR coverage is thin-to-absent, and kaam.work has the Booking.com stakes figure but no older precedent at all.

2. **An operationally rigorous, non-reassuring answer to "does an EOR eliminate PE risk."** Observed gap: the only 2 pages that address it (wisemonk.io, kaam.work) both stop at "keep contract-signing authority in the US and you're largely fine" — neither names the specific conduct short of formal contract-signing (habitual involvement in deal terms, de facto operational control, day-to-day negotiation behavior) that tribunals have treated as creating dependent-agent or fixed-place exposure, and neither specifies what documentation/operating practice actually supports a defensible "no PE" position under audit. kaam.work's "reduces but doesn't eliminate" framing is the most honest starting point on the SERP and should be matched, then taken one level deeper — not contradicted.

3. **The narrow subsidiary/E-Funds legal answer (vjmglobal.com's core strength) combined with the full four-way typology in one page.** Observed gap: vjmglobal.com's page is a single-issue legal note that doesn't serve the broader "what is PE risk in India" reader; no page in the set does both at once.

4. **The employee-vs-independent-consultant distinction as two separate legal tests**, not folded into one generic "hiring creates PE" statement. Observed gap: only ksandk.com does this (1/6), and it does so without day-thresholds or a consequence figure attached — AU Corporate's version can carry both.

5. **A single explicit pre-incorporation → post-incorporation decision path**: no entity yet (contractor/EOR exposure) → what changes once incorporated → what does NOT change (residual PE exposure survives incorporation). Observed gap: 0/6 competitor pages structure this as one journey; pieces are scattered across different competitors. This is also the exact gap in AU Corporate's own existing FEMA content (`/india-entry-for-us-companies/fema-compliance-us-company-india-subsidiary`), which opens with "Once your Indian subsidiary is incorporated and receives foreign direct investment..." — explicitly post-incorporation scope only, confirmed by direct read of that page.tsx. This PE page is the natural place to own the pre-incorporation leg the existing cluster doesn't cover.

6. **Explicit treaty-vs-domestic-law sequencing guidance** (DTAA protection is generally more favorable than the Income Tax Act's domestic "business connection" test; state outright that a foreign company should deliberately invoke treaty position). Observed gap: only beaconfiling.com (1/6) states this outright rather than assuming it.

7. **A genuinely verified consequence stat**, replacing the two unverified figures currently circulating (kaam.work's Booking.com $475M demand, beaconfiling.com's INR 15,000 cr aggregate) with either (a) the same figures cited with a confirmed primary or authoritative secondary source, or (b) a different, verifiable figure. If AU Corporate can genuinely attribute and date this page, it's a trust-signal differentiator the whole SERP is currently missing — not a content-topic gap, but worth flagging to Stage 6 as a structural element.

9. **Resolution of whether the Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbers the PE/business-connection provisions** the way it renumbered transfer pricing into Sections 161-173 (AU Corporate's own transfer-pricing page already tracks that Act). ## Explicit Handling of Open Items Carried Forward from Stage 2/3

### Open Item 1 — PE effective tax rate conflict (~40%+surcharge/cess≈43.68% vs ~35%+surcharge/cess≈38.22%)
**Decision: state the mechanism without committing to a single hard effective-rate percentage at this stage, and flag the figure for Stage 9 primary-source verification before publish.**
- Neither figure has been checked against a primary source (Finance Act rate schedule / Income Tax Act rate provisions) anywhere in Stages 1-3, and no competitor snippet reviewed cited a primary source for either number either — this is a SERP-wide data gap, not something AU Corporate can resolve by picking whichever number "sounds more common."
- Recommended handling for Stage 5/6 content architecture: write the consequence section around the **mechanism** ("a foreign company's India-sourced profits attributable to a PE are taxed at the higher foreign-company corporate rate rather than the lower domestic-company rate, plus applicable surcharge and cess" — a structurally true statement regardless of which exact number is correct) and either (a) omit a specific effective percentage until Stage 9 verifies it against the Finance Act/Income Tax Act rate schedule in force for the relevant assessment year, or (b) present it explicitly as a range ("historically in the 35-40% base rate band before surcharge and cess") with a stated caveat that the reader should confirm the current-year rate, rather than asserting either 43.68% or 38.22% as fact.
- Do not let this block publish — the mechanism itself (higher rate applies) is well-established and doesn't require the exact number to be useful and accurate. But **do not ship either specific percentage without Stage 9 sign-off against a primary rate-schedule source.**
- If a verified consequence stat is needed sooner for the "why should a CFO care" hook, prefer kaam.work's Booking.com figure or beaconfiling.com's aggregate assessment figure — but only after Stage 9 independently confirms at least one of them (dollar/INR conversion, tribunal outcome, and figure) against a primary or clearly authoritative secondary source (e.g., a reputable tax-news outlet's direct reporting of the tribunal order, not a repeated blog citation). Do not repeat either figure as fact until that check happens.

### Open Item 2 — Hyatt International case-law lead (surfaced by treelife.in's snippet synthesis, Supreme Court, not independently verified)
**Decision: do not include in the page's initial case-law set; flag as a candidate for Stage 6/9 primary-source verification, include only if confirmed.**
- This lead exists only as one competitor's (treelife.in) synthesized snippet summary — no citation (case name format, year, SCC/court reporter reference), no independent confirmation against a primary case-law database, and no second source corroborating it anywhere in Stages 1-3.
- The page is already fully competitive on case-law currency without it: Morgan Stanley (SC 2007), Formula One (SC 2017), E-Funds (SC), Clifford Chance (Delhi HC, Dec 2025), and Booking.com (ITAT, Feb 2026) together already clear the bar no single competitor reviewed meets (see Differentiation #1). Hyatt International would be a bonus, not a requirement.
- Recommended path: Stage 6 should draft the case-law section without Hyatt International as a load-bearing citation. If Stage 9 (or whichever stage does legal/primary-source verification) can confirm the case name, court, year, and holding against a primary source before publish, add it as an additional data point in the Fixed-Place-PE subsection — it would strengthen an already-strong section, particularly since no competitor is currently citing it. If it cannot be verified in time, ship without it rather than publishing an unverified Supreme Court holding.
