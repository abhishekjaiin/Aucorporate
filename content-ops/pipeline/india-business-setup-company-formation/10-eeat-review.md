## Where the Page Demonstrates Genuine Expertise

- The distinction between the "fastest possible filing" and "what a first-time foreign parent should actually plan for" — naming the four specific, recurring causes of delay — is exactly the kind of granular, seen-it-before detail that separates practitioner content from a rewritten government FAQ.
- The FC-GPR compounding explanation and the FOCC/Rule 23 explanation are both precise without being padded, matching Stage 5's explicit instruction ("a tight, accurate paragraph beats a long one") — this reads as someone who has actually filed these forms, not someone summarizing a law firm blog.
- The compliance roadmap (Day 0 → bank account → capital infusion/FIRC → FC-GPR → GST → FLA → ongoing RoC filings) genuinely reflects what Stage 5's positioning doc says AU Corporate actually does — narrate incorporation as the first step in one continuous compliance relationship — rather than a generic "don't forget your annual filings" gesture. It condenses and links out rather than duplicating `regulatory-compliance`/`banking-taxation`, exactly per the positioning guidance.
- The apostille-vs-notarisation and cost-vs-timeline sections both name the specific, non-obvious failure point a first-time founder wouldn't know to ask about — that's earned authority, not asserted authority.

## Where It Falls Short

1. **The "reviewed by" byline is currently ahead of the actual review it claims.** This is the single item that must be resolved before this page can be called EEAT-clean. The fact-check stage is unambiguous that human professional sign-off on three specific points (s.149(3) Bare Act wording, current FC-GPR Master Direction, DTAA section-mapping status) has not yet occurred. A dated "reviewed by [practice]" line is a trust signal precisely because it implies that review happened — publishing it now would make that signal not-yet-true.
2. **No visible mechanism in the draft for closing that loop before publish.** There's nothing wrong with the content strategy here (firm-level credit, no fabricated author), but the pipeline needs an explicit step where the practice actually performs the sign-off the byline claims, dated appropriately, before this goes live — otherwise the EEAT signal is asserted rather than earned.
3. Minor: the GST threshold and FC-GPR-window claims are both correctly caveat-free in body text (appropriately, since fact-check confirmed them as currently correct), but both are the kind of figure that changes with limited notice (GST Council notification; RBI Master Direction updates) — worth a lightweight internal process note (not necessarily page copy) that these need a freshness check on some periodic cadence, not just at initial publish.

## Author/Expert Attribution Recommendation

Keep the firm-level credit — do not add an invented named CA/partner, per the explicit Stage 5 constraint and the fact that the site has no individual-byline pattern to draw from. However: for content in this risk category (director-compliance obligations, FEMA reporting deadlines, tax mechanics), a *real*, verifiable named reviewer (an actual CS/CA/tax professional at AU Corporate who has genuinely reviewed the flagged items) would be a stronger trust signal than the firm-level statement alone, if AU Corporate has such a person available and willing to be named — this is a "nice to have if genuinely available," not a blocker. What is a blocker is that whichever form the byline takes, the review it describes needs to have actually happened first.

It would likely pass EEAT review cleanly on content quality alone.
