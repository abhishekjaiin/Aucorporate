## Minimum Coverage List
(What the page MUST address to be competitive — table stakes. Missing any of these will make the page look thinner than the median competitor, even before AU's own differentiation is layered on.)
1. Core PLI mechanics stated correctly and specifically: eligible sectors, incentive-rate range (cite it as sector-dependent, not one flat number — competitors cite figures from 4% up to 18-25% depending on sector/version), the FY 2019-20 incremental-sales base year, and that a foreign company cannot apply directly — only through an Indian-registered subsidiary/JV (the KNM India / Tecnova bar).
2. Entity-structure mechanics under the Companies Act, 2013 — JV vs. wholly-owned subsidiary, with at least the india-briefing/knmindia-level rationale for why entity choice affects both operational control and incentive capture, not just "you need to incorporate."
3. FDI Automatic Route stated explicitly (100% FDI allowed in most manufacturing categories) — present in some form on nearly every page in this set; its absence would be a visible gap.
4. China+1 named as a strategic term, not just implied — every strong page in this SERP either uses it as a heading or opens with it; a page that doesn't name it explicitly will look out of step with the whole ranking cluster (per Stage 1's "treated as a named strategic term" finding).
5. Explicit "US company/investor" audience framing, evidenced by Stage 2 as the real country-modifier convention on this SERP (not "foreign" or "international" at the page/title level) — reserve "foreign company/investor" language specifically for the PLI-eligibility subsection and FAQ, where it is separately evidenced.
6. Direct, clear answer to "what is PLI eligibility for a foreign manufacturer" and "how much investment is required," including a sector-range table rather than a single number — this is now load-bearing content; a page silent or vague on it will read thinner than Tecnova's page, which is otherwise the weakest page in the set.
7. Some concrete, dated figures (not just qualitative description) — the Beacon Filing/KNM India pattern of citing specific investment/disbursement/threshold numbers "as of [date]" is what separates the top-tier pages from the generic ones in this SERP.

## Differentiation List
(What almost nobody covers well, or covers at all — the real opportunity. Every item below traces to a specific observed gap from the coverage table or Stage 3's per-page findings — none are generic "add more detail" suggestions.)

1. **Unifying China+1 strategic framing with PLI-eligibility mechanics and entity/FEMA incorporation steps in one reading path.** Observed gap: 0/6 pages do this. Beacon Filing splits it across at least 4 URLs (the PLI/China+1 post, the FEMA-compliance/USA page, the payroll/USA page, the register-company page); Ahlawat splits it across at least 3 (the manufacturing guide, a separate US-China-trade-tension post, a separate US-market-entry page). This is the single meta-gap Stage 3's verdict identifies as the bar to clear, and no competitor examined closes it even on its own domain.

2. **Explicitly dating PLI as a scheme in active transition** — naming ECMS and the ₹22,919 crore proposed Component Manufacturing Scheme, and stating plainly that original PLI sector windows are closing in 2026. Observed gap: only 1/6 pages does this explicitly (beaconfiling's tracker post, and even that's a sibling URL to its flagship China+1 post, not confirmed to be integrated there); knmindia shows only implicit awareness (PLI 2.0 shifting toward value-addition-linked payouts, without naming ECMS); the other 4/6 analyzed pages (india-briefing, ascgroup, tecnovaglobal, ahlawatassociates) present PLI as static/current with no visible transition acknowledgment at all.

3. **Combining Factories Act / physical-plant procedural depth with PLI/China+1 incentive-and-strategy content.** Observed gap: Ahlawat is the only page with real Factories Act specificity (1948 registration thresholds — 10+ workers with power, 20+ without; the NOC sequence from Fire/Water/Pollution Control; the 15-day advance-notice rule; differentiated clearance timelines of 30 days to 6 months by pollution category) — and that same page has zero PLI content. No page anywhere in the set gives a reader both "how to actually open the factory" and "how to capture the incentive" together.

4. **The US parent's own compliance exposure for a manufacturing (tangible-goods) subsidiary** — Section 482/transfer-pricing treatment for intercompany manufacturing/contract-manufacturing arrangements (as distinct from a services/SaaS transfer-pricing setup), and Form 5471/5472 filing obligations. Observed gap: completely open across all 6 pages examined — not a single page, including the high-authority india-briefing.com piece, addresses the US-side reporting obligations at all; all tax coverage found is India-side (Indian transfer pricing, GST).

5. **State-selection guidance as a real decision factor**, not an afterthought — labor availability, SEZ status, industrial policy, incentives, logistics, and supplier-cluster variance by state. Observed gap: only india-briefing.com (1/6) treats this with real sophistication, and even there it's one of four buyer personas the page serves, not manufacturing-exclusive depth.

6. **FEMA/RBI filing mechanics tied specifically to a manufacturing entity's capital inflow and PLI disbursement cycle** (FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA, the 30-day filing deadline Beacon Filing names as a FEMA-violation trigger if missed). Observed gap: 1/6, and even Beacon Filing keeps this on a separate USA-cluster URL rather than inside its PLI/China+1 content.

7. **Local value-addition/domestic-content thresholds by sector, scaling year over year** (KNM India's cited 40-60% Year 1 to 60-80% Year 3 pattern). Observed gap: only 1/6 pages states this mechanic at all — a genuinely underserved but concrete, checkable data point.

## Potential Unique Angles
(Grounded in the gaps above — not invented.)
- **A single unified reading path**: China+1 "why now" (with dated proof points) → PLI/incentive eligibility (with a sector-range table) → entity choice and FEMA mechanics → physical-plant/Factories Act procedure, in that order, in one page. No competitor structures content this way; the closest analog (Beacon Filing's cluster) requires reading 4 separate URLs to assemble the same journey.
- **An explicitly dated "PLI is transitioning" framing** — stating plainly that original PLI sector windows are closing in 2026 and naming ECMS/the proposed Component Manufacturing Scheme as the successor framework, with a stated plan to keep the page current (a maintenance note, not a one-time write) — directly responding to the fact that 4 of 6 analyzed pages present PLI as static.
- **A worked example or short decision framework connecting entity choice to both PLI capture and Section 482 exposure** — no page reviewed connects "which entity you choose" to consequences on both sides of the Pacific (Indian incentive eligibility AND US parent transfer-pricing/reporting obligations) — this is the single most open gap in the entire set.
- **A Factories Act procedural checklist paired with the PLI eligibility table** — registration thresholds, NOC sequence, and realistic clearance timelines (matching Ahlawat's specificity) sitting alongside the incentive-capture mechanics (matching KNM India's specificity) — nobody, including Ahlawat itself, currently does this.
- **A state-selection comparison element** (even a lighter-weight version of india-briefing's framing) that ties state choice to PLI-relevant factors specifically (SEZ status, sector-cluster presence, logistics) rather than generic "ease of doing business" language.
