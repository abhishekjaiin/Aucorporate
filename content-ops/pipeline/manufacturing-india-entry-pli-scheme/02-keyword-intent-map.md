## Foreign/International Framing Check
Evidence found, and it is narrower and more specific than generic "foreign/international" language:

1. **"Foreign" framing is evidenced only in the PLI-eligibility context**, not for manufacturing entry generally. Two independent competitor titles use it verbatim: Tecnova's "PLI Scheme and what is in Store for **foreign investors**?" and ASC Group's "India Market Entry Guide 2026: Rules for **Foreign Companies**." The proxy query "PLI scheme eligibility **foreign company**" also returned a distinct, on-topic result set (Tecnova ranks for it, per Stage 1 row 5). startupsolicitors.com's title uses "**as a Foreigner**" specifically for manufacturing-unit setup. This justifies using "foreign company/investor/manufacturer" language in the PLI-eligibility subsection and FAQ — not in the page's primary or title-level keyword.
2. **"US" (country-specific), not "foreign/international," is the evidenced audience modifier where a country modifier appears at all.** india-briefing.com's page is literally titled "...for US Investors," ASC Group has "...for US Companies," Ahlawat and Inaac Advisors each run dedicated US-audience pages. No competitor title anywhere in the Stage 1 corpus uses "international" or "overseas" or "cross-border" framing. This matches AU's own existing cluster convention (`/india-entry-for-us-companies/`) and should be preserved — if a country/audience modifier is used at all in title/H1, "US" is the evidenced choice, not "foreign" or "international."
3. **No evidence exists for the literal compound "US manufacturer" + "PLI" + "China+1" as one searched phrase.** That convergence is AU Corporate's synthesis of the topic, not an observed query. This is fine as the page's positioning angle (that's Stage 5's job), but it should not be presented to Stage 6 as a keyword string to optimize a title around verbatim.

**Working rule for Stage 5/6:** target the audience (US manufacturers) through framing, examples (Cummins/BorgWarner/P&G-style), and positioning — not by inserting "foreign" or "international" into the primary keyword. Where a country modifier is used, use "US," which is evidenced; reserve "foreign company/investor" language specifically for the PLI-eligibility subsection and FAQ, where it is evidenced.

## PLI vs. China+1 Durability — Note for Stage 5/6
Stage 1 flagged that PLI's original sector windows are closing in 2026, with ECMS and a proposed Component Manufacturing Scheme emerging as successors — meaning "PLI" as a standalone hook carries real staleness risk within the page's own publish year. From a keyword-durability standpoint:
- **"PLI" is a real, currently-searched, currently-ranked term** (dominates the competitor titles observed) but is tied to a scheme in active transition. Treat it as a high-value **secondary/supporting** keyword, not the page's sole long-term anchor — plan for a content-maintenance note (Stage 5/11) rather than a one-time write.
- **"China+1" / "China plus one"** is used as a named strategic term across nearly every publisher in the SERP (Stage 1: "treated as a named strategic term ... several pages use it as a section heading or title term") and is not tied to any single scheme's legislative lifecycle — it describes a durable geopolitical/supply-chain trend, not a program with a sunset date. This makes it the more durable of the two anchor candidates.
- **"India manufacturing FDI"** is the most durable of all three — it is regulatory-structure language (FDI Automatic Route, entity mechanics) that does not depend on any specific incentive scheme's status.
- **Recommendation carried forward, not decided here:** anchor the page's title/H1 on "manufacturing plant in India" / "China+1" / "India manufacturing FDI" framing (durable), and treat "PLI" and "PLI 2.0/ECMS" as a prominent but explicitly-dated subsection ("as of [date], PLI operates as follows; successor schemes ECMS/Component Manufacturing Scheme are emerging") rather than the keyword the whole page is built to rank for. This is a positioning/architecture call for Stage 5 and Stage 6, not resolved by this stage — flagging it here per the task instruction.
