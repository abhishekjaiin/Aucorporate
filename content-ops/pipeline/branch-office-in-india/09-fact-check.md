## Regulatory Currency Check

**Branch Office validity/renewal period — the highest-priority open item.** After extensive searching, I was not able to fetch RBI's Master Direction or FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB text directly (every attempted primary/near-primary source domain was blocked by the egress proxy this session, confirmed with a non-Indian control domain too). However, the WebSearch evidence available this round is meaningfully stronger than what prior stages documented, and consistently supports the draft's framing:

- Multiple independent search syntheses returned the specific, repeated formulation: *"A branch office (BO) does not have a fixed validity period under FEMA unless specified at the time of approval"* — distinct from Liaison Offices, which are explicitly described everywhere as having a 3-year cycle (2 years for NBFC/construction-development applicants).
- The strongest corroborating signal is structural: coverage of RBI's October 2025 draft Establishment Regulations consistently describes one of its reforms as **removing tenure/validity limits specifically for Liaison Offices** ("An important reform removes tenure limits for liaison offices... the previous three-year tenure limit for liaison offices has been eliminated"). No source describes the draft as removing a tenure limit for Branch Offices. If Branch Offices currently had a fixed renewal cycle under the 2016 regulations, coverage of a reform explicitly targeted at removing that constraint would very likely mention BOs alongside LOs — it doesn't, anywhere I found. That's a meaningful (if indirect) confirmation that BOs don't currently have a fixed cycle to begin with.
- A minority of lower-quality SEO/content-mill sources (e.g., bizfoc.com, groomtax.com) assert a "3-year, renewable" cycle for Branch Offices specifically — but this reads as the same LO-to-BO conflation error that Stage 3's orchestrator follow-up already identified and that the draft already flags. These sources do not cite a regulation number, and are contradicted by the more careful sources above.

**Conclusion: the draft's framing is well-supported and should stand as published as-is.** This is a "confirmed no fixed cycle" outcome per the task's own success criteria, though I'd still class it VERIFIED WITH CAVEAT rather than fully VERIFIED, because I was never able to read the primary regulation/Master Direction text directly to check for a specific sub-regulation stating this affirmatively (vs. it simply not appearing in any secondary description). If a future stage or a human reviewer gets non-proxy-restricted access to rbi.org.in, a final primary-source read is still worth doing, but I would not hold up publication for it — the draft is already appropriately hedged and honest about the limits of its own sourcing, which is the correct posture here.

**RBI/AD-bank approval timeline.** Confirmed the draft's approach is correct. No primary source specifies a fixed number of days. Secondary/advisory sources give wildly inconsistent estimates (45–60 working days; 6–8 weeks; 3–6 weeks; 4–6 months; up to 180 days for land-border-country applicants going through MHA security review) — the inconsistency itself is evidence there's no regulatory anchor for any single figure. The draft's decision not to invent a number is the right call and should stand.

**2025 draft Establishment Regulations — notification status.** Re-confirmed via multiple independent sources (EY India alert, dated December 2025; India Briefing's 2026-dated article; Lexology) that as of their most recent updates, the draft had **not** been notified in the Official Gazette, and FEMA 22(R)/2016-RB remains the operative framework. One search synthesis characterized this as holding true generally "as of September 2026," but that specific phrasing came from the search tool's own summarization rather than a dated primary citation, so I'd treat it as directionally reliable but not as strong as a dated official confirmation. **This is exactly the kind of fact that can flip between fact-check and actual publish date — re-verify at the moment of publish**, ideally by someone who can reach rbi.org.in directly (this session's proxy blocked it entirely). If it has since been notified, the "Regulatory watch" callout box, the eligibility section (net worth/profit thresholds may be removed under the draft), and potentially the UIN-issuance sequencing would all need updating, since the draft's reforms touch exactly those areas.

**Other regulatory-currency notes:**
- The AAC's 3-consecutive-year non-filing → automatic closure trigger, and the requirement for restricted-country applicants to register with State Police Authorities and have their approval letter copied to MHA's Internal Security Division, are real and consistently documented but **not mentioned in the draft**. Neither omission makes the draft inaccurate — the draft's framing ("additional scrutiny... changes the approval route and timeline") is not wrong, just less granular — but both would be reasonable, low-risk additions if the writer wants more depth, not corrections.
- The ROC closure filing is specifically **Form FC-2** (distinct from the establishment-stage Form FC-1) per multiple closure-procedure sources. The draft's closure section doesn't name a form for the ROC closure step. This is a minor completeness gap, not an error — worth considering for a future revision but not a must-fix.
